Part I · Chapter 1Definition of Sporting™

What Sporting™ is

Chapter 1, Definition of sporting · PDF, p. 3

Sporting (Parcours de Chasse) is a FITASC discipline that imitates hunting situations. It is shot with a hunting shotgun at artificial clay targets thrown over natural terrain, so that the layouts test both technique and sporting skill. Trajectories copy the flight or run of game and vary in difficulty.

  • Hunting-style discipline created by FITASC
  • Hunting shotgun + artificial targets
  • Uses natural features of the terrain
  • Trajectories of varied difficulty
Terms: Sporting™ · Layout · Trajectory

Safety, environment and no live animals

Chapter 1, Definition of sporting · PDF, p. 3

When a Sporting layout is organised, the safety of everyone present comes first, together with protection of plants and care for the environment. Shooting at live animals is not allowed in this discipline.

  • Safety of all present is a design requirement
  • Flora and environment must be protected
  • No shooting at live animals
Terms: Safety · Layout

Part I · Chapter 2Sporting™ layouts

What a layout must offer

Chapter 2, 2.1 Layout · PDF, p. 3

A Sporting range needs enough traps, placed according to the terrain, to give conditions as close as possible to real game shooting: partridge, duck, pheasant, rabbit and so on. The set of trajectories should mix incoming, outgoing, crossing and quartering targets, low and high, in open ground or in woodland, with or without trees and bushes partly hiding them.

  • Enough traps to imitate natural game shooting
  • Incoming, outgoing, crossing, quartering
  • Low and high targets
  • Open ground or woods, with or without cover
Terms: Layout · Trap · Trajectory

Number of traps and targets per layout type

Chapter 2, 2.2 Traps · PDF, p. 3

Every layout type gives 25 targets: 15 singles and 5 doubles. The old-system layout uses 5 traps for 3 shooting positions. The new-system line with 4 shooting positions has three positions with 4 traps and one position (number 2 or 3) with 3 traps; 2 of the 5 doubles are shot at the 3-trap position. The new-system line with 5 positions has 3 traps per position. A line with 3 positions and 5 traps per position is allowed for Grand Prix only.

  • Always 15 singles + 5 doubles = 25 targets
  • Old system: 5 traps, 3 shooting positions
  • New 4-position line: 3 x 4 traps + 1 x 3 traps (position 2 or 3)
  • New 5-position line: 3 traps per position
  • 3-position line with 5 traps each: Grand Prix only
Terms: Trap · Layout · Line (new system) · Single target · Double · Grand Prix

Trap types and letters

Chapter 2, 2.2 Traps · PDF, p. 4

Traps can be manual, automatic or a mix of both. They are identified by letters A, B, C and D, read from left to right as seen from the shooting stand. These letters are what the target menu and the scorecard use.

  • Manual, automatic or mixed traps
  • Letters A, B, C, D from left to right
  • Seen from the shooting stand
Terms: Trap · Target menu · Shooting stand

Permitted target types and colours

Chapter 2, 2.3 Targets · PDF, p. 4

Sporting may use these targets: standard, midi, super mini, battue, rabbit, ZZ and flash. Whatever the type, its colour has to be easy to see against the background. Clays in two colours (bicolour) are advised against.

  • Standard, midi, super mini, battue, rabbit, ZZ, flash
  • Colour easy to see against the background
  • Bicolour clays are advised against
Terms: Target · Rabbit · Midi · Mini / super mini · Battue · Flash target · ZZ target

Size and surface of the shooting stand

Chapter 2, 2.4 Shooting stands · PDF, p. 4

A shooting stand is a 1 m square or a circle 1 m in diameter. Where possible it should be level, and at the very least the shooter must be able to stand on it without slipping, which is a safety matter.

  • 1 m square or 1 m diameter circle
  • Level if possible
  • Must not be slippery (safety)
Terms: Shooting stand · Safety

Target menu at the stand

Chapter 2, 2.4 Shooting stands · PDF, p. 4

In front of each stand there is a target menu that the shooter can read. It lists the traps in shooting order (for example A, B, C) and must show any special target types: 90 mm, 70 mm, 60 mm, battue or rabbit. Before the competition the referees check that the machines are loaded with targets that match the menu.

  • Menu placed in front of the stand, visible to the shooter
  • Traps listed in shooting order
  • Special targets (90/70/60 mm, battue, rabbit) must be stated
  • Referees check machines match the menu before the competition
Terms: Target menu · Trap · Referee

Waiting positions

Chapter 2, 2.4 Shooting stands · PDF, p. 4

Places where the rest of the squad waits should be as close as possible to the shooting stand, but must not distract the person shooting and must respect safety rules.

  • As close as possible to the stand
  • No distraction for the shooter
  • Must comply with safety rules
Terms: Squad · Safety

Hunting ethics and shooting distances

Chapter 2, 2.5 Trajectories, 2.5.1 · PDF, p. 4

Layouts must respect hunting ethics and realistic distances. Targets should not be set so close that real game would be spoiled, nor so far that a real animal would only be wounded. Every single target must leave enough time to fire two cartridges at it.

  • No shooting at too close range
  • No shooting beyond the effective range of gun or cartridge
  • Two cartridges must be possible on every single target
Terms: Trajectory · Single target · Hunting ethics

Difficulty classes A, B and C

Chapter 2, 2.5 Trajectories, 2.5.1 · PDF, p. 4

Each trajectory is graded by how many targets an average shooter breaks: class A more than 80%, class B from 60% to 80%, class C from 40% to 60%. For a stand the recommended mix is one A, two B and one C trajectory. If a stand has five single targets, the extra one may only be class A or B.

  • Class A: more than 80% broken
  • Class B: 60% to 80%
  • Class C: 40% to 60%
  • Recommended per stand: 1 A + 2 B + 1 C
  • Fifth single target: class A or B only
Terms: Difficulty class · Trajectory

How doubles are composed

Chapter 2, 2.5 Trajectories, 2.5.2 · PDF, p. 5

Doubles may only use targets that have already been shown as singles from the same position. A class C trajectory may appear only once in the doubles of a stand, preferably paired with a class A target. A double may consist of two B trajectories, or of one B together with one A.

  • Doubles use targets already shot as singles from that position
  • Class C only once in the doubles of a stand, preferably with an A
  • Allowed: B + B, or B + A
Terms: Double · Difficulty class · Single target

Doubles at a position with 3 machines

Chapter 2, 2.5 Trajectories, 2.5.3 · PDF, p. 5

At a shooting position with 3 machines and 2 doubles, at least one of the two doubles must be simultaneous or rafale: one simultaneous (the other on report), one rafale (the other on report), or one of each. Two doubles on report are not allowed there. The rulebook does not say whether two doubles of the same kind (two simultaneous or two rafale) are allowed.

  • Positions with 3 machines and 2 doubles
  • At least one double is simultaneous or rafale
  • Not two doubles on report
  • Two of the same kind: the rulebook does not say
Terms: Double · Double on report · Simultaneous double · Rafale double

Part I · Chapter 3Shooting rules

Ready position: feet and gun

Chapter 3, 3.1 Shooting position, 3.1.1 · PDF, p. 5

Before calling, the shooter stands with both feet inside the shooting stand and holds the gun so that the heel of the stock touches the body. Whatever the stock (standard, Monte Carlo, adjustable comb or any device shortening the mounting distance), the rear end of the highest part of the stock must stay below a horizontal line marked on the jacket. The shooter keeps this position until the target has been released and can be seen.

  • Both feet within the stand
  • Heel of the stock touches the body
  • Highest rear point of the stock below the jacket line
  • Applies to all stock types, incl. adjustable comb
  • Hold until the target is released and visible
Terms: Ready position · Gun position · Shooting stand

The 25 cm marking line on the jacket

Chapter 3, 3.1 Shooting position, 3.1.1 · PDF, p. 5

The reference line is drawn on the shooter's jacket or vest 25 cm (9.85 inches) below the axis of the shoulder and parallel to it. It must be in a colour that contrasts with the jacket so the referee can see it. The rulebook shows a diagram of the vest and three stock types.

  • 25 cm (9.85") below the shoulder axis
  • Parallel to the shoulder axis
  • Colour must contrast with the jacket
  • Diagram on page 6
Terms: Jacket line · Ready position

When the gun may be mounted

Chapter 3, 3.1 Shooting position, 3.1.2 · PDF, p. 6

The shooter may not mount the gun before the target appears. Every target, rabbits included, must be shot with the gun mounted to the shoulder; firing from the hip is not allowed.

  • No mounting before the target appears
  • All targets shot with gun mounted
  • Rabbits included
Terms: Gun position · Rabbit

Gun position between the two targets of a double

Chapter 3, 3.1 Shooting position, 3.1.3 · PDF, p. 6

In any double (on report, simultaneous or rafale) the shooter may hold the gun as they wish between the first and second targets. The free position applies only between the two targets, not before the first one.

  • Free gun position only between 1st and 2nd target
  • Applies to report, simultaneous and rafale doubles
  • Ready position still required before the first target
Terms: Double · Gun position

No test shots on the stand

Chapter 3, 3.1 Shooting position, 3.1.4 · PDF, p. 6

Guns may never be tested on a shooting stand. A shooter who wants to check that the gun works can do so before going to the layout, on a special test stand near the clubhouse if one is provided.

  • Never test a gun on the shooting stand
  • Use the dedicated test stand near the clubhouse
  • Do it before going onto the layout
Terms: Gun test · Shooting stand

Hearing protection and safety glasses (cross-reference)

Chapter 3, 3.2 Hearing protection; 3.3 Safety glasses · PDF, p. 6

Chapter 3 only points to the safety chapter: hearing protection is regulated in Article 8.9 and safety glasses in Article 8.10.

  • Hearing protection: see 8.9
  • Safety glasses: see 8.10
Terms: Hearing protection · Safety glasses

Part I · Chapter 4Definitions

Single target

Chapter 4, 4.1 Definition of a single target · PDF, p. 7

A single is one target, of any clay type, launched by one trap. The shooter must be able to fire two cartridges at it, so a miss with the first barrel can still be followed by a second shot.

  • One target from one trap
  • Any clay type
  • Two cartridges may be fired at it
Terms: Single target · Trap

Doubles: two cartridges only

Chapter 4, 4.2 Definition of doubles · PDF, p. 7

For a double the shooter has only two cartridges in total. Both may be fired at the same target if the shooter wishes.

  • Two cartridges per double
  • Both may be fired at one target
Terms: Double · Cartridge

Double on report

Chapter 4, 4.2 Definition of doubles, 4.2.1 Double on report · PDF, p. 7

Two targets come from one or two traps; the second is released between 0 and 3 seconds after the shooter fires at the first. The shot at the first target triggers the second one.

  • One or two traps
  • Second target 0 to 3 seconds after the shot at the first
  • Released on the sound of the first shot
Terms: Double on report

Simultaneous double

Chapter 4, 4.2 Definition of doubles, 4.2.2 Simultaneous double · PDF, p. 7

Both targets are thrown at the same moment by one or two traps. The shooter may take them in either order.

  • Thrown at the same time
  • One or two traps
  • Any shooting order
Terms: Simultaneous double

Rafale double

Chapter 4, 4.2 Definition of doubles, 4.2.3 Rafale double · PDF, p. 7

Two targets are thrown from the same trap along the same trajectory, one after the other as fast as the trap arm reloads. Either may be shot first.

  • Same trap, same trajectory
  • Second follows at the trap's reload cycle
  • Any shooting order
Terms: Rafale double · Trap

Glossary of basic terms

Glossary · PDF, p. 26

The rulebook ends with a short glossary. A squad is a group of at most six shooters drawn by lot who shoot together on the same installations. A round is 25 targets shot on one layout or line. A layout is a set of shooting stands giving 25 targets. A trap is the throwing machine; a shot is one fired cartridge; a trajectory is the path of a target. 'One' means a target broken under the rules, 'zero' a target missed under the rules, and 'No Bird' an irregular target or trajectory. The course designer sets up the layouts.

  • Squad: max 6 shooters, drawn by lot
  • Round: 25 targets on one layout or line
  • One = broken, Zero = missed (by the rules)
  • No Bird = irregular target or trajectory
  • Course designer sets the layouts
Terms: Squad · Round · Layout · Trap · One · Zero · No Bird · Course designer

Part II · Chapter 5Referees

Referee's commitments

Chapter 5, 5.1 · PDF, p. 9

At an international competition every referee formally commits to apply the rules and make others respect them, to decide honestly and impartially, and to set aside their own nationality and federation. The referee must stand where compliance with the rules can be judged well, announce decisions loudly and clearly, listen to protests politely without being swayed, and carry an up-to-date copy of the rules in the FITASC official languages.

  • Honest and impartial decisions
  • Nationality and home federation ignored
  • A good position to judge
  • Decisions announced clearly and loudly
  • Listens to protests without being influenced
  • Carries current rules in FITASC official languages
Terms: Referee

Referee certificates

Chapter 5, 5.2 · PDF, p. 9

Each referee needs a valid referee certificate from their national federation. The chief referee needs an international certificate issued by FITASC. Someone without the required certificate may only act as an assistant referee, and only if the jury of that competition approves it in advance.

  • Referee: valid national certificate
  • Chief referee: FITASC international certificate
  • Otherwise only assistant referee
  • Assistant needs prior jury approval
Terms: Referee · Chief referee · Jury

Order on the stand and during shoot-offs

Chapter 5, 5.3 · PDF, p. 9

Referees are responsible for good order and correct behaviour at the shooting stand, and this duty continues during shoot-offs.

  • Referee keeps order on the stand
  • Also during shoot-offs
Terms: Referee · Shoot-off

Calling for the target and the 3-second release

Chapter 5, 5.4 · PDF, p. 9

When ready, the shooter calls 'ready' or 'pull'. The referee passes the command to the trapper as fast as possible with an audio signal, and the target must then be thrown within three seconds of that signal. A target released later is a No Bird (see 6.5.2).

  • Shooter calls 'ready' or 'pull'
  • Referee relays by audio signal to the trapper
  • Target within 3 seconds of the signal
  • Late target = No Bird
Terms: Referee · Trapper · No Bird

Interrupting shooting for bad weather

Chapter 5, 5.5 · PDF, p. 9

In exceptional cases the referee may stop shooting during a sudden heavy shower or a violent storm that is expected to be short. If it becomes clear the stop will last longer than expected, the referee must inform the jury.

  • Referee may pause for sudden heavy rain or storm
  • Intended for short interruptions
  • Longer interruption: inform the jury
  • Over 10 minutes: targets shown again (16.9)
Terms: Referee · Jury · Interruption

Refusing a target; who rules on trajectories

Chapter 5, 5.6 · PDF, p. 9

A shooter may not refuse a target they called for; the only valid refusal is a target that was not called. Only the referee decides whether a trajectory is correct and whether a target is a No Bird. The referees' quick guide uses this article (with 6.4) to score a correctly thrown target the shooter did not fire at as zero.

  • No refusing a called target
  • A target not called may be refused
  • Only the referee rules on trajectory and No Bird
  • Not firing at a correct target = zero (quick guide, 5.6 & 6.4)
Terms: Referee · No Bird · Trajectory

Part II · Chapter 6Target refereeing

The referee decides alone

Chapter 6, 6.1; 6.2 · PDF, p. 9

Target decisions are made by the referee alone. Members of the squad have no right to step in or to give their opinion about whether a target was hit (the referee may only consult them himself in case of doubt, see 9.5.2).

  • Referee rules alone
  • Squad members may not intervene or comment
  • Consultation only at the referee's initiative (9.5.2)
Terms: Referee · Squad

When a target is scored 'one' (hit)

Chapter 6, 6.3 · PDF, p. 10

A target counts as hit when it was properly launched, the shooter fired at it in line with the shooting-position rules of Article 3.1, and at least one piece visibly broke off, or the target was wholly or partly destroyed. The same applies to flash targets.

  • Target properly launched
  • Shooter complied with Article 3.1
  • At least one visible piece, or total/partial destruction
  • Same rule for flash targets
Terms: One · Flash target · Ready position

When a target is scored 'zero' (lost)

Chapter 6, 6.4 · PDF, p. 10

A target is lost if the shooter does not fire at it without a valid reason (for a rafale or simultaneous double this means zero and zero); if the shot misses and no piece visibly comes off, only dust or smoke; if, after a malfunction, the shooter opens the gun or touches the safety before the referee checks it; or if it is the second gun or ammunition malfunction in the same round.

  • Not fired at without valid reason (rafale/simultaneous: zero & zero)
  • Only dust or smoke = zero
  • Gun opened or safety touched before referee's check = zero
  • Second gun/ammunition malfunction in the same round = zero
Terms: Zero · Malfunction · Simultaneous double · Rafale double

Gun or ammunition fault: first incident in a round

Chapter 6, 6.5 "NO BIRD", 6.5.1 "NO BIRD" due to the weapon or ammunition · PDF, p. 10

The first gun or ammunition incident in a round gives the shooter a warning and the target is declared No Bird and repeated. After that first incident, every target not shot for the same reason in that round is scored zero. The table in 6.5.1 says what is repeated in each case (see the next items).

  • First incident in the round: warning + No Bird
  • Later incidents for the same reason in that round: zero
  • Counted per round (25 targets)
Terms: No Bird · Malfunction · Warning · Round

Double discharge (both barrels fire together)

Chapter 6, 6.5.1, table: two shots at the same time (double discharge) · PDF, p. 10

If both barrels go off at once, the referee declares No Bird. On a single the target is repeated; on the first target of a double on report, on a simultaneous double or on a rafale double the whole double is repeated. As with any gun fault, this is the first incident only once per round.

  • No Bird
  • Single: target repeated
  • Double on report (1st target), simultaneous, rafale: double repeated
  • No result recorded (also Article 7.7)
Terms: Double discharge · No Bird · Malfunction

Malfunction on the first shot

Chapter 6, 6.5.1, table: malfunction with the first shot · PDF, p. 10

If the gun or cartridge fails on the first shot, it is a No Bird: a single is repeated, and any double (on report, simultaneous or rafale) is repeated as a whole. However, if the shooter still manages to fire the second shot, that shot counts and the target is scored one or zero as it falls.

  • No Bird on the first-incident basis
  • Single: repeated; any double: repeated
  • If the second shot is fired, the result stands (one or zero)
Terms: Malfunction · No Bird · Single target · Double

Malfunction on the second shot

Chapter 6, 6.5.1, table: malfunction with the second shot · PDF, p. 10

On a single, a second-shot failure gives a No Bird and the target is repeated, but on the repeat it may only be broken with the second shot: breaking it with the first shot scores zero, so the shooter must deliberately miss first. On a double on report the double is repeated and the result of the first shot is kept. On simultaneous and rafale doubles the double is repeated.

  • Single: repeat; only a second-shot break counts
  • Single repeat: break with the first shot = zero
  • Report double: repeated, first-shot result noted
  • Simultaneous / rafale: double repeated
Terms: Malfunction · No Bird · Double on report

Second malfunction in the same round

Chapter 6, 6.4; 6.5.1 (quick guide) · PDF, p. 10

From the second gun or ammunition incident in a round, the target is no longer repeated but scored zero. The referees' quick guide applies this to doubles: on a simultaneous or rafale double, a second failure on the first shot (or both barrels together) gives zero and zero, while a second failure on the second shot keeps the first shot's result and scores the second target zero.

  • Second incident in the round: zero, no repeat
  • Simultaneous/rafale, 2nd time on first shot: zero-zero
  • Simultaneous/rafale, 2nd time on second shot: first result kept, second zero
Terms: Malfunction · Zero · Round

When the target itself is a No Bird

Chapter 6, 6.5.2 «NO BIRD» due to the target · PDF, p. 11

The referee calls No Bird because of the target when: a broken target is thrown; it comes from the wrong trap; two targets leave traps of the same stand; colour, size or type is wrong; the trajectory is judged irregular; it is released more than three seconds after the referee's command; the shooter did not call for it; the shooter was clearly disturbed; or the referee cannot decide whether it was hit.

  • Broken target thrown, wrong trap, or two targets
  • Wrong colour, size or type
  • Irregular trajectory (referee's decision)
  • Released more than 3 s after the command
  • Not called, shooter clearly disturbed, or referee cannot rule
Terms: No Bird · Trajectory · Trap · Referee

Target No Bird on a single and on a rabbit

Chapter 6, 6.5.2, table · PDF, p. 11

On a single, a target-related No Bird simply means the target is repeated. A special case is a rabbit missed with the first shot that then breaks by itself (for example on the ground) before the second shot: it is a No Bird and the rabbit is repeated, but on the repeat only a break with the second shot counts; a first-shot break scores zero.

  • Single: target repeated
  • Rabbit missed then broken before 2nd shot: No Bird
  • On the repeat only a second-shot break counts
  • First-shot break on the repeat = zero
Terms: No Bird · Rabbit · Single target

Target No Bird in doubles

Chapter 6, 6.5.2, table · PDF, p. 11

Double on report: if the first target is irregular, the double is repeated. If the first target (or its pieces) breaks the second before the shooter fires the second shot, or if the second target is irregular, the double is repeated and the first target's result is kept. On simultaneous and rafale doubles an irregular target means the double is repeated with no score recorded.

  • Report double, 1st target irregular: repeat double
  • Report double, 2nd broken by 1st or 2nd irregular: repeat, 1st result kept
  • Simultaneous / rafale: repeat, no score established
Terms: No Bird · Double on report · Simultaneous double · Rafale double

Two targets broken with one shot

Chapter 6, 6.5.2, note under the table; Chapter 7, 7.9 · PDF, p. 11

In any type of double, if a single shot breaks both targets, both are scored: 'one' and 'one'.

  • Applies to all doubles
  • Result: one + one
Terms: Double · One

Part II · Chapter 7Weapons and ammunition

Permitted shotguns

Chapter 7, 7.1 Definition · PDF, p. 11

Any hunting shotgun may be used, including semi-automatics, but pump-action guns are not allowed. The calibre may not exceed 12 gauge and barrels must be at least 66 cm long. Micro cameras fitted to the gun and any artificial aiming device are forbidden.

  • Hunting shotguns incl. semi-automatics
  • No pump-action guns
  • Maximum 12 gauge
  • Minimum barrel length 66 cm
  • No gun cameras or artificial sights
Terms: Shotgun · Semi-automatic · Gauge

Carrying and storing guns

Chapter 7, 7.1 Definition · PDF, p. 11

Every gun, even unloaded, must be handled with great care. Guns are carried open, semi-automatics with the breech open, muzzle pointing straight up or straight down. Straps and slings are not permitted. When not in use, guns stand upright in a gun rack or a similar place, with semi-automatic breeches open.

  • Always carried open, semi-autos with breech open
  • Muzzle straight up or down
  • No straps or slings
  • Stored upright in a gun rack, semi-auto breech open
Terms: Gun handling · Safety · Semi-automatic

Other shooters' guns; sharing a gun

Chapter 7, 7.2 · PDF, p. 12

Nobody may touch another shooter's gun without permission. In an official competition or championship two shooters in the same squad may not use or share the same gun.

  • Do not touch another's gun without permission
  • No gun sharing within a squad in official competitions
Terms: Squad · Shotgun

Borrowing or replacing a gun, choke or barrel

Chapter 7, 7.3 · PDF, p. 12

Exceptionally, if a gun breaks down, the shooter may borrow another competitor's gun, with that person's consent, to finish the round. During a round the whole gun, a removable choke or a barrel may be changed between stands or between the singles and the doubles, provided it causes no delay.

  • Borrowing allowed after a malfunction, with the owner's agreement
  • Change of gun, choke or barrel allowed between stands
  • Or between singles and doubles
  • Must not cause delay
Terms: Choke · Malfunction · Shotgun

No changes once on the stand

Chapter 7, 7.4 · PDF, p. 12

Once the shooter has stepped onto the shooting stand, changing the gun, choke or barrel is no longer allowed.

  • Changes forbidden on the stand
  • Applies to gun, choke and barrel
Terms: Choke · Shooting stand

15 seconds between targets

Chapter 7, 7.5 · PDF, p. 12

A shooter has at most fifteen seconds between single targets or between doubles. Going over this limit first earns a warning from the referee; after that the penalties of Article 12.2 apply.

  • Max 15 seconds between singles or doubles
  • First time: warning
  • Then Article 12.2 (red card, zero)
Terms: Warning · Firing time

What happens after a gun malfunction

Chapter 7, 7.6 · PDF, p. 12

In one round, only the first gun malfunction is a No Bird; any later one in the same round is zero. What the shooter does next depends on the repair. Fixed before the squad has finished the stand: the shooter may shoot last there, if the referee allows it. Cannot be fixed: the shooter may stay with the squad, with the referee's agreement, by switching to another gun at once (7.3). Neither: the shooter leaves the squad and can shoot the remaining targets only in a free place in another squad, and only if the jury allows it.

  • 1st malfunction in a round: No Bird; later ones: zero
  • Fixed in time: may shoot last on that stand
  • Cannot be fixed: stay only by switching guns at once
  • Otherwise: finish later in another squad, with the jury's approval
Terms: Malfunction · No Bird · Jury · Squad

Double discharge due to a gun fault

Chapter 7, 7.7 · PDF, p. 12

Sometimes a faulty gun fires both barrels at once. If that happens on a single or on the first target of a double, the referee calls No Bird and no score is written down (see the table in 6.5).

  • Both barrels at once on a single or the first target of a double
  • No Bird, no score written down
  • Counts as a malfunction incident
Terms: Double discharge · No Bird

Cartridge rules

Chapter 7, 7.8 · PDF, p. 12

The shot load may not exceed 28 g of lead, with a maximum tolerance of +2%. Pellets must be spherical with a diameter of 2.0 to 2.5 mm (tolerance ±0.1 mm). Dispersers or other unusual loading devices, reloaded cartridges, mixed shot of different sizes or qualities, black powder and tracer cartridges are all strictly forbidden.

  • Max 28 g lead, tolerance +2%
  • Spherical shot 2.0–2.5 mm (±0.1 mm)
  • No dispersers or reloaded cartridges
  • No mixed shot sizes/qualities
  • No black powder, no tracers
Terms: Cartridge · Shot size

Cartridge checks by the referee

Chapter 7, 7.10 · PDF, p. 13

Before the shooter starts at a stand, the referee may ask for two cartridges to check, taking one from the right pocket of the vest and one from the left pocket.

  • Referee may take 2 cartridges
  • One from the right pocket, one from the left
  • Before shooting at the stand
Terms: Cartridge · Referee

Release trigger: mandatory 'R' sticker

Chapter 7, 7.11 Release trigger mandatory marking · PDF, p. 13

Everyone on the stand must be able to see that a gun has a release trigger. So the gun carries a fluorescent sticker with a large letter 'R', placed on the outside of the stock. Where the law of the host country forbids release triggers, the programme of the international competition has to announce it.

  • Fluorescent sticker with a large 'R'
  • On the outside of the stock
  • A national ban is announced in the programme
Terms: Release trigger

Release-pull trigger: what to do on a No Bird

Chapter 17, 17.1 · PDF, p. 24

Breaking the dress or competition-number rule first brings a warning from the referee. If the shooter still does not put it right, more penalties follow; the jury can go as far as excluding the shooter from the competition.

  • First: a warning from the referee
  • Still not put right: more penalties
  • The jury may go as far as exclusion

Dress at the opening and closing ceremonies

Chapter 17, 17.1 · PDF, p. 24

In the parade of national teams at the opening ceremony, team members wear their national team outfit or pressed trousers with a blazer. All prize winners must attend the prize-giving at the closing ceremony, dressed the same way: national team outfit or pressed trousers and a blazer.

  • Opening parade: national team attire or pressed trousers + blazer
  • Prize winners must attend the prize-giving
  • Same dress options at the closing ceremony

Part III · Chapter 18Shoot-offs

Shoot-off for the top three; ties below

Chapter 18, 18.1; 18.2 · PDF, p. 24

Ties for the first three individual places are settled by a shoot-off, on a new layout set by the jury if time allows. Below third place there is no shoot-off: tied shooters share the place and are listed by counting back their scores from the 8th layout to the 1st.

  • Shoot-off only for places 1–3
  • New layout set by the jury if time allows
  • Below 3rd: ranked equal
  • Order by countback from layout 8 to layout 1
Terms: Shoot-off · Countback · Jury

Shoot-off format and sudden death

Chapter 18, 18.3 · PDF, p. 25

A shoot-off is a round of 25 targets. If still tied, a second round is shot in which the first shooter to score a zero is eliminated (sudden death), provided all have shot the same number of targets; the last one remaining wins. In sudden death only, a double on report counts as a pair of two targets regardless of which one was missed, so 'one-zero' equals 'zero-one'.

  • Shoot-off = round of 25 targets
  • Still tied: second round, sudden death
  • Same number of targets shot before elimination
  • Report double in sudden death: one-zero = zero-one
Terms: Shoot-off · Sudden death · Double on report

Shoot-off squads, call-up and postponement

Chapter 18, 18.4; 18.5; 18.6 · PDF, p. 25

A shoot-off is shot under the usual rules, except that free places in the squad stay empty. When a shoot-off was not in the programme, the shooters involved keep in touch with the jury and must be able to shoot within 15 minutes of the call; a shooter who is not there is disqualified. In a case of force majeure the jury may move the shoot-off to the next day, and again any absent shooter is disqualified.

  • Usual rules; free squad places stay empty
  • Not in the programme: ready within 15 minutes of the call
  • Not there = disqualified
  • Force majeure: the jury may move it to the next day
Terms: Shoot-off · Jury · Squad

Team ties

Chapter 18, 18.7 · PDF, p. 25

Teams with equal totals are separated by what their members scored together on the 8th layout. If that is equal too, the 7th layout decides, then the 6th, and so on back to the 1st. Teams never shoot a shoot-off.

  • First: the team's combined score on layout 8
  • Then layouts 7, 6 … down to 1
  • No team shoot-off

Common questions

Where must my gun be before I call for the target?

Both feet inside the stand, heel of the stock touching your body, and the top rear point of the stock below the line on your vest, 25 cm under the shoulder axis. Keep it there until the target is out and visible (Chapter 3, 3.1.1).

Do I need a line on my shooting vest? How far below the shoulder?

Yes. The line is 25 cm (9.85 inches) below the shoulder axis, parallel to it, in a colour that contrasts with the vest, so the referee can judge your ready position.

I have an adjustable comb. Does the low-gun rule still apply?

Yes. The rule covers standard stocks, Monte Carlo stocks and stocks with an adjustable comb or any device that shortens the mount: the highest rear point of the stock must be below the vest line.

What happens if I mount the gun too early?

The first time you get a verbal warning with a yellow card. Any further offence on the same layout brings a red card: zero on a single, zero and No Bird on a double on report, zero and zero on simultaneous or rafale doubles.

Can I keep the gun on my shoulder between the two targets of a double?

Yes. Between the first and second target of any double you may hold the gun as you like. Before the first target the normal ready position applies.

May I shoot a rabbit from the hip?

No. Every target, rabbits included, must be shot with the gun mounted to the shoulder.

What do I say to call for a target, and how fast must it come?

Call 'ready' or 'pull'. The referee relays it with an audio signal and the target must be released within three seconds of that signal. A later target is a No Bird.

How much time do I have between targets?

At most fifteen seconds between singles or between doubles. Exceeding it gives a warning first, then the penalties of 12.2.

How quickly must I continue after the referee has ruled on my protest?

Within fifteen seconds of the final ruling. Otherwise it is a refusal to comply, punishable under Chapter 13.

What is the difference between simultaneous and rafale doubles?

A simultaneous double is two targets thrown at the same moment from one or two traps. A rafale is two targets from the same trap on the same trajectory, one after the other at the trap's reload cycle. In both you may shoot the targets in any order.

Can I use both cartridges on one target of a double?

Yes. You have only two cartridges for a double, but both may be fired at the same target.

I broke both targets of a double with one shot. What is the score?

Both count: 'one' and 'one', in any type of double.

How many targets are on one layout and how are they split?

25 targets: 15 singles and 5 doubles, whatever the layout type. A full international competition is 200 targets, in rounds of 25.

What clay types can appear in Sporting?

Standard, midi, super mini, battue, rabbit, ZZ and flash targets. The target menu at the stand must say when special sizes (90, 70, 60 mm), battue or rabbit are used.

Will the targets be shown to me before I shoot?

All trajectories are shown at each stand to the first shooter of the squad. On request the referee repeats up to two targets per menu target. Simultaneous and rafale doubles are shown (repeat up to two), but doubles on report are never shown.

Can I mount the gun and track the targets during the presentation?

No. Firing or mock firing during the presentation is forbidden and is penalised under Articles 12.1 and 12.2. Guns must be open and unloaded while targets are shown.

In what order do we shoot at a stand?

Each member of the squad shoots the singles first, then the doubles, in rotation; the order rotates at each stand and for the doubles, unless the jury decides otherwise.

A tiny piece came off the target. Is it a hit?

Yes, if at least one piece visibly broke off and you fired in line with Article 3.1. Only dust or 'smoke' without a visible piece is a zero.

Can other shooters in my squad say whether my target was hit?

No. The referee decides alone and squad members may not intervene. Only the referee, if in doubt, may ask them for advice.

I didn't shoot at a correct target. What is the result?

Zero, unless you had a valid reason. On a simultaneous or rafale double it is zero and zero. According to the referees' quick guide, on a double on report the first target is zero and the double is repeated to establish the second.

Can I refuse a target that I think was bad?

You may not refuse a target you called for. Only the referee decides whether a trajectory is correct or a No Bird. If you have not called, you may let it go.

When does the referee call No Bird because of the target?

Broken target thrown, wrong trap, two targets from the same stand, wrong colour/size/type, irregular trajectory, release more than 3 seconds after the command, target not called, shooter clearly disturbed, or the referee cannot judge it.

The second target of my report pair was irregular. Do I keep my first result?

Yes. The double is repeated and the result of the first target is noted; the repeat only establishes the second target.

I missed a rabbit with my first shot, then it broke on a stone. Is it scored?

It is a No Bird and the rabbit is repeated. On the repeat only a break with the second shot counts; a break with the first shot is zero, so you must miss the first deliberately.

Can I shoot at a target the referee has already called No Bird?

No, never. After a first warning, doing so is penalised: zero on a single, zero and No Bird on a report pair, zero and zero on simultaneous or rafale.

My gun clicked on the first shot. What should I do?

Stay in place, keep the gun pointing down the range, do not open it or touch the safety, and wait for the referee to check it. If it is your first gun or ammunition incident in the round, it is a No Bird with a warning and the target or double is repeated.

I opened my gun after a misfire before the referee looked at it. What happens?

The target or targets are scored zero.

How many malfunctions am I allowed in a round?

One. The first gun or ammunition incident in a round is a No Bird with a warning. From the second incident in the same round, targets are scored zero instead of being repeated.

My second barrel failed on a single. How is it repeated?

It is a No Bird and the target is repeated, but on the repeat only a break with the second shot counts. If you break it with the first shot it is zero.

My second barrel failed on a double on report. What is kept?

The double is repeated and the result of your first shot is noted.

Both barrels fired at once. Is that a lost target?

No. A double discharge due to a gun fault on a single or on the first target of a double is a No Bird with no result recorded; the target or double is repeated. It counts as your malfunction incident for the round.

My gun broke during the round. Can I borrow one?

Yes, exceptionally, with the owner's agreement, to finish the round. If your own gun is repaired before the squad finishes the stand, you may shoot last with the referee's permission. If you cannot get another gun at once, you leave the squad and finish later in a free place in another squad with jury approval.

Can two of us share one gun in the same squad?

Not in an official competition or championship. Borrowing is only an emergency solution after a malfunction.

Can I change chokes during a round?

Yes, between stands or between singles and doubles, if it causes no delay. Once you are on the shooting stand, no changes are allowed.

Can I fire a test shot on the stand to check my gun?

Never on the shooting stand. Use the test stand near the clubhouse before going to the layout.

I have a release trigger. What must I do?

Mark the gun: a fluorescent sticker with a large 'R' goes on the outside of the stock. If a No Bird is called on your first shot, do not let go of the trigger. Then either call for a new target, or open the gun with the trigger still held, or fire in the safe direction the referee shows you.

Which guns are allowed? Can I use a pump-action or a 20 gauge?

Any hunting shotgun up to 12 gauge with barrels of at least 66 cm, including semi-automatics. Pump-action guns are not allowed. A smaller gauge such as 20 is within the 'not exceeding 12 gauge' limit.

What cartridges may I use?

Lead shot load up to 28 g (+2% tolerance), spherical pellets 2.0–2.5 mm (±0.1 mm). No reloads, dispersers, mixed shot, black powder or tracers. The referee may take two cartridges from your vest pockets (one right, one left) to check.

How many cartridges can I put in a semi-automatic?

A maximum of two. Load only on the stand, gun pointing down the range, after the referee allows shooting to start.

How should I carry my gun between stands? Is a sling allowed?

Always open (semi-automatics with breech open), muzzle straight up or down. Slings and straps are not allowed; when not in use, the gun stands upright in a rack.

Are ear protection and glasses really compulsory?

Yes, for shooters, referees, staff and anyone near a stand (hearing protection also for spectators). A shooter on the stand without either is treated as absent and Article 16.7 applies.

Can my coach or interpreter give me tips while I shoot?

No coaching is allowed during the competition. If the referee sees coaching, you get a yellow card and repeats lead to Chapter 13 penalties; the person coaching is removed from the shooting area. An interpreter should limit themselves to translating, not advising.

Can I practise my swing before calling?

No. Mounting and swinging along the target line before calling counts as mock shooting. The first time is a warning; after that a repeat is scored zero on the next target hit.

Do I have to pick up my empty cases?

Drop them in the bin if the stand has one. With a semi-automatic you must collect your ejected cases after leaving the stand and bin them. Non-compliance brings yellow/red card sanctions.

How do I protest a referee's call on the stand?

Immediately, before calling the next target: raise your hand and say 'appeal' or 'protest', then give your reason. The referee either confirms at once or consults another referee, the trapper or the squad, then gives a final decision.

Can I appeal a hit/miss decision to the jury?

No. Decisions on 'one' or 'zero', on No Bird and on whether a trajectory is correct cannot go to the jury. Also no appeal if the referee rules a target was shot behind an obstacle or beyond a limit.

How much does a written protest to the jury cost?

You pay a deposit of 40% of the entry fee. It is refunded if the jury agrees with you; otherwise it goes to a charity chosen by the FITASC Chairman.

Can a jury decision be appealed?

Only if an appeal jury has been set up for that competition (FITASC Chairman, technical commission chairman and organising member's chairman). Without an appeal jury, jury decisions are final.

The score on my card is wrong. What do I do?

Check your score as you leave each stand and tell the referee immediately. The referee alone decides, possibly after consulting; after that no appeal is accepted.

What penalties can the jury give?

For a first breach a warning; for a repeat offence, refusal to comply or influencing the referee: loss of one target, loss of a 25-target round, or expulsion. Immediate expulsion is possible for loss of self-control, refusal to comply, influencing a referee or deliberately shooting live animals.

What counts as trying to influence the referee?

Continuing to dispute a final ruling after shooting resumes, conspicuously challenging decisions, or other shooters openly criticising a ruling without the referee's permission.

If I am expelled, do I get my entry fee back?

No. Expelled shooters receive no refund or compensation. The expulsion is reported to your federation and FITASC and kept on record for five years; a second within three years can lead to a permanent ban.

Can I shoot in shorts and sandals?

Short shorts are not allowed; Bermuda-style shorts are fine if they end no higher than 5 cm above the knee. Sandals are banned for safety.

Is a sleeveless top allowed?

No. Shirts need at least short sleeves and a collar or a round T-shirt neck. A vest worn with nothing underneath is not allowed.

Where do I wear my competitor number?

On the upper back between shoulders and waist, fully visible and uncut. The first breach gets a warning; not correcting it can lead to further penalties up to exclusion by the jury.

What should I wear for the prize-giving?

Prize winners must attend, in national team attire or pressed trousers and a blazer. The same applies to the opening parade of national teams.

I arrived late for my squad. Can I still shoot?

If you arrive before your singles turn on the first stand, you join without penalty. Otherwise all targets you have not shot are zero, and you may not shoot that layout in another squad. If the delay was beyond your control, do not rejoin; appeal to the jury in writing instead.

Shooting stopped for a long time. Will the targets be shown again?

Yes, if a trap broke down or the interruption lasted more than ten minutes, the referee must show the stand targets again.

How are ties for a medal decided?

Ties for places 1–3 are shot off over a round of 25 targets, preferably on a new layout. If still tied, a second round is shot on sudden death: first zero is eliminated, provided all have shot the same number of targets.

I tied for 5th place. Is there a shoot-off?

No. Below third place tied shooters share the place and are listed by countback from the 8th layout to the 1st.

How long do I have to get to an unplanned shoot-off?

Keep in touch with the jury: once you are called, you must be ready to shoot within 15 minutes. A shooter who is not there is disqualified.

How are team ties broken?

By the team's total on the 8th layout, then by counting back through layouts 7 to 1.

Does the rulebook define age or gender categories (Lady, Junior, Veteran)?

No. This Sporting rulebook covers the shooting, refereeing and organisation rules but does not list categories. FITASC publishes them in a separate table on its website (Competitions, Categories); from 1 January 2027: Junior 13–20, Lady from 13, Men (Homme) 21–55, Senior 56–65, Veteran 66–72, Master from 73, counted by year of birth. National events may use their own categories, as stated in the programme. The rulebook only mentions senior teams when describing the jury.

We want to host an international Sporting event on a new ground. What is required?

FITASC has to approve any layout for an international event. If your ground is not approved yet, a FITASC representative must see the ground and your organisation plan no later than eight months before the date, and FITASC may ask you to name a course designer. Even when a club runs the event, your federation keeps the responsibility.

How many shooters can we accept with 4 lines over two days?

Appendix 2 gives 258 to 408 shooters depending on pace and daily hours: for example 336 shooters (56 squads) at 10 minutes per squad with 9:55 of shooting, up to 408 (68 squads) with 11:55.

Can shooters practise on the competition layouts before the event?

No. Before the events, nobody may shoot practice targets on the layouts that the layout officials have set for the competition.

Can the stand referee change a trajectory if the wind changes?

Only in exceptional cases (trap malfunction or exceptional weather change) and only to reset a trajectory where the target fails to appear or is dangerous. It is advised to have the course designer or a jury member check it.

How should the trajectories of a stand be graded for difficulty?

Recommended: one class A (over 80% broken by an average shooter), two class B (60–80%) and one class C (40–60%). A fifth single may only be A or B. A C target may appear only once in the doubles, preferably with an A.

Who keeps the scorecard and what does the referee do with it?

The referee, or a person under the referee's supervision. A different coloured pencil is used for each stand; before the squad moves on the referee photographs the card and marks with an asterisk the shooter who carries it.

Can the referee stop shooting because of a thunderstorm?

Yes, in exceptional circumstances for a sudden heavy shower or violent storm expected to be short. If it will last longer, the jury must be informed. Guns are opened and unloaded during the interruption.
