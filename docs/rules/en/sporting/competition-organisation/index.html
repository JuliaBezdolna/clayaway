Competition organisation

Chapter 15 of the FITASC Sporting™ rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

15.1 · 15.2PDF, p. 21 ↗

Who approves layouts

The national federation approves layouts for national competitions; FITASC approves them for international ones. If a federation organising an international championship wants to use a ground that is not approved yet, a FITASC representative must see the ground and the organisation plan no later than eight months before the competition. FITASC can also make the federation name a course designer who designs the layouts and is responsible for running them.

  • National events: approved by the national federation
  • International events: approved by FITASC
  • Ground not approved yet: shown to FITASC no later than 8 months ahead
  • FITASC may ask for a course designer

Maximum capacity (appendices)

How many shooters an event can take depends on the type of organisation and the number of competition days. Appendix 1 gives the figures for old-system layouts and Appendix 2 for the new-system lines with 4 stations.

  • Depends on organisation type and number of days
  • Appendix 1: old-system layouts (page 27)
  • Appendix 2: new-system 4-station lines (page 28)

Capacity for old-system layouts

The table assumes 45 minutes per squad per layout and gives three options for two, three and four days with 4 or 8 layouts. With 4 layouts the range is from 78 shooters (13 squads, 9:45 of shooting) to 96 (16 squads, 12:00) over two days, up to 120 (20 squads) over three days and up to 192 (32 squads) over four days. With 8 layouts the figures double at the top: up to 192 over two days, 240 over three and 384 (64 squads) over four.

  • 45 minutes per squad per layout
  • 4 layouts: max 96 (2 days), 120 (3 days), 192 (4 days)
  • 8 layouts: max 192 (2 days), 240 (3 days), 384 (4 days)
  • Daily shooting time 9:00 to 12:00 depending on the option

Capacity for new-system lines

For 4 or 8 lines the table gives options at 10, 11, 12 and 13 minutes per squad, for two, three and four days. With 4 lines at 10 minutes the capacity runs from 336 shooters (56 squads) over two days up to 816 (136 squads) over four days; with 8 lines at 10 minutes, from 672 up to 1632 (272 squads). Daily shooting time in the table is roughly 9:12 to 11:57.

  • Options: 10, 11, 12, 13 minutes per squad
  • 4 lines, 10 min: 336 (2 days) to 816 (4 days)
  • 8 lines, 10 min: 672 (2 days) to 1632 (4 days)
  • Slower pace (13 min) lowers capacity

Delegating to a club

A FITASC member federation may hand the organisation of a competition to one of its clubs, but FITASC still holds the federation fully responsible for applying everything that was approved.

  • Federation may delegate to a club
  • Federation remains fully responsible to FITASC
Terms:Organiser

Who sits on the jury

The jury at an international event has these members: the FITASC sporting commission members who are present, plus one representative from every nation that enters a senior team (the rulebook's wording). Its chair is the representative of the FITASC member organising the event. FITASC Management Committee members are jury members by right, unless they sit on the appeal jury.

  • FITASC sporting commission members who are present
  • One representative per nation with a senior team
  • Chair: the organising member's representative
  • Management Committee members by right, unless on the appeal jury
15.4.2–15.4.4PDF, p. 21 ↗

How the jury decides

The jury decides by majority of members present; in a tie the chairman or the chairman's representative has the casting vote. A decision is valid only if the chairman (or representative) and at least a quarter of the members are present. In an emergency, for example if shooting risks being stopped, two members named by the chairman may take an exceptional decision that the full jury must later ratify.

  • Majority of members present
  • Tie: chairman's casting vote
  • Quorum: chairman + at least 1/4 of members
  • Emergency: 2 appointed members, later ratified
15.5PDF, p. 21 ↗

Duties of the jury

Before shooting, technical commission members on the jury check the ranges and layouts. After the opening ceremony the organiser announces the shooting times set by the jury. The jury makes sure rules and safety are respected, may test guns, cartridges and targets with technical equipment, decides on technical problems if the chief referee has not, hears appeals and sets penalties. At least two jury members must always be on the shooting ground. A jury member who sees an irregularity must not intervene with the referees directly but must report it to the jury.

  • Checks layouts before shooting
  • Sets shooting times, announced after the opening ceremony
  • May test guns, ammunition and targets
  • Hears appeals and imposes penalties
  • At least 2 members always on the ground
  • Members report irregularities to the jury, not to referees
15.6.1–15.6.4PDF, p. 22 ↗

Presenting and validating layouts

In the days before the competition the course designers present the layouts, trajectories and planned target types to the jury members responsible for the layouts and to the chief referee. The proposals can be changed to satisfy technical, refereeing, safety and environmental rules and the spirit of FITASC events. Layouts are final only after the jury members and the chief referee validate them; the chief referee then briefs each stand referee.

  • Course designers present layouts before the event
  • To responsible jury members and the chief referee
  • Changes possible for technical, safety, environmental reasons
  • Final only after validation
  • Chief referee briefs stand referees
15.6.5PDF, p. 22 ↗

Referee may not change trajectories (exceptions)

The trajectories chosen by the jury or the course designers are not the stand referee's to change. There are two exceptions, after a trap breakdown or an unusual change in the weather: the referee may put a faulty trajectory right when the target does not come out, or when the referee judges it dangerous. Either way, it is advisable to have the course designer or a jury member check the corrected trajectory.

  • Trajectories set by the jury or course designers stay as they are
  • Exception: trap breakdown or unusual weather change
  • When the target does not come out or is dangerous
  • Preferably checked by the course designer or a jury member
15.6.6PDF, p. 22 ↗

No practice on competition layouts

Before the events, nobody may practise on the layouts defined by the layout officials.

  • No practice on the competition layouts before the event
  • Practice only on other ranges
15.7PDF, p. 22 ↗

Appeal jury

At an international competition an appeal jury can be formed to deal with protests against the jury's decisions, whether they come from shooters or from FITASC. Its three members are the FITASC Chairman, the chairman of the technical commission and the chairman of the FITASC member organising the event; each may send a representative. It is formed together with the jury. Where there is no appeal jury, nobody can overturn the jury: its decisions are final.

  • Optional, for an international competition
  • Deals with protests against jury decisions
  • FITASC Chairman, technical commission chairman, organising member's chairman (or representatives)
  • Formed together with the jury
  • No appeal jury: jury decisions are final

Common questions on this chapter

Can a jury decision be appealed?

Only if an appeal jury has been set up for that competition (FITASC Chairman, technical commission chairman and organising member's chairman). Without an appeal jury, jury decisions are final.

Does the rulebook define age or gender categories (Lady, Junior, Veteran)?

No. This Sporting rulebook covers the shooting, refereeing and organisation rules but does not list categories. FITASC publishes them in a separate table on its website (Competitions, Categories); from 1 January 2027: Junior 13–20, Lady from 13, Men (Homme) 21–55, Senior 56–65, Veteran 66–72, Master from 73, counted by year of birth. National events may use their own categories, as stated in the programme. The rulebook only mentions senior teams when describing the jury.

We want to host an international Sporting event on a new ground. What is required?

FITASC has to approve any layout for an international event. If your ground is not approved yet, a FITASC representative must see the ground and your organisation plan no later than eight months before the date, and FITASC may ask you to name a course designer. Even when a club runs the event, your federation keeps the responsibility.

How many shooters can we accept with 4 lines over two days?

Appendix 2 gives 258 to 408 shooters depending on pace and daily hours: for example 336 shooters (56 squads) at 10 minutes per squad with 9:55 of shooting, up to 408 (68 squads) with 11:55.

Can shooters practise on the competition layouts before the event?

No. Before the events, nobody may shoot practice targets on the layouts that the layout officials have set for the competition.

Can the stand referee change a trajectory if the wind changes?

Only in exceptional cases (trap malfunction or exceptional weather change) and only to reset a trajectory where the target fails to appear or is dangerous. It is advised to have the course designer or a jury member check it.
