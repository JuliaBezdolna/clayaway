Championship procedure

Chapter 16 of the FITASC Sporting™ rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

16.1PDF, p. 23 ↗

200 targets in rounds of 25

An international competition is 200 targets, shot in rounds of 25. In exceptional cases the jury may change the number if it is essential; shooters are then reimbursed by the organiser for unused targets at the practice-target price.

  • 200 targets
  • Round = 25 targets
  • Jury may change it in exceptional cases
  • Unused targets refunded at practice price
16.2PDF, p. 23 ↗

Squads of six and rotation

A draw puts the shooters into squads of six. Who shoots first changes from stand to stand, and it changes for the doubles too. At international events the organisers try to put team-mates from one nation into different squads. Squad lists and times are published by the organising committee beforehand.

  • Squads of 6, by draw
  • Order rotates on each stand and for doubles
  • Team-mates put in different squads where possible
  • Lists and times published in advance
More:Rotation
16.3PDF, p. 23 ↗

Singles first, then doubles

On each stand the squad works through the singles first: every shooter in turn. Then the doubles follow in the same rotation. The jury may decide on a different order.

  • Singles first, each shooter in turn
  • Then the doubles in the same rotation
  • The jury may set another order
16.4 · 16.5PDF, p. 23 ↗

Presentation of targets

On every stand the first shooter of the squad, already standing ready on the stand, is shown all the trajectories. If the shooter on the stand asks, the referee shows them again, but no more than two targets for each target of the menu. Nobody may fire or pretend to fire while targets are being shown; doing so brings Articles 12.1 and 12.2 into play.

  • All trajectories shown to the first shooter
  • That shooter is already on the stand
  • Shown again on request: up to 2 per menu target
  • No firing, no pretend firing (12.1, 12.2)
More:Target presentation Mock shooting
16.6PDF, p. 23 ↗

Which doubles are shown

Doubles on report are never demonstrated. Simultaneous and rafale doubles are shown to the first shooter of each squad, and the referee will show them again on request, up to two shown doubles.

  • Doubles on report: not demonstrated
  • Simultaneous and rafale: shown to the first shooter
  • Shown again on request: up to 2 doubles
More:Target presentation Simultaneous double
16.7PDF, p. 23 ↗

Late or absent shooter

Shooters must be on time. If a shooter is not there when called, the referee calls the name and number three times within one minute. A shooter who arrives before missing his turn on the singles of the first stand joins without penalty. If he misses his turn, all singles and doubles not shot are scored zero; arriving at a later stand (2, 3, 4 and so on), all targets not shot on earlier stands are zero. A layout where stands were missed can never be shot in another squad.

  • Name and number called 3 times within 1 minute
  • Before his singles turn on stand 1: no penalty
  • Missed turn: unshot targets = zero
  • Joining at a later stand: earlier stands = zero
  • Never re-shoot the layout in another squad
16.8PDF, p. 24 ↗

Lateness beyond the shooter's control

A shooter who believes there was a good reason for being late, outside their control, must not rejoin the squad during the round, must appeal to the jury in writing, accept the jury's decision and, if the jury allows it, shoot the round in another squad. If the jury rejects the reason, the shooter gets 25 zeros for each layout not shot.

  • Do not rejoin the squad during the round
  • Written appeal to the jury
  • Abide by the decision
  • If allowed: shoot in another squad
  • Reason rejected: 25 zeros per layout not shot
16.9PDF, p. 24 ↗

Interruption over 10 minutes

If a trap breaks down or shooting is stopped for more than ten minutes, the referee must show the targets of that stand to the shooters again.

  • Trap breakdown or interruption over 10 minutes
  • Targets of the stand shown again
More:Target presentation
16.10 · 16.11PDF, p. 24 ↗

Recording results and checking your score

At international events results are recorded by the referee or a representative, who may be a shooter, and each round is posted on a central scoreboard. When leaving each stand, shooters must check that their score is correct on the card. A disagreement must be raised with the referee at once; the referee alone decides, possibly after taking advice as in 9.5.2, and no appeal is possible after that.

  • Recorded by referee or representative (may be a shooter)
  • Each round on a central scoreboard
  • Check your score when leaving each stand
  • Tell the referee immediately
  • Referee decides; no appeal afterwards

Common questions on this chapter

How many targets are on one layout and how are they split?

25 targets: 15 singles and 5 doubles, whatever the layout type. A full international competition is 200 targets, in rounds of 25.

Will the targets be shown to me before I shoot?

All trajectories are shown at each stand to the first shooter of the squad. On request the referee repeats up to two targets per menu target. Simultaneous and rafale doubles are shown (repeat up to two), but doubles on report are never shown.

Can I mount the gun and track the targets during the presentation?

No. Firing or mock firing during the presentation is forbidden and is penalised under Articles 12.1 and 12.2. Guns must be open and unloaded while targets are shown.

In what order do we shoot at a stand?

Each member of the squad shoots the singles first, then the doubles, in rotation; the order rotates at each stand and for the doubles, unless the jury decides otherwise.

Are ear protection and glasses really compulsory?

Yes, for shooters, referees, staff and anyone near a stand (hearing protection also for spectators). A shooter on the stand without either is treated as absent and Article 16.7 applies.

The score on my card is wrong. What do I do?

Check your score as you leave each stand and tell the referee immediately. The referee alone decides, possibly after consulting; after that no appeal is accepted.

I arrived late for my squad. Can I still shoot?

If you arrive before your singles turn on the first stand, you join without penalty. Otherwise all targets you have not shot are zero, and you may not shoot that layout in another squad. If the delay was beyond your control, do not rejoin; appeal to the jury in writing instead.

Shooting stopped for a long time. Will the targets be shown again?

Yes, if a trap broke down or the interruption lasted more than ten minutes, the referee must show the stand targets again.

Does the rulebook define age or gender categories (Lady, Junior, Veteran)?

No. This Sporting rulebook covers the shooting, refereeing and organisation rules but does not list categories. FITASC publishes them in a separate table on its website (Competitions, Categories); from 1 January 2027: Junior 13–20, Lady from 13, Men (Homme) 21–55, Senior 56–65, Veteran 66–72, Master from 73, counted by year of birth. National events may use their own categories, as stated in the programme. The rulebook only mentions senior teams when describing the jury.
