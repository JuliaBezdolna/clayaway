Rules of conduct / Safety

Chapter 8 of the FITASC Sporting™ rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

8.1PDF, p. 14 ↗

Loss of self-control

If a shooter clearly loses self-control, for example by throwing the gun down, using abusive language or behaving violently towards the referee or other competitors, the referee must report it to the jury as fast as possible. The jury can expel the shooter immediately (13.4).

  • Throwing the gun, abuse, violence
  • Referee refers to the jury at once
  • Possible immediate expulsion (13.4)
8.2PDF, p. 14 ↗

Shoot only in turn; not at others' targets or animals

A shooter fires only in their own turn and only at a target that has been launched. Aiming or shooting at another shooter's target, or knowingly at live animals, is forbidden; deliberately firing at live animals can lead to expulsion (13.4).

  • Only in your own turn
  • Only after a target is launched
  • Never at another shooter's target
  • Never knowingly at live animals (13.4)
More:Safety
8.2PDF, p. 14 ↗

Mock shooting and accidental shots

Mock shooting is not allowed on or off the ranges. If, before calling, a shooter mounts the gun and swings along the imagined line of the target, or fires a shot unintentionally, the referee gives a warning. After that first warning, any repeat is scored zero on the next target the shooter hits (wording of the rule).

  • No mock shooting anywhere on the ground
  • Includes swinging along the trajectory before calling
  • Includes an involuntary shot
  • 1st time: warning
  • Repeat: zero on the next target hit
8.3PDF, p. 14 ↗

Be ready; wait for the stand to clear

A called shooter must be able to start straight away and bring enough cartridges and kit for the whole round. Nobody walks up to the stand while the shooter before them is still on it, and only when it is their own turn.

  • Ready to start as soon as called
  • Cartridges and kit for the whole round
  • Wait until the stand is free and it is your turn
More:Squad
8.4 · 8.5PDF, p. 14 ↗

When and how to load

Three conditions must all be met before loading: the shooter is on the stand, the gun points down the range, and the referee has said shooting may start. A semi-automatic may take two cartridges at most.

  • Only on the stand
  • Gun pointing down the range
  • Only after the referee says shooting may start
  • Semi-auto: 2 cartridges at most
More:Safety
8.6PDF, p. 14 ↗

Turning around; empty cases

Before turning around on the stand, the shooter must open the gun and remove all cartridges, fired or not. Where there is a bin for empty cases, they go in it; users of semi-automatics must pick up their ejected cases after leaving the stand and bin them. Breaking these rules leads to the usual yellow/red card sanctions (Chapter 13).

  • Open and unload before turning around
  • Fired or unfired cartridges
  • Empties into the bin if provided
  • Semi-auto users pick up their cases
  • Sanction: yellow / red card
More:Safety
8.7PDF, p. 14 ↗

Gun open during presentation and interruptions

While targets are being shown and whenever shooting is stopped, shooters must open and unload their guns. They close them again only when the referee allows it.

  • Open and unload during target presentation
  • Same during any interruption
  • Close only with referee's permission
More:Safety

After a misfire: do not open the gun

If the shooter does not fire, or the gun or a cartridge fails, the shooter stays on the spot, gun still pointing down the range, and neither opens it nor touches the safety until the referee has looked at it. Doing either before the referee's check makes the target or targets zero.

  • Stay on the spot, gun pointing down the range
  • Do not open the gun or touch the safety
  • Wait for the referee's check
  • Otherwise the target(s) are zero
More:Malfunction
8.9PDF, p. 15 ↗

Hearing protection is compulsory

Everyone on or near a layout must wear hearing protection: shooters, referees, staff and spectators. A shooter on the stand without it is treated as absent, and the late/absent rule of Article 16.7 applies. Children on the range outside the clubhouse must always wear hearing protection.

  • Compulsory for shooters, referees, staff, public
  • Shooter without it = absent (Article 16.7)
  • Children outside the clubhouse must wear it
More:Safety
8.10PDF, p. 15 ↗

Safety glasses are compulsory

Safety glasses must be worn, with no exceptions, by shooters, referees, staff and anyone close to a stand. A shooter on the stand without them is treated as absent and Article 16.7 applies.

  • Compulsory, no exceptions
  • Shooters, referees, staff, anyone near a stand
  • Shooter without them = absent (16.7)
More:Safety
8.11PDF, p. 15 ↗

No coaching during competition

Coaching a competitor during the competition is not allowed. If the referee judges that coaching is happening, the competitor gets a yellow card, and repeat offences of the same kind lead to the penalties of Chapter 13. The person trying to coach is sent out of the shooting area by the referee.

  • Coaching during competition forbidden
  • Competitor: yellow card
  • Repeat: Chapter 13 penalties
  • The coach is removed from the shooting area

Common questions on this chapter

Can I mount the gun and track the targets during the presentation?

No. Firing or mock firing during the presentation is forbidden and is penalised under Articles 12.1 and 12.2. Guns must be open and unloaded while targets are shown.

My gun clicked on the first shot. What should I do?

Stay in place, keep the gun pointing down the range, do not open it or touch the safety, and wait for the referee to check it. If it is your first gun or ammunition incident in the round, it is a No Bird with a warning and the target or double is repeated.

I opened my gun after a misfire before the referee looked at it. What happens?

The target or targets are scored zero.

How many cartridges can I put in a semi-automatic?

A maximum of two. Load only on the stand, gun pointing down the range, after the referee allows shooting to start.

Are ear protection and glasses really compulsory?

Yes, for shooters, referees, staff and anyone near a stand (hearing protection also for spectators). A shooter on the stand without either is treated as absent and Article 16.7 applies.

Can my coach or interpreter give me tips while I shoot?

No coaching is allowed during the competition. If the referee sees coaching, you get a yellow card and repeats lead to Chapter 13 penalties; the person coaching is removed from the shooting area. An interpreter should limit themselves to translating, not advising.

Can I practise my swing before calling?

No. Mounting and swinging along the target line before calling counts as mock shooting. The first time is a warning; after that a repeat is scored zero on the next target hit.

Do I have to pick up my empty cases?

Drop them in the bin if the stand has one. With a semi-automatic you must collect your ejected cases after leaving the stand and bin them. Non-compliance brings yellow/red card sanctions.

Can the referee stop shooting because of a thunderstorm?

Yes, in exceptional circumstances for a sudden heavy shower or violent storm expected to be short. If it will last longer, the jury must be informed. Guns are opened and unloaded during the interruption.
