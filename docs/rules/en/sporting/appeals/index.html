Appeals

Chapter 9 of the FITASC Sporting™ rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

9.1 · 9.2PDF, p. 15 ↗

Break targets where the referee can see

It is the shooter's responsibility to break targets in places the referee can see. If the referee rules that a target was shot behind a natural obstacle or beyond a shooting limit, that decision cannot be appealed.

  • Onus on the shooter to break targets visibly
  • Shot behind an obstacle or beyond a limit: referee's call
  • No appeal against that ruling
Terms: Shooting limit Appeal Referee
9.3 · 9.4PDF, p. 15 ↗

No checking targets; calling the next accepts the ruling

A shooter may not walk off the stand, or go and pick up a target, to find out whether it was hit. Calling for the next target counts as agreeing with the referee about the previous one, so a disagreement has to be raised before that call.

  • No leaving the stand, no picking up targets
  • Next call = agreeing with the previous ruling
  • Disagree before calling again
Terms: Appeal Referee
9.5.1PDF, p. 15 ↗

How to protest on the stand

A shooter who disagrees must protest immediately on the stand by raising a hand and saying 'appeal' or 'protest'. The referee stops shooting and the shooter explains why. If the referee is sure the ruling is right, it is confirmed at once and becomes final without recourse (Articles 10.1 and 10.2 then apply).

  • Immediately, on the stand
  • Raise a hand and say 'appeal' or 'protest'
  • Referee stops shooting; shooter gives the reason
  • Referee sure: decision confirmed and final
Terms: Appeal Referee
9.5.2 · 9.5.3PDF, p. 16 ↗

Referee in doubt: consultation

If the referee has doubts, he or she may ask, purely for advice, another referee present, the trapper or the shooters of the squad concerned. The referee does not have to follow their opinions. After this consultation the referee's ruling is final and binding on the shooter.

  • Consult: another referee, the trapper, the squad's shooters
  • Advisory only
  • Referee not bound by opinions
  • Then the decision is final
Terms: Referee Trapper Squad Appeal
9.6 · 9.7PDF, p. 16 ↗

What can go to the jury and the 40% deposit

Decisions on whether a trajectory is correct, on No Birds and on whether a target is 'one' or 'zero' cannot be taken to the jury. Any other referee decision can be appealed to the jury in writing, together with a deposit equal to 40% of the entry fee.

  • Not appealable to jury: trajectory, No Bird, one/zero
  • Other referee decisions: appeal to the jury
  • In writing
  • Deposit: 40% of the entry fee
Terms: Appeal Jury Deposit
9.8PDF, p. 16 ↗

Outcome of a jury appeal

If the jury agrees with the shooter, the deposit is refunded; if not, it goes to a charity chosen by the FITASC Chairman. When the jury finds a protest justified (outside the non-appealable cases of 9.6), it may tell the referee to change the ruling, appoint a new referee or amend the ruling itself.

  • Upheld: deposit returned
  • Rejected: deposit to a charity chosen by the FITASC Chairman
  • Jury may order a change, appoint a new referee, or amend the ruling
Terms: Jury Deposit Appeal

Common questions on this chapter

Can other shooters in my squad say whether my target was hit?

No. The referee decides alone and squad members may not intervene. Only the referee, if in doubt, may ask them for advice.

How do I protest a referee's call on the stand?

Immediately, before calling the next target: raise your hand and say 'appeal' or 'protest', then give your reason. The referee either confirms at once or consults another referee, the trapper or the squad, then gives a final decision.

Can I appeal a hit/miss decision to the jury?

No. Decisions on 'one' or 'zero', on No Bird and on whether a trajectory is correct cannot go to the jury. Also no appeal if the referee rules a target was shot behind an obstacle or beyond a limit.

How much does a written protest to the jury cost?

You pay a deposit of 40% of the entry fee. It is refunded if the jury agrees with you; otherwise it goes to a charity chosen by the FITASC Chairman.
