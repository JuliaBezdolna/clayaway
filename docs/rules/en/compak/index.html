Part I · Chapter 1General information

What Compak® Sporting is

Chapter 1, General Information · PDF, p. 5

Compak Sporting is a clay target discipline governed by FITASC. The name and the rules are a trade mark registered and protected by FITASC; the cover page adds that the discipline was created in 1989 and adopted by FITASC in 1991. This guide follows the edition dated 01/01/2025, approved by the FITASC General Assembly of 18 July 2024.

  • Discipline and name belong to FITASC (registered trade mark).
  • Created in 1989, adopted by FITASC in 1991.
  • Edition used here: 01/01/2025 (approved 18 July 2024).
  • The rulebook has four parts: definition, organisation, running and refereeing of competitions.
Terms: Compak® Sporting · FITASC

Part I · Chapter 2Shooting installations

The target fly-over rectangle

Chapter 2, 2.1 Terrain · PDF, p. 5

Every Compak has a compulsory rectangular zone over which the targets must fly. It is 35 m to 40 m wide and 25 m deep. Its corners are marked with four stakes about 50 cm high carrying a flag or another visible marker, which helps when setting trajectories and when checking them. Going clockwise from the left, the sides are named AB, BC, CD and DA; DA is the side facing the shooters.

  • Width: minimum 35 m, maximum 40 m.
  • Depth: 25 m.
  • Four corner stakes about 50 cm high with a flag or marker.
  • Sides clockwise from the left: AB, BC, CD, DA.
Terms: Fly-over zone (ABCD rectangle) · Compak® Sporting

Five shooting positions: size and spacing

Chapter 2, 2.2 Shooting positions · PDF, p. 6

A Compak has five shooting positions, each a 1 m square. They stand in a line with 2 to 5 m between the centres of neighbouring positions. The line along the front of the positions must be parallel to side AD of the rectangle and 4 to 8 m behind it, and station No. 3 must be centred on side AD.

  • 5 positions, each 1 m x 1 m.
  • 2 to 5 m between centres.
  • Front line 4 to 8 m behind side AD and parallel to it.
  • Station No. 3 centred on AD.
Terms: Shooting stand

Firing Angle Limiters (F.A.L.)

Chapter 2, 2.2 Shooting positions (F.A.L.) · PDF, p. 6

For everyone's safety each shooting position has a Firing Angle Limiter (F.A.L.). It must limit how far the gun can swing to the sides and how high or low it can point, and it must keep the shooter from leaning or stepping forward out of the position. The model the rulebook advises is a frame of coloured 50 mm PVC tubes joined with elbows; the bottom part is weighed down with water or sand, or anchored to the ground.

  • Required at every position.
  • Limits the side angle and the vertical angle.
  • Keeps the shooter's body inside the position.
  • Advised model: coloured 50 mm PVC tubes, base weighed down with water/sand or anchored.
Terms: Firing Angle Limiter (FAL) · Shooting stand · Safety

Releasing targets: manual, remote, sonopull

Chapter 2, 2.3 Triggering systems · PDF, p. 7

Traps may be released by hand, by remote control or by a voice-activated sonopull system. With manual release the target must leave immediately after the call, within 0 to 3 seconds. A sonopull box must apply a fixed delay of 0.5 seconds that cannot be changed. It is recommended that all machines on one Compak have the same delay.

  • Manual release: 0 to 3 seconds after the call.
  • Sonopull: fixed 0.5 second delay, not adjustable.
  • Same delay on all machines of one Compak is recommended.
  • A target later than 3 seconds after the call is a NO BIRD (20.3.2).
Terms: Sonopull · Trap · Call

Equipment for Compak in line

Chapter 2, 2.3 Triggering systems (Compak in line) · PDF, p. 7

For Compak shot in line, international competitions use only sonopull release together with electronic refereeing and score management systems approved by FITASC. Manual release is therefore not used for in-line international events.

  • In line, international level: sonopull only.
  • Electronic refereeing and scoring must be FITASC-approved.
Terms: Compak® in line · Sonopull · Electronic refereeing

Six traps, A to F

Chapter 2, 2.4 Traps · PDF, p. 7

Every Compak has six traps; manual, semi-automatic and automatic machines are all allowed. Looking from left to right they carry the letters A to F, and a sign with the letter stands at each trap so everyone knows which is which. The layout also has 5 shooting stations.

  • 6 traps on each Compak.
  • Letters A-F from left to right, a sign at each trap.
  • Manual, semi-automatic or automatic machines.
  • 5 shooting stations.
Terms: Trap · Compak® Sporting

Where traps may be placed; tower

Chapter 2, 2.4 Traps (placement) · PDF, p. 7

Traps may be placed freely as long as the targets, even a NO BIRD, cannot endanger shooters, the referee, staff or spectators. A trap placed behind the shooting positions must be raised at least 4 m above the level of the positions (a tower). In still air every target must pass over the ABCD rectangle at some point of its flight.

  • Placement is free, but safety comes first, including for broken or stray targets.
  • Trap behind the stations: at least 4 m higher than the stations (tower).
  • In calm wind every target must fly over the ABCD rectangle.
Terms: Trap · Tower · Fly-over zone (ABCD rectangle) · Safety

Part I · Chapter 3Targets and trajectories

Varied trajectories, safe from all five stations

Chapter 3, 3.1 Trajectories · PDF, p. 7

Trajectories should be as varied as possible: rising, dropping, going away, incoming, lofted, rabbits and so on. Every single target must allow two shots from each of the five stations, safely for shooters, referees, staff and spectators. Compak trajectories are short so that they do not interfere with the neighbouring Compak.

  • Mix of rising, falling, receding, approaching, lofted and rabbit targets.
  • Each single must be shootable twice from all 5 stations.
  • Trajectories are short to avoid the adjacent Compak.
Terms: Trajectory · Rabbit

Three compulsory trajectories

Chapter 3, 3.1.1 Compulsory trajectories · PDF, p. 7

Every Compak must include three compulsory trajectories: one from left to right crossing sides AB and CD, one from right to left crossing sides CD and AB, and one going away that crosses side BC. The going-away target may come from a trap sunk into the ground or standing on it in front of the stations, or from a raised trap (tower) behind them.

  • Left to right: crosses AB and CD.
  • Right to left: crosses CD and AB.
  • Receding: crosses BC.
  • Receding trap: sunk into or on the ground in front, or tower behind.
Terms: Trajectory · Tower

Free trajectories

Chapter 3, 3.1.2 Free trajectories · PDF, p. 8

Apart from the three compulsory ones, the other trajectories are chosen by the organiser according to preference and how the ground works. They must still be short so as not to impinge on the next Compak, and safe from every station.

  • Chosen by the organiser.
  • Must stay short and must not reach the adjacent Compak.
Terms: Trajectory · Organiser

Target types and colour

Chapter 3, 3.2 Targets · PDF, p. 8

Any target type may be used, standard or special: battue, 60 mm, 90 mm, rabbit, rocket and others. The colour should stand out well against the local background. Two-coloured targets are not advised, and battue targets are not advised on very short trajectories.

  • All standard and special targets allowed.
  • Colour chosen to stand out well.
  • Bicolour targets not advised.
  • Battue not advised on very short trajectories.
Terms: Target · Battue · Rabbit · Midi · Mini / super mini

Double on report

Chapter 3, 3.3.1 Double on report · PDF, p. 8

In a double on report two targets come from one trap or from two different traps. The shooter's call releases the first; the shot at the first target releases the second, within the time allowed by article 2.3.

  • Call releases target 1; the shot releases target 2.
  • One or two traps.
  • Release time as in 2.3.
Terms: Double on report · Double

Simultaneous double

Chapter 3, 3.3.2 Simultaneous double · PDF, p. 8

A simultaneous double means two targets released together on the shooter's call from two different traps. The shooter may take them in either order.

  • Both targets on one call.
  • Always from two different traps.
  • Order of shooting is free.
Terms: Simultaneous double · Double

Doubles: one cartridge per target, no doubling the first

Chapter 3, 3.3.3 One cartridge per target in doubles · PDF, p. 8

In any double the shooter may fire only one cartridge at each target; firing both shots at the first target is prohibited. The referees' quick guide adds that deliberately firing both shots at the same target in any type of double is scored ZERO-ZERO.

  • One cartridge per target.
  • Doubling the first target is prohibited.
  • Quick guide: both shots deliberately at one target = ZERO-ZERO.
Terms: Double · Zero

Double on report: both targets broken with one shot

Chapter 3, 3.3.3 (double on report: two targets with one shot) · PDF, p. 8

If both targets of a double on report break with a single cartridge, the result on the first target stands and the second target is a NO BIRD, so the double is shot again. The lines about a second and third occurrence (the third being ZERO-ZERO) follow the simultaneous-double paragraph, and the text does not say clearly whether they also cover doubles on report; ask the referee or jury if it matters.

  • Result on target 1 is recorded.
  • Target 2 is NO BIRD.
  • The double is repeated.
Terms: Double on report · No Bird

Simultaneous double: both targets broken with one shot

Chapter 3, 3.3.3 (simultaneous double: two targets with one shot) · PDF, p. 8

If one cartridge breaks both targets of a simultaneous double, the double is a NO BIRD: nothing is scored and it is shot again. The same happens the second time. The third time, the double is scored ZERO-ZERO. The rulebook does not say over what period these times are counted (per double, round or competition).

  • 1st time: NO BIRD, nothing scored, shot again.
  • 2nd time: the same.
  • 3rd time: ZERO-ZERO.
  • Counting period: not stated in the rulebook.
Terms: Simultaneous double · No Bird · Zero

Part II · Chapter 4Jury

Who sits on the jury

Chapter 4, 4.1 Constitution of the Jury · PDF, p. 10

An international event is run by a jury with three groups of members: the Technical Commission members who are present, one representative from every country that enters a men's national team, and the FITASC Executive Committee members who are present (but not those already on the appeal jury). The chair is the President or a representative of the federation organising the event.

  • Technical Commission members who are present.
  • One representative per country with a men's national team.
  • FITASC Executive Committee members who are present, except appeal-jury members.
  • Chair: the President or the organising federation's representative.
Terms: Jury · Technical Commission

Jury badges, presence and observed irregularities

Chapter 4, 4.1 Constitution of the Jury (badges, presence) · PDF, p. 10

Every jury member wears a badge so that everyone can identify them, and at least two jury members must be present at the event at all times. A jury member who sees an irregularity must not step in with the referee directly; they report it to the jury, which decides what to do.

  • Badge for every jury member.
  • At least 2 jury members always present.
  • No direct intervention with the referee: report to the jury.
Terms: Jury · Referee

What the jury does

Chapter 4, 4.2 Role of the jury · PDF, p. 10

Together with the competition secretariat, the jury sets the shooting times and supervises the draw for the shooting order. It makes sure the rules are applied, checks guns, ammunition and targets through technical tests, settles technical failures the referee has not decided, and decides sanctions against shooters who break the rules or behave unsportingly. If a member sees a breach of the rules, they alert the jury, which must act at once.

  • Sets shooting times and supervises the draw.
  • Checks guns, ammunition and targets.
  • Handles complaints and technical failures.
  • Decides sanctions.
Terms: Jury · Penalty

What you cannot complain about

Chapter 4, 4.2 Role of the jury, point 3 (complaints) · PDF, p. 10

A complaint can never concern whether a target was hit or missed, whether a target was defective, or whether it left the planned trajectory: the referee's decision on these points cannot be appealed. All other referee decisions may be contested before the jury.

  • Hit or miss: no appeal.
  • Defective target or off-trajectory target: no appeal.
  • Other referee decisions may be contested.
Terms: Appeal · Jury · Referee

How to lodge a complaint and the deposit

Chapter 4, 4.2 Role of the jury, point 3 (complaint procedure) · PDF, p. 10

A complaint is made in writing to the jury together with a deposit of the amount in force on the day of the competition. It must concern an incident of the same day, never earlier days. If the jury accepts the complaint, the deposit is refunded; if the complaint is justified, the jury may instruct the referee for future decisions or appoint another referee.

  • In writing, with a deposit.
  • Same-day incidents only.
  • Accepted complaint: deposit refunded.
  • Justified complaint: referee instructed or replaced.
Terms: Appeal · Jury · Deposit

Emergency decisions, quorum and voting

Chapter 4, 4.2 Role of the jury, points 6-7 · PDF, p. 10

In an emergency, for example when the shoot risks stopping, two jury members named by the President may take an exceptional decision with the Head Referee's agreement, subject to later ratification by the jury. The jury can only deliberate validly with its President or the President's representative and a quarter of its members. Decisions are taken by a majority of members present; in a tie the President has the casting vote.

  • Emergency: 2 members + Head Referee's agreement, ratified later.
  • Quorum: President (or representative) plus a quarter of members.
  • Majority vote; President decides a tie.
Terms: Jury · Chief referee

Jury of appeal and disciplinary committee

Chapter 4, 4.3 Jury of appeal · PDF, p. 11

At every international competition an appeal jury is set up at the same time as the jury. It consists of the FITASC President, the Chairman of the Technical Committee and the Chairman of the organising federation, or their representatives. No jury member may sit on it. It can be called on when shooters or FITASC contest a decision of the jury. Disciplinary matters go to the FITASC disciplinary committee.

  • Three members: FITASC President, Technical Committee Chairman, organising federation Chairman (or representatives).
  • No overlap with the jury.
  • Hears challenges to jury decisions.
  • Discipline: FITASC disciplinary committee.
Terms: Appeal jury · Jury

Part II · Chapter 5Layouts

The eight Compaks of an international competition

Chapter 5, Layouts · PDF, p. 11

An international competition is shot on four kinds of Compak: three with 3 singles and a double on report per station, one with 3 singles and a simultaneous double, three with 1 single and two doubles on report, and one with 1 single and two simultaneous doubles. That adds up to eight Compaks of 25 targets, i.e. 200 targets (our calculation; the rulebook lists only the four kinds).

  • 3 x (3 singles + 1 double on report).
  • 1 x (3 singles + 1 simultaneous double).
  • 3 x (1 single + 2 doubles on report).
  • 1 x (1 single + 2 simultaneous doubles).
  • 8 Compaks x 25 = 200 targets.
Terms: Layout · Target menu · Double on report · Simultaneous double

Who sets and checks the layouts

Chapter 5, Layouts (setting the layouts) · PDF, p. 11

The layouts are settled before the competition starts, with the person in charge of the ground present. There are two ways: the jury or a person it names picks ready-made trajectory setting tables (Annex 3), or the person in charge of the layouts draws them up. Either way, the FITASC Technical Committee members who are present check the result and may change it so the trajectories are safe, varied and balanced. If none of them is present, the jury does this check.

  • Settled before the start, with the ground manager present.
  • Way 1: ready-made trajectory setting tables (Annex 3).
  • Way 2: layouts drawn up by the person in charge.
  • Checked by the Technical Committee members present, otherwise by the jury.
Terms: Layout · Trajectory setting table · Technical Commission · Jury

When layouts may be changed

Chapter 5, Layouts (changes between rounds) · PDF, p. 11

Trajectories, traps, menus, and the distances between stations or between the stations and the theoretical line may be changed only after a complete round, once all shooters have shot the same round of targets. A complete round means 50 targets with 2 installations or 100 targets with 4 installations.

  • Changes only after a complete round shot by everyone.
  • Complete round: 50 targets (2 installations) or 100 targets (4 installations).
  • Covers trajectories, traps, menus and station distances.
Terms: Layout · Target menu · Round

Championships: 48 new machines

Chapter 5, Layouts (continental and world championships) · PDF, p. 11

Continental and world Compak Sporting championships must be fair to everyone, so the host club may not use the machines it normally shoots on. Instead, 48 new machines are required, set up and adjusted for that championship alone.

  • The host club's everyday machines may not be used.
  • 48 new machines are required.
  • Set up and adjusted for that championship alone.
Terms: Trap · Layout

Part II · Chapter 6Practice

Practice on competition trajectories

Chapter 6, Practice · PDF, p. 12

Shooters may practise on the trajectories that will be used in the competition, but these may still be modified when the Commission members inspect them or when the trajectory setting tables are put in place. The trajectory setting tables used for practice must differ from those of the competition.

  • Practice may use competition trajectories.
  • Trajectories may still be modified afterwards.
  • Practice tables must differ from competition tables.
Terms: Practice · Trajectory setting table

Championships: official practice from the Saturday before

Chapter 6, Practice (championships) · PDF, p. 12

At continental and world championships, official practice opens on the Saturday before the championship. Before that day nobody may train on the championship machines and trajectories.

  • Official practice opens on the preceding Saturday.
  • No training on championship machines before that.
Terms: Practice

Part II · Chapter 7Dress code

What to wear at the shooting position

Chapter 7, 7.1 Dress code · PDF, p. 12

Shooters must come to the stand dressed suitably and practically for a public event. Short shorts are banned; only long shorts such as Bermudas ending no more than 5 cm above the knee are allowed. Tops need at least short sleeves and must reach the neck (a T-shirt is fine, collar optional), and being bare-chested under the shooting vest is not allowed. Sandals are forbidden in practice and in competition for safety reasons.

  • No short shorts; Bermudas no higher than 5 cm above the knee.
  • At least short sleeves, up to the neck.
  • No bare chest under the vest.
  • No sandals, in practice or competition.
Terms: Dress code

Dress for opening and closing ceremonies

Chapter 7, 7.1 Dress code (ceremonies) · PDF, p. 12

Ceremonies have their own dress rule. When national teams parade at the opening, their members wear the team uniform or smart trousers and a blazer. At the closing, every prize winner comes to the prize giving dressed the same way: team uniform, or smart trousers and a blazer.

  • Opening parade: team uniform, or smart trousers + blazer.
  • Prize giving: winners in team uniform, or smart trousers + blazer.
Terms: Dress code

Competition number on the back

Chapter 7, 7.2 Competition numbers · PDF, p. 12

The competition number must be fixed on the shooter's back, between the shoulders and the waist, and be fully visible. A first breach brings a WARNING from the referee. If the shooter does not correct it, further sanctions follow, up to exclusion from the competition decided by the jury.

  • On the back, between shoulders and waist, fully visible.
  • First breach: WARNING.
  • Not corrected: further sanctions up to exclusion (jury).
Terms: Competitor number · Warning · Jury

Part II · Chapter 8Shooting times

Shooting schedule: the shooter's responsibility

Chapter 8, Shooting Times · PDF, p. 12

Shooting times are published on notices and/or in a printed schedule, and keeping to them is the shooter's responsibility. Shooters should be behind their Compak early enough to report to the referee and confirm their presence (compulsory), avoid being late, be ready to shoot without delay and look at the layouts. During the event they must follow any delays or advances in the timetable.

  • Times on notices and/or leaflets.
  • Compulsory: report to the referee and confirm presence.
  • Be ready to shoot without delay.
  • Follow schedule changes during the day.
Terms: Shooting times · Referee

Part II · Chapter 9Lateness of a shooter

Be ready with a full round of equipment and ammunition

Chapter 9, Lateness of a Shooter · PDF, p. 13

When their turn comes, the shooter must be ready to shoot at once and carry the equipment and ammunition needed for a whole round. The referee is not responsible for looking for or calling a missing shooter.

  • Ready immediately at your turn.
  • Bring ammunition and equipment for a full round.
  • The referee does not look for missing shooters.
Terms: Round · Referee

Late in squad shooting: 25 zeros

Chapter 9, Lateness of a Shooter (squad) · PDF, p. 13

In squad shooting, if a shooter arrives after the first shooter of the squad has called for the first target, the late shooter is scored 25 zeros for that round.

  • Deadline: first call of the first shooter in the squad.
  • Penalty: 25 zeros.
  • Appeal possible (see procedure).
Terms: Squad · Zero

Late in Compak in line: 25 zeros

Chapter 9, Lateness of a Shooter (in line) · PDF, p. 13

In Compak in line a shooter counts as late once two things have happened: their competition number is already cancelled in the computer system, and the next shooter is standing on station 1. The late shooter is then scored 25 zeros.

  • Number already cancelled in the system.
  • Next shooter already on station 1.
  • Result: 25 zeros.
Terms: Compak® in line · Zero

Appealing a lateness penalty

Chapter 9, Lateness of a Shooter (appeal to the jury) · PDF, p. 13

A shooter who believes the lateness had a valid reason must appeal to the jury in writing on the same day, with the appeal fee, and accept the jury's decision. If the reason is accepted, the jury may let them shoot the round with another group without penalty and the fee is returned. If not, the 25 zeros stand and the fee is kept.

  • Written appeal, same day, with the fee.
  • Valid reason: shoot with another group, fee refunded.
  • Not valid: 25 zeros, fee kept.
Terms: Appeal · Jury · Deposit

Part II · Chapter 10Compak in line / Absence of a shooter

Absent on day one

Chapter 10, Compak in line / Absence of a shooter · PDF, p. 13

Missing the first day of the competition has a cost: from day two on, the shooter's name is taken off the list. A shooter who appears on day two or later and still wants to shoot has to appeal to the jury, following the procedure in chapter 9.

  • Missed day 1: off the list from day 2.
  • Wants to shoot later: appeal to the jury as in chapter 9.
Terms: Compak® in line · Jury

Part II · Chapter 11Testing guns

Where to test your gun

Chapter 11, Testing Guns · PDF, p. 13

Before taking part, a shooter may test that the gun works on a stand set up specially for this, near the gun room. Guns may never be test-fired on the competition stand before the round starts.

  • Dedicated test stand near the gun room.
  • No test shots on the competition stand before the round.
Terms: Gun test

Part II · Chapter 12Borrowing a shotgun

Borrowing a gun after a breakdown

Chapter 12, Borrowing a Shotgun · PDF, p. 13

Only when a gun breaks down may a shooter, as an exception, borrow the gun of another shooter who is not in their group or squad to finish the round. Both the owner and the referee must agree.

  • Only for a malfunctioning gun.
  • Lender must not be in the same group/squad.
  • Owner's and referee's agreement needed.
  • See 21.4 for the 2-minute repair limit.
Terms: Malfunction · Referee

Part II · Chapter 13The use of a shotgun by several shooters

No sharing a gun within a group

Chapter 13, The Use of a Shotgun by Several Shooters · PDF, p. 14

Several shooters of the same group may not use one and the same shotgun.

  • One gun cannot be shared within a group or squad.
  • Exceptional borrowing is only from outside the group (chapter 12).
Terms: Squad

Part II · Chapter 14Shoot-offs

Where shoot-offs are shot

Chapter 14, Shoot-offs (stands and layout) · PDF, p. 14

The Technical Commission members present choose the Compak and the layouts for shoot-offs; if none is present, the jury does. Shoot-offs are shot on a Compak with one single and two simultaneous doubles per station.

  • Chosen by the Technical Commission, otherwise the jury.
  • Format: 1 single + 2 simultaneous doubles per station.
Terms: Shoot-off · Technical Commission · Simultaneous double

Shoot-offs for the top three: 25 targets, then sudden death

Chapter 14, Shoot-offs (first three places) · PDF, p. 14

A tie for a top-three place, in the open ranking or in a category, is settled by shooting 25 more targets. If the shooters are still level, they go on to sudden death: the first zero is out, and everyone shoots the same number of targets from the same stand (Annex 5). If shooters in an open-ranking shoot-off are in the same category, its result also decides their category places. Free places in a shoot-off squad stay empty.

  • Top 3 (open and categories): 25 more targets.
  • Still level: sudden death, the first zero is out.
  • Same number of targets for all, same stand.
  • Open shoot-off also settles a shared category.
  • Free squad places stay empty.
Terms: Shoot-off · Sudden death · Category

Staying available for a shoot-off

Chapter 14, Shoot-offs (availability) · PDF, p. 14

If no time is fixed in advance for the shoot-off, the shooters concerned must stay in contact with the jury and be ready to shoot less than 15 minutes after being called. A shooter absent when the shoot-off starts is treated as having withdrawn. For exceptional reasons the jury may postpone the shoot-off to the next day; absentees are then also considered withdrawn.

  • Ready in less than 15 minutes after the call.
  • Absent = withdrawn.
  • Jury may postpone to the next day.
Terms: Shoot-off · Jury

Ties below third place: countback

Chapter 14, Shoot-offs (ranking below third place) · PDF, p. 14

Below third place in the individual ranking there is no shoot-off: tied shooters are ranked equal and listed by their scores counted back from the 8th Compak to the 1st.

  • No shoot-off below 3rd place.
  • Ranked equal, listed by countback from Compak 8 to Compak 1.
Terms: Countback · Shoot-off

Ties between national teams

Chapter 14, Shoot-offs (national teams) · PDF, p. 14

Tied national teams are separated by the combined score of their members on the 8th Compak; if still level, on the 7th, then the 6th and so on back to the 1st.

  • Team totals on Compak 8 first.
  • Then Compak 7, 6, ... 1.
Terms: Countback · Team

Sudden death with two shooters

Annex 5, Running a sudden death shoot off (2 shooters) · PDF, p. 40

The sudden-death Compak uses a table of one single and two simultaneous doubles. After a draw, shooter 1 and then shooter 2 shoot the single on station 1. If equal, shooter 2 shoots the first double first, then shooter 1; if still equal, shooter 1 shoots the second double, then shooter 2. They then move to station 2, where the starting order keeps alternating, and so on until the first eliminating zero with an equal number of targets shot.

  • Order fixed by drawing lots.
  • Single, then double 1, then double 2 at each station.
  • The starting shooter alternates at each step.
  • Ends at the first eliminating zero after equal targets.
Terms: Sudden death · Shoot-off · Simultaneous double

Sudden death with three shooters

Annex 5, Running a sudden death shoot off (3 shooters) · PDF, p. 40

With three shooters the same scheme applies after a draw: all three shoot the station 1 single in order 1-2-3, the first double in order 2-3-1, the second double in order 3-1-2, then on station 2 the single in order 2-3-1, and so on in rotation. It continues until the first eliminating zero, always with an equal number of targets shot.

  • Station 1: single 1-2-3, double 1: 2-3-1, double 2: 3-1-2.
  • Station 2 single starts with shooter 2 (2-3-1).
  • Rotation continues until the first eliminating zero.
Terms: Sudden death · Shoot-off

Part II · Chapter 15Score sheet

Who keeps the score sheet

Chapter 15, Score Sheet · PDF, p. 14

In squad shooting the referee keeps the score sheet (example in Annex 1). In line with manual scoring the referee also keeps it (example in Annex 2). In line with an electronic system the results appear on a screen.

  • Squad: referee keeps the sheet (Annex 1).
  • In line manual: referee keeps the sheet (Annex 2).
  • In line electronic: results on screen.
Terms: Score sheet · Referee · Compak® in line

Announcing zeros; marks O and X

Chapter 15, Score Sheet (announcing zeros, marks) · PDF, p. 14

Squad and group

Chapter 16, Glossary (Squad, Group) · PDF, p. 15

The rulebook's glossary uses two different words. A squad (Compak by squad) means up to 6 shooters who shoot one round together, at the same time, on one installation. A group (Compak in line) is worked out by dividing the number of competitors by the number of Compaks; each shooter in a group begins on station 1 and ends on station 5.

  • Squad: up to 6 shooters, one round, together.
  • Group (in line): number of competitors ÷ number of Compaks.
  • In line everyone goes from station 1 to station 5.
Terms: Squad · Group (in line) · Compak® in line

Range means all the sporting installations; a Compak Sporting is the installation for this discipline. A round is 25 targets shot on the same Compak. A trap is the machine that throws targets, and sonopull is the acoustic device that releases them at the sound of the shooter's voice. A shot is the firing of one cartridge, a target is a clay pigeon, and a trajectory is the path of a target through the air.

  • Round = 25 targets on one Compak.
  • Trap = throwing machine; sonopull = voice release.
  • Shot = one cartridge fired.
  • Trajectory = flight path of a target.

A semi-automatic shotgun must have a device that stops its ejected cases from bothering the shooter next to it, and it may be loaded with no more than two cartridges.

The referee keeps order and good conduct at the stand, judges the result of every shot and signals every zero with a sound signal. He takes decisions alone. When a target is a hit (ONE)

Chapter 20, 20.1 The target is declared "ONE" · PDF, p. 23

Three things make a target ONE: it was thrown properly, the shot followed the rules, and the target visibly lost at least one piece or was turned to dust, fully or partly. Flash targets are judged the same way.

  • Proper target, shot within the rules.
  • At least one visible piece, or fully/partly turned to dust.
  • Flash targets: same rule.

When a target is a miss (ZERO)

Chapter 20, 20.2 The target is declared "ZERO" · PDF, p. 23

A target is ZERO when it is not hit: no visible piece comes off, or only dust appears (a 'smoking' or dusty target).

  • No visible piece = ZERO.
  • Dust only (smoking target) = ZERO.

Gun or cartridge failures: first and second incident

Chapter 20, 20.3.1 Due to guns or ammunition · PDF, p. 23

Failures are counted within one round of 25 targets on a Compak. The first time a gun or cartridge fails in that round, the NO BIRD table is used and the shooter gets a first warning (yellow card). If the same kind of failure happens again in that round, every target not yet shot at is scored ZERO (red card).

  • Counted within one round of 25 targets.
  • First failure: NO BIRD table + yellow card.
  • Same kind of failure again: remaining targets = ZERO (red card).

Both barrels fire together or the first cartridge fails

Chapter 20, 20.3.1 Due to guns or ammunition (both shots at once / first cartridge fails) · PDF, p. 23

On a first incident where both barrels go off together, or the first cartridge fails, the shooter simply gets the target or double again. Single: a new target. First target of a double on report: a new double. Simultaneous double: a new double. The failed attempt does not count for anything.

  • Single: NO BIRD, new target.
  • First target of a report double: NO BIRD, new double.
  • Simultaneous double: NO BIRD, new double.
  • First warning (yellow card).

The second cartridge fails

Chapter 20, 20.3.1 Due to guns or ammunition (second cartridge fails) · PDF, p. 23

When the second cartridge fails on a first incident, the outcome depends on the target. Single: a new target is thrown, and only the second shot may break it, so the shooter has to miss on purpose with the first. Double on report: the first target's result stands and the double is thrown again to score the second. Simultaneous double: a new double is thrown. The quick guide adds that on a second occurrence with a simultaneous double the first result is kept and the second target is zero.

  • Single: new target, only the second shot may break it.
  • Report double: first result kept, the double again.
  • Simultaneous: a new double (first incident).
  • Second occurrence on a simultaneous double: first result kept, second = ZERO (quick guide).

  • Broken on release, wrong machine, wrong colour.
  • Off trajectory (referee's judgement).
  • More than 3 seconds after the call, or not called.
  • Shooter visibly disturbed, or referee cannot judge.

What happens after a target NO BIRD

What follows a target NO BIRD depends on where it happened. Single: a new target is thrown. First target of a double on report: the whole double is thrown again. Second target of a report double irregular, or the first target (or a piece of it) breaks the second before the second shot: the result of the first is kept and the double is thrown again to score the second. Simultaneous double with any irregular target: the double is thrown again.

  • Single: a new target.
  • Report double, first target: the double again, nothing scored.
  • Report double, second target (or the first breaks the second): first result kept, double again.
  • Simultaneous double: the double again.

If a rabbit is missed with the first shot and then breaks before the second shot (for example on the ground), it is a NO BIRD and the rabbit is thrown again; it can then only be scored with the second shot, so the shooter must deliberately miss with the first.

  • Missed with shot 1, breaks before shot 2: NO BIRD.
  • Rabbit thrown again.
  • Only the second shot can score it.

Chapter 20, 20.3.2 Due to targets (targets from another Compak) · PDF, p. 24

A target, or bits of one, flying over from a neighbouring Compak does not give the shooter a NO BIRD, even if they can see it. A NO BIRD for a stray target applies only when it comes from a machine of the shooter's own Compak.

  • Stray target from the own Compak: NO BIRD.

No NO BIRD for bad weather

Bad weather never justifies a NO BIRD. Every target broken counts as ONE and every target missed as ZERO, whatever the conditions.

  • No NO BIRD because of weather.
  • Broken = ONE, missed = ZERO.

Malfunction: inspection, 2 minutes, replacement gun

Loss of self-control

Chapter 22, Attempts to influence · PDF, p. 26

The rulebook names three kinds of attempt to influence: a shooter who goes on disputing the referee's final decision, in words or by gestures, after shooting has started again; a shooter who argues about a decision for show; and shooters or spectators who openly voice their opinion of a decision, or criticise it, without the referee's permission. Any of these can bring jury sanctions, up to exclusion (chapter 23).

  • Still disputing after shooting has started again.
  • Arguing about a decision for show.
  • Shooters or spectators openly criticising a decision.

Common questions

How many targets is an international Compak competition?

It is shot on eight Compaks of 25 targets each: three with 3 singles + a double on report, one with 3 singles + a simultaneous double, three with 1 single + two doubles on report, and one with 1 single + two simultaneous doubles. That makes 200 targets (our calculation).

Can I start with the gun already mounted in Compak?

Yes. The gun position at the moment of calling is free, mounted or not. You must, however, shoot every target with the gun shouldered. Compak has no low-gun rule and no vest marker.

Do I need a marker on my vest like in Sporting?

No. The Compak rulebook has no vest marker or low-gun requirement; the gun position when you call is free.

How long do I have to call for my target?

At most 10 seconds after the target of the previous station has been shot. The first time you exceed it you get a warning noted on the score sheet; after that the targets are penalised per the 21.5 table.

How quickly must the target come out after I call?

With manual release within 0 to 3 seconds; a sonopull applies a fixed 0.5-second delay. A target released more than 3 seconds after the call is a NO BIRD.

What happens if my gun misfires?

Stay in place, gun pointed down range, and do not open it or touch the safety until the referee has checked it. The first failure in the round is a NO BIRD with a first warning (yellow card); a second failure of the same type means the targets not shot are scored zero (red card).

How many malfunctions am I allowed per round?

One. The first gun or ammunition incident in a round of 25 targets is repeated as a NO BIRD with a yellow card. A second incident of the same type is not repeated: the targets not shot count as ZERO.

My second cartridge failed on a single. What now?

On a first incident the target is thrown again and can only be scored with the second shot, so you have to miss deliberately with the first.

Both barrels fired together on a double. Is the double repeated?

Yes, on a first incident another double is thrown and nothing from the failed attempt counts. It also earns a first warning.

My gun broke in the middle of a round. How long do I have?

Two minutes to repair or replace it. With the referee's agreement you can continue with your group using another gun; otherwise the remaining targets are zero.

Can I borrow a friend's gun if mine breaks?

Yes, exceptionally and only after a malfunction, if the friend is not in your group or squad and both he and the referee agree. Sharing one gun within the same group is forbidden.

Can my partner and I share one gun in the same squad?

No. The same shotgun may not be used by several shooters in the same group.

Can I change chokes between stations?

No. Changing the gun, chokes or barrels is prohibited within a round; it is allowed between rounds.

What cartridges can I use?

Lead shot up to 28 g (+2% tolerance), spherical pellets 2 to 2.5 mm (+/-0.1 mm). Reloads, dispersers, mixed shot, black powder and tracers are banned.

Are 20-gauge guns allowed?

Yes. Any smooth-bore hunting gun of calibre 12 or smaller with a barrel of at least 66 cm (26 in) is allowed, except pump-actions and drillings.

Can I use a semi-automatic?

Yes, with a device that keeps ejected cases away from the neighbouring shooter and no more than two cartridges loaded. You must also collect your empty cases when you leave the stand.

Is a sling or a camera on the gun allowed?

No. Slings and straps are banned, and so are micro cameras mounted on the gun and any artificial aiming device.

I shoot with a release trigger. What do I need to do?

Put a sticker with a big 'R' on a fluorescent background on the outer side of the stock. On a NO BIRD before your first shot, keep the trigger held and call again, open the gun while holding it, or tell the referee and fire in the safe direction he shows. Check the programme in case the host country bans release triggers.

What if the second target is a NO BIRD and I have a release trigger?

With a release-pull system, once the first shot has been fired you can simply open the gun. With a double release trigger, follow the same procedure as for the first shot (25.1.1).

When is a target counted as hit?

When it was regular, you shot by the rules, and at least one visible piece came off or it was partly or fully pulverised. Dust alone is a ZERO.

The target just smoked. Is that a hit?

No. If only dust appears and no visible piece comes off, it is ZERO.

Can I pick up the target to show it was hit?

No. Shooters are never allowed to pick up a target to check it; only the referee judges hits and misses, and that decision is final.

How do I protest a referee's decision?

At once, on the stand and before the next shooter calls: raise your arm, say 'PROTEST' or 'APPEAL' and explain why. The referee stops the shooting and gives his final decision; he may consult others first but is not bound by them.

Can I complain to the jury that a target was a hit?

No. Complaints about hit or miss, defective targets or off-trajectory targets are not admissible; the referee's decision there is final. Other referee decisions can be contested in writing with a deposit.

How do I file a complaint with the jury?

In writing, with the deposit in force that day, and only about something that happened the same day. If the complaint is accepted the deposit is refunded. Note that if you refused to sign the score sheet, no complaint is admissible.

Do I have to sign the score sheet?

Yes, at the end of every round. If you refuse to sign, you lose the right to any complaint.

How are zeros announced and marked?

The referee announces every zero loudly or with a sound signal so you can protest immediately. On the sheet a miss is 'O' and a hit is 'X'; in electronic in-line shooting the results appear on a screen.

I arrived late and my squad had started. What happens?

If the first shooter of your squad has already called for the first target, your round is scored as 25 zeros. Had a good reason? Write to the jury on the same day and pay the appeal fee. If the jury accepts the reason, you may shoot the round with another squad or group, without a penalty.

When am I considered late in Compak in line?

Once your competition number is cancelled in the computer system and the next shooter is already on station 1. Your round is then scored 25 zeros, though you can still appeal to the jury.

I missed the first day. Can I still shoot?

You are removed from the list from day two onward. To shoot, you have to appeal to the jury following the chapter 9 procedure.

Where can I test-fire my gun before the round?

Only on the stand set up for testing near the gun room. Test shots on the competition stand before the round are never allowed.

Can I train on the championship layouts?

Practice may take place on the competition trajectories, using different trajectory setting tables. At continental and world championships official practice opens on the Saturday before; nobody may use the championship machines before that.

Are shorts and sandals allowed?

Long shorts such as Bermudas ending no more than 5 cm above the knee are allowed; short shorts are not. Sandals are forbidden in both practice and competition.

Where must my competition number be?

On your back between the shoulders and the waist, fully visible. A breach first gets a warning; if not corrected, further sanctions up to exclusion by the jury.

Are ear and eye protection compulsory?

Yes. A competitor at the station without hearing protection or without protective glasses is treated as absent and may not shoot.

What happens if I break both targets of a simultaneous double with one shot?

The double is a NO BIRD and is shot again, with nothing scored; the same happens the second time. The third time it is scored ZERO-ZERO.

Both targets of a double on report broke with my first shot. How is it scored?

The first target's result is recorded, the second is a NO BIRD, and the double is repeated.

Can I fire both barrels at the first target of a double?

No. Only one cartridge per target is allowed in a double. According to the referees' quick guide, deliberately firing both shots at one target in any double scores ZERO-ZERO.

The first target of my report double broke the second one. What now?

If the first target or its pieces break the second before your second shot, the first result stands and the double is repeated to score the second target.

The rabbit bounced and broke after I missed with my first shot. Is it a NO BIRD?

Yes. The rabbit is thrown again, and only the second shot can score it, so you must deliberately miss with the first.

A target from the next Compak flew through my shot. Do I get a NO BIRD?

No. Targets or pieces from another Compak do not give a NO BIRD. Only a target thrown by mistake from a machine of your own Compak does.

The wind threw the target around. Can I ask for a NO BIRD?

No. Bad weather never justifies a NO BIRD: broken targets count as ONE and missed targets as ZERO.

I was not ready when the target came. Can I refuse it?

Only if you did not call for it, which is a NO BIRD. Refusing a regular target you called is penalised: a single is ZERO, a report double ZERO with the double repeated for the second, and a simultaneous double ZERO-ZERO.

Something distracted me during my shot. Is that a NO BIRD?

Only if the referee judges that you were visibly disturbed or distracted. That is his decision alone.

The referee called NO BIRD but I shot anyway and broke it. Does it count?

No. Shooting at a target after a clearly announced NO BIRD is forbidden and is penalised per the 21.5 table.

What do the yellow and red cards mean in Compak?

A first breach is warned with a yellow card. A further breach on the same Compak gets a red card and the targets are scored per the 21.5 table. Reoffending can lead the jury to take away a target, a whole round, or exclude you.

Can my coach give me advice between stations?

No. Coaching during competition is not allowed. The competitor gets a yellow card, repeats bring chapter 23 sanctions, and the person coaching is removed from the shooting area.

As an interpreter, can I stand next to my shooter on the stand?

The rulebook does not mention interpreters at all. It forbids coaching during competition and removes the person coaching from the shooting area, so keep strictly to translating and agree with the referee in advance where you may stand.

I keep arguing after the referee's final decision. What can happen?

You must resume within 10 seconds of the final decision, otherwise it is a refusal to comply. Continuing to dispute or arguing ostentatiously is an attempt to influence; both can bring chapter 23 sanctions up to exclusion.

What happens after an exclusion?

Your federation and FITASC are told, and FITASC keeps the record for five years. Being excluded again within three years can mean a permanent ban. No money is paid back.

My cartridges turned out to be non-compliant. Are my scores lost?

In principle every shot fired with them before the inspection counts as zero. The jury may keep the results if you could not have known and gained no advantage, provided you fix it immediately.

How do we move between stations in a squad?

Station 1 always starts. When the shooter on station 5 has finished, he goes to wait behind station 1, everybody else moves one station to the right, and the waiting shooter takes station 1. Always move with the gun open and unloaded.

Will I see the targets before shooting?

Shooting by squad: the six singles (A to F) and the simultaneous doubles are demonstrated only once a day, in the morning, for the first squad, and again after a break of more than 10 minutes. Shooting in line: the first shooter is shown the singles (at most twice), and each later shooter is shown the first target of their doubles.

What is the difference between Compak by squad and Compak in line?

By squad, up to 6 shooters rotate through the five stations together. In line, each shooter goes from station 1 to station 5 in a continuous line, the group size being the total number of shooters divided by the number of Compaks; international in-line events use sonopull and FITASC-approved electronic scoring.

Why does each double start with the target I just shot?

It is how a Compak menu is built: a double always opens with the target that closed the station before, for example A-F, F-C, C-E, E-B, B-D.

How is a tie for a medal decided?

By a 25-target shoot-off on a Compak with one single and two simultaneous doubles. If still tied, sudden death follows: the first zero eliminates, with equal targets shot. Be ready within 15 minutes of the call or you are considered withdrawn.

How does a sudden-death shoot-off between two shooters run?

After a draw, both shoot the station 1 single, then the first double and the second double, the starting shooter alternating each time; then they move on to station 2 and so on, until the first eliminating zero.

I tied for 5th place. Will there be a shoot-off?

No. Below third place tied shooters are ranked equal and listed by countback from the 8th Compak to the 1st.

Organiser: what are the field dimensions of a Compak?

A fly-over rectangle 35-40 m wide and 25 m deep, marked by four stakes about 50 cm high. Five 1 m square stations 2-5 m apart, with the front line 4-8 m behind side AD and station 3 centred on it, each fitted with a Firing Angle Limiter.

Organiser: how many traps do I need and where can they go?

Six traps, lettered A-F from left to right with signs. Placement is free if every target, even a NO BIRD, is safe for everyone; a trap behind the stations must be at least 4 m higher (tower), and every target must fly over the rectangle in calm air.

Organiser: which trajectories are compulsory?

Three: a left-to-right crosser over AB and CD, a right-to-left crosser over CD and AB, and a going-away target over BC (from a trap sunk into or standing on the ground in front, or from a tower behind). The rest are free but must stay short.

Organiser: can I use the club's own machines for a world championship?

No. Continental and world championships must use 48 new machines installed and adjusted for that championship only.

Organiser: when may we change the layouts?

Only after a complete round has been shot by all shooters: 50 targets with 2 installations or 100 targets with 4. Layouts are checked by the Technical Committee members present, or by the jury if none is present.

Organiser: are there ready-made menus we can use?

Yes, Annex 3 has 40 trajectory setting tables in five groups of eight, one group for each per-station combination of singles and doubles; FITASC advises using them.

Organiser: can one Compak mix report and simultaneous doubles?

No. On one Compak the double type must be the same on all five stations.

Organiser: which target types and colours are recommended?

Any standard or special target may be used. Pick a colour that stands out well; bicolour targets are not advised, nor battues on very short trajectories.

Who is on the jury and when can it decide?

Technical Commission members present, one representative per country with a men's national team, and Executive Committee members present (not on the appeal jury). It needs its President or representative plus a quarter of its members, decides by majority, and the President breaks a tie.

What safety rules apply when carrying a gun around the ground?

Carry it open and unloaded (semi-autos with the breech open, muzzle straight up or down), put it upright in a rack when not in use, never touch another competitor's gun without permission, and do not simulate shooting outside the stations.

When may I load and close my gun on the stand?

Load only on your station after the referee authorises the start, keeping the gun open and the muzzle down range inside the Firing Angle Limiter. Close it only when it is your turn, and unload before turning round or leaving.

Can the shoot be stopped for a thunderstorm?

Yes. The Head Referee may exceptionally interrupt for a sudden heavy downpour or violent storm that seems brief, and must inform the jury if it may last. After more than 10 minutes the singles are shown again.
