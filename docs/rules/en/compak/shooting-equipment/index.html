Shooting equipment

Chapter 18 of the FITASC Compak® Sporting rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

18.1PDF, p. 19 ↗

Which guns are allowed

Any smooth-bore hunting gun is allowed if its calibre is not larger than 12 and its barrel is at least 66 cm (26 inches) long. Pump-action shotguns and drillings are prohibited. Slings and straps are banned on all guns, as are micro cameras fixed to the gun and any artificial aiming device.

  • Smooth bore, calibre 12 or smaller.
  • Barrel at least 66 cm (26 in).
  • Pump-action and drilling prohibited.
  • No slings, no gun cameras, no aiming devices.
18.1PDF, p. 19 ↗

Semi-automatics: shell catcher and two cartridges

A semi-automatic shotgun must have a device that stops its ejected cases from bothering the shooter next to it, and it may be loaded with no more than two cartridges.

  • Shell deflector/catcher compulsory.
  • Max 2 cartridges loaded.
18.1PDF, p. 19 ↗

No changing gun, chokes or barrels during a round

Within the same round it is prohibited to change the gun, wholly or in part, including interchangeable chokes or barrels. Such changes are allowed between rounds. The exception is a malfunction, where chapters 12 and 21.4 apply.

  • No change of gun, choke or barrel within a round.
  • Allowed between rounds.
  • Malfunction: see chapter 12 and 21.4.
18.2PDF, p. 19 ↗

Release trigger: the 'R' sticker

A gun with a release trigger has to be marked so others can see it: its owner sticks a fluorescent label with a large letter 'R' on the outside of the stock. Some host countries forbid release triggers by law; in that case the programme of the international competition must say so.

  • Fluorescent label with a large 'R'.
  • On the outside of the stock.
  • A legal ban in the host country is announced in the programme.
18.3PDF, p. 19 ↗

Cartridges: 28 g lead, shot 2-2.5 mm

Each cartridge may hold at most 28 g of lead shot; a tolerance of +2% is accepted. The pellets must be round and all of the same size, 2 to 2.5 mm across, with ±0.1 mm accepted.

  • Shot load: 28 g of lead at most, +2% accepted.
  • Round pellets of one size, 2 to 2.5 mm, ±0.1 mm.
18.3PDF, p. 19 ↗

Prohibited ammunition

Reloaded cartridges are prohibited, and so are dispersers or any other special loading devices. Mixing shot of different sizes or qualities is strictly forbidden, and black powder and tracer cartridges are also banned. Shots fired with non-compliant ammunition before an inspection count as zero (chapter 23).

  • No reloads.
  • No dispersers or other loading devices.
  • No mixed shot sizes or qualities.
  • No black powder or tracers.
18.3PDF, p. 20 ↗

Disposing of empty cases

If a station has a bin or container for empty cases, shooters must use it. Shooters with semi-automatics must pick up their empty cases when leaving the stand and put them in the bin. Breaking this rule brings the usual penalties (yellow card / red card).

  • Use the bin if provided.
  • Semi-auto users collect their cases.
  • Penalty: yellow card / red card.
18.4PDF, p. 20 ↗

Hearing protection is compulsory

Competitors, referees, staff and spectators near a Compak Sporting event must wear hearing protection. A competitor who arrives at the station without it is treated as absent and may not shoot.

  • Compulsory for competitors, referees, staff and public nearby.
  • Without it at the station: considered absent, no shooting.
More:Safety
18.5PDF, p. 20 ↗

Protective glasses are compulsory

Competitors, referees and staff must wear protective eyewear. A competitor who comes to the station without it is treated as absent and may not shoot.

  • Compulsory for competitors, referees and staff.
  • Without eyewear at the station: considered absent, no shooting.
More:Safety

Common questions on this chapter

Can I change chokes between stations?

No. Changing the gun, chokes or barrels is prohibited within a round; it is allowed between rounds.

What cartridges can I use?

Lead shot up to 28 g (+2% tolerance), spherical pellets 2 to 2.5 mm (+/-0.1 mm). Reloads, dispersers, mixed shot, black powder and tracers are banned.

Are 20-gauge guns allowed?

Yes. Any smooth-bore hunting gun of calibre 12 or smaller with a barrel of at least 66 cm (26 in) is allowed, except pump-actions and drillings.

Can I use a semi-automatic?

Yes, with a device that keeps ejected cases away from the neighbouring shooter and no more than two cartridges loaded. You must also collect your empty cases when you leave the stand.

Is a sling or a camera on the gun allowed?

No. Slings and straps are banned, and so are micro cameras mounted on the gun and any artificial aiming device.

I shoot with a release trigger. What do I need to do?

Put a sticker with a big 'R' on a fluorescent background on the outer side of the stock. On a NO BIRD before your first shot, keep the trigger held and call again, open the gun while holding it, or tell the referee and fire in the safe direction he shows. Check the programme in case the host country bans release triggers.

Are ear and eye protection compulsory?

Yes. A competitor at the station without hearing protection or without protective glasses is treated as absent and may not shoot.
