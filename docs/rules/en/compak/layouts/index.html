Layouts

Chapter 5 of the FITASC Compak® Sporting rulebook in our own words: every rule point with its reference and a link to the official PDF page.

Source: Official FITASC PDF ↗ · Edition: 01/01/2025 · Updated: 2026-10-09

The eight Compaks of an international competition

An international competition is shot on four kinds of Compak: three with 3 singles and a double on report per station, one with 3 singles and a simultaneous double, three with 1 single and two doubles on report, and one with 1 single and two simultaneous doubles. That adds up to eight Compaks of 25 targets, i.e. 200 targets (our calculation; the rulebook lists only the four kinds).

  • 3 x (3 singles + 1 double on report).
  • 1 x (3 singles + 1 simultaneous double).
  • 3 x (1 single + 2 doubles on report).
  • 1 x (1 single + 2 simultaneous doubles).
  • 8 Compaks x 25 = 200 targets.

Who sets and checks the layouts

The layouts are settled before the competition starts, with the person in charge of the ground present. There are two ways: the jury or a person it names picks ready-made trajectory setting tables (Annex 3), or the person in charge of the layouts draws them up. Either way, the FITASC Technical Committee members who are present check the result and may change it so the trajectories are safe, varied and balanced. If none of them is present, the jury does this check.

  • Settled before the start, with the ground manager present.
  • Way 1: ready-made trajectory setting tables (Annex 3).
  • Way 2: layouts drawn up by the person in charge.
  • Checked by the Technical Committee members present, otherwise by the jury.

When layouts may be changed

Trajectories, traps, menus, and the distances between stations or between the stations and the theoretical line may be changed only after a complete round, once all shooters have shot the same round of targets. A complete round means 50 targets with 2 installations or 100 targets with 4 installations.

  • Changes only after a complete round shot by everyone.
  • Complete round: 50 targets (2 installations) or 100 targets (4 installations).
  • Covers trajectories, traps, menus and station distances.

Championships: 48 new machines

Continental and world Compak Sporting championships must be fair to everyone, so the host club may not use the machines it normally shoots on. Instead, 48 new machines are required, set up and adjusted for that championship alone.

  • The host club's everyday machines may not be used.
  • 48 new machines are required.
  • Set up and adjusted for that championship alone.

Common questions on this chapter

How many targets is an international Compak competition?

It is shot on eight Compaks of 25 targets each: three with 3 singles + a double on report, one with 3 singles + a simultaneous double, three with 1 single + two doubles on report, and one with 1 single + two simultaneous doubles. That makes 200 targets (our calculation).

Organiser: can I use the club's own machines for a world championship?

No. Continental and world championships must use 48 new machines installed and adjusted for that championship only.

Organiser: when may we change the layouts?

Only after a complete round has been shot by all shooters: 50 targets with 2 installations or 100 targets with 4. Layouts are checked by the Technical Committee members present, or by the jury if none is present.
