Clays, not birds: what this sport is

Clay shooting is a sport in which a shooter breaks small flying discs with a shotgun. The disc is called a target or a clay; shooters often call it a bird, but nobody shoots at live animals. The FITASC rules say plainly that shooting at live animals is not allowed, and safety and care for the environment come first.

A machine called a trap throws each target. Its path through the air (or along the ground) is the trajectory. The gun is a smooth-bore shotgun: it fires a cartridge full of small pellets called shot, which spread into a small cloud. That is why the shooter points the gun where the target is going, not where it is now.

This course is about two disciplines of the international federation FITASC. Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ (Sporting™ for short) imitates hunting: traps are hidden in fields and woods, and targets fly like partridge, duck or pheasant, or roll like a hare. Compak® Sporting is the compact version: the same variety of targets, but on one small field with five stations in a line and six traps.

You will also hear about Trap and Skeet. They are different disciplines with fixed, repeated trajectories; Olympic Trap and Skeet belong to another federation. Do not mix their rules with Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting.

A hit is visible at once: the target breaks or at least a visible piece flies off. This clarity is what makes the sport easy to watch and, for you, easy to follow from the very first day.

Key points
  • People shoot at clay targets thrown by traps, never at live animals.
  • A shotgun fires a cloud of small pellets (shot), so the shooter points ahead of the target.
  • Sporting™ imitates hunting over natural ground; Compak® Sporting is its compact version on one field.
  • Trap and Skeet are other disciplines with their own rules.

The coach says

“Watch this bird off trap B.”
'Bird' is a target, not a real bird; trap B is the machine marked B.
“Today we walk the Sporting course, tomorrow it's Compak.”
Two different disciplines on two different days.
“Point it, don't aim it.”
A shotgun is swung and pointed with both eyes on the target, not aimed like a rifle.

Check yourself

A coach says 'Watch the next bird.' What will you see?
  1. A live pigeon released from a box
  2. A clay target thrown by a trap
  3. A bird-shaped paper target on a board
  4. A drone
Correct!

A clay target thrown by a trap — 'Bird' is shooters' slang for a clay target; the FITASC rules forbid shooting at live animals.

What does a shotgun fire?
  1. One single bullet
  2. A small arrow-like dart
  3. A cloud of small pellets (shot)
  4. A ball filled with paint
Correct!

A cloud of small pellets (shot) — The shot leaves the muzzle as a small cloud of pellets, so the shooter points ahead of the moving target.

Which statement about Compak® Sporting is correct?
  1. A compact version on one field, stations in a line
  2. It is the Olympic version of the Skeet discipline
  3. It is shot only at live game birds in the field
  4. It uses a single trap and a single station
Correct!

A compact version on one field, stations in a line — Compak® Sporting brings Sporting™-style targets onto one small field with five stations and six traps.
