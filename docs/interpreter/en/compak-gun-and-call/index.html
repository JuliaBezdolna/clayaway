Gun position, the call and time

Here Compak® Sporting differs most visibly from Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™. At the call the gun position is free: the gun may already be at the shoulder, or not. There is no gun-down start and no line on the vest. Every target is still shot with the gun at the shoulder.

Release: traps are freed by hand, by remote control or by a voice-activated sonopull. With manual release the target must leave 0 to 3 seconds after the call; a sonopull has a fixed delay of 0.5 seconds. International in-line events use sonopull only. A target released more than 3 seconds after the call, or one the shooter did not call for, is a No Bird.

Time: the shooter has at most 10 seconds to call after the previous station's target has been shot, shorter than the 15 seconds in Sporting™. The first time, the referee notes a warning on the score sheet; if it happens again in the same round, it is a red card and the targets are scored: single zero, double on report zero and No Bird, simultaneous double zero and zero.

Viewing: in squad shooting the targets are shown once in the morning, to the first squad. The shooter on station 1 calls each single, A to F; then the referee shows the simultaneous doubles and says 'start' or 'competition'. After an interruption of more than 10 minutes, the six singles are shown again.

Loading: only on your own station, gun open and pointing down range inside the angle limiter; close it only when it is your turn.

Key points
  • Gun position at the call is free; shots always from the shoulder.
  • Manual release 0–3 s after the call; sonopull fixed 0.5 s; later than 3 s = No Bird.
  • Max 10 seconds to call: warning noted, then targets scored zero.
  • Targets are shown once in the morning to the first squad; again after a break over 10 minutes.

The coach says

“Start! / Competition!”
Referee: the presentation is over and scored shooting begins.
“Gun up or down, your choice here.”
Coach: in Compak® the gun position at the call is free.
“Ten seconds, call your target.”
Reminder of the 10-second limit to call.
“Warning: time.”
First time over 10 seconds; noted on the score sheet.

Check yourself

In Compak® Sporting a shooter calls with the gun already at the shoulder. What is the referee's decision?
  1. Yellow card
  2. Allowed: the gun position at the call is free
  3. Zero
  4. Allowed only on doubles
Correct!

Allowed: the gun position at the call is free — Compak® Sporting has no gun-down start and no vest line; only the shot itself must be fired from the shoulder.

Manual release. The target leaves about four seconds after the call, and the shooter misses. Decision?
  1. Zero
  2. One
  3. A warning for the trapper only, zero stands
  4. No Bird
Correct!

No Bird — A target released more than 3 seconds after the call is a No Bird and is thrown again.

For the second time in the same round, a shooter takes more than 10 seconds to call. The target is a single. Decision?
  1. Zero
  2. Another warning only
  3. No Bird
  4. Expulsion
Correct!

Zero — After the first warning, a repeat in the same round is penalised by the table: a single is scored zero.
