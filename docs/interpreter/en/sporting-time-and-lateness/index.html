Time limits and lateness

Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ runs on a tight rhythm, and several rules protect it. When the shooter calls 'pull' or 'ready', the referee relays the call to the trapper by an audio signal, and the target must leave within three seconds; a later target is a No Bird.

Between singles, or between doubles, the shooter has at most 15 seconds. The first time this is exceeded the referee gives a warning; after that the red-card penalties apply. After a final decision on a protest, the shooter must resume within 15 seconds, otherwise it counts as refusal to comply; deliberate stalling is treated the same way.

Lateness: when a shooter is missing, the referee calls the name and number three times within one minute. Arriving before one's turn on the singles of the first stand costs nothing. After that, every target not shot is zero; joining at a later stand means all targets of earlier stands are zero. A layout with missed stands can never be shot again in another squad.

A shooter late for reasons outside their control must not rejoin the squad in that round but appeal to the jury in writing. If the jury accepts, the round is shot in another squad; if not, 25 zeros for each layout not shot.

Stepping onto the stand without hearing protection or safety glasses counts as being absent. For an unscheduled shoot-off, shooters must be ready within 15 minutes of being called.

For you: never hold the squad up with a long translation. Short now, details after the stand.

Key points
  • Target within 3 seconds of the referee's signal, otherwise No Bird.
  • Max 15 seconds between singles or doubles: warning, then red card.
  • Resume within 15 seconds after a final ruling, or it is refusal to comply.
  • Late: name and number called three times in one minute; missed targets are zero.

The coach says

“Shooter number twenty-three!”
Referee calling a missing shooter (three times within a minute).
“Watch your time.”
Reminder of the 15-second limit between targets.
“Over fifteen seconds. This is a warning.”
First time over the limit: warning; next time a penalty.
“Glasses on, or you can't shoot.”
Without eye protection the shooter is treated as absent.

Check yourself

The shooter calls, the referee relays the signal, but the target leaves about five seconds later. The shooter fires and misses. Decision?
  1. Zero
  2. No Bird
  3. One
  4. A warning for the shooter
Correct!

No Bird — A target released more than three seconds after the referee's signal is a No Bird.

A shooter arrives while his squad is still shooting singles on stand 1, before his turn has come. What happens?
  1. He joins without penalty
  2. 25 zeros
  3. Zero on all singles of stand 1
  4. He must shoot with another squad
Correct!

He joins without penalty — Arriving before missing one's turn on the singles of the first stand carries no penalty.

Between two singles a shooter takes about 25 seconds, for the first time today. Decision?
  1. Zero on the next target
  2. Red card
  3. No consequence at all
  4. A warning from the referee
Correct!

A warning from the referee — The limit is 15 seconds; the first time it is exceeded the referee warns, and only then do penalties follow.
