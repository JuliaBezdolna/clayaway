Pick-up, hold and break point: planning a stand

Before every target the shooter sets three points. The pick-up point is where the eyes first see the target clearly, a little out from the trap rather than at its mouth, where the clay is only a blur. The hold point is where the muzzles wait at the call, usually on or just under the target's line, between the pick-up point and the break point. The break point is where the shooter plans to break the target.

Planning order: choose the break point first, set the feet to it, wind back to the hold point, then put the eyes on the pick-up point. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the stock is down until the target appears, so the hold point there means where the muzzles point while the gun is low.

Typical errors: a break point too far away means a fast, uncontrolled swing or a target already dropping; too close means too little movement and a “poke”. A hold point too near the trap makes the gun chase; too far out and the target overtakes the gun.

Targets are read during the presentation. In Sporting™ all trajectories are shown to the first shooter, and on request the referee repeats the presentation, up to two targets for each target of the menu; in squad Compak® Sporting the singles are shown once in the morning, for the first squad. The target must be broken where the referee can see it, inside the shooting limit.

“Where are you taking it?” asks for the break point; “kill point” is a neutral synonym.

Key points
  • Pick-up = eyes; hold = muzzles; break = where the target is broken.
  • Plan from the break point backwards: feet, hold, eyes.
  • The hold point is usually between pick-up and break, on or just under the line.
  • Read targets during the presentation; break them where the referee can see.
  • “Where are you taking it?” = where is your break point?

The coach says

“Where are you taking it?”
The coach asks where the shooter plans to break the target.
“Move your hold point halfway back to the trap.”
Hold the muzzles closer to the trap, at half of the current distance.
“Look further out — pick it up where it's sharp.”
Move the eyes away from the trap to where the target is first seen clearly.
“Hold a little lower, just under the line.”
Keep the muzzles slightly below the target's path at the call.

Check yourself

Which point is about the eyes, not the gun?
  1. Hold point
  2. Break point
  3. Pick-up point
  4. Insertion point
Correct!

Pick-up point — The pick-up point is where the eyes first see the target clearly; the hold point is for the muzzles.

What usually happens if the hold point is too close to the trap?
  1. The target overtakes the gun
  2. The referee calls No Bird
  3. The gun has to chase the target
  4. The target slows down
Correct!

The gun has to chase the target — Too near the trap, the target leaves at full speed past the muzzles and the shooter has to chase it.

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™, the shooter on the stand asks to see the presentation again. What is the maximum per target of the menu?
  1. Two targets
  2. None: no repeats are allowed
  3. As many as he wants
  4. Exactly five
Correct!

Two targets — The referee repeats the presentation on request, up to a maximum of two targets for each target on the menu.
