Singles and doubles

A single is one target from one trap. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the shooter may fire two cartridges at it, so a first-barrel miss can still be saved with the second. Every single must leave time for two shots.

A double is two targets, and the shooter has only two cartridges for it; both may go at the same target. There are three kinds. On report: the second target leaves 0 to 3 seconds after the shot at the first, released by that shot. Simultaneous: both leave at the same moment, from one or two traps. Rafale: two targets from the same trap on the same line, the second as fast as the trap reloads. In simultaneous and rafale doubles the targets may be taken in either order. Doubles use only targets already shown as singles from the same stand.

Decisions the referee often has to make: if one shot breaks both targets of any double, both count, one and one. If the shooter does not fire at a rafale or simultaneous double without a valid reason, it is zero and zero.

On a double on report, an irregular first target means the double is repeated; if the first target or its pieces break the second before the second shot, or the second is irregular, the double is repeated and the first result is kept. On simultaneous and rafale doubles, an irregular target means a repeat with no score recorded.

Tip: pass on a double's result as a pair, in the referee's order.

Key points
  • Single: two cartridges allowed.
  • Double: two cartridges in total, both may go at one target; kinds: on report, simultaneous, rafale.
  • One shot breaks both targets: one and one.
  • Not firing at a simultaneous or rafale double without reason: zero and zero.

The coach says

“Report pair: the second comes on your shot.”
Coach: the second target is released by the shot at the first.
“On the rafale, take either one first.”
Order is free in a rafale double.
“One and one.”
Referee: both targets of the double are hits.
“No Bird, repeat the double.”
Irregular target; the double is thrown again.

Check yourself

Simultaneous double. One shot breaks both targets. What is the referee's decision?
  1. One and one
  2. One and No Bird
  3. No Bird, the double is repeated
  4. One and zero
Correct!

One and one — In Sporting™, two targets broken with one shot in any double score one and one (Compak® Sporting differs).

Double on report. The shooter breaks the first target, and a piece of it smashes the second before the second shot. Decision?
  1. One and one
  2. The double is repeated and the first result is kept
  3. Zero on the second target
  4. One and zero
Correct!

The double is repeated and the first result is kept — When the first target or its pieces break the second before the second shot, the double is repeated and the first result stands.

Rafale double. Both targets fly correctly, but the shooter lowers the gun and fires at neither, giving no valid reason. Decision?
  1. No Bird
  2. Zero and No Bird
  3. A yellow card only
  4. Zero and zero
Correct!

Zero and zero — A correct target not fired at without a valid reason is lost; on a rafale or simultaneous double that means zero and zero.
