Hit or lost: the visible piece and the 'smoke' trap

A target is scored 'one' (a hit, «влучання») when it was properly thrown, the shooter fired by the rules, and at least one piece visibly broke off, or the target was wholly or partly destroyed. Flash targets follow the same rule. A target is 'zero' (lost, «промах») when no visible piece comes off and there is only dust or smoke.

So the rule turns on one picture: a visible piece. A small chip that flies off counts as a hit. A puff of dust with the target flying on in one piece is lost. On the score sheet a hit is usually marked X and a miss O.

Only the referee decides. Squad members may not give opinions, and nobody may pick up a target to check it. In Compak® Sporting the referee announces every zero loudly or with a sound signal. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the shooter must break targets where the referee can see them. By calling the next target the shooter accepts the decision on the previous one, so any objection must come at once.

Now the trap for interpreters. Coaches praise a perfect hit with 'smoked it', 'inked it' or 'powdered it': the target turned into a black cloud. That is the best possible hit, not a zero. But in the rules, 'only dust or smoke' means lost. 'Dusted it' is risky: it usually means only dust came off, a lost target, but some speakers use it as praise. If you are not sure, ask, because the two meanings are opposite.

Also: 'dead' means a hit (not «мертва»), 'lost' a miss (not «загублена»).

Key points
  • One = at least one visible piece broke off, or the target was wholly or partly destroyed.
  • Zero = no visible piece, only dust or smoke.
  • Only the referee decides; no picking up targets; calling the next target accepts the decision.
  • Coach slang 'smoked it' = perfect hit; rule wording 'only smoke' = lost.
  • 'Dead' = hit, 'lost' = miss; ask if 'dusted' is unclear.

The coach says

“Smoked it! That's the picture.”
A perfect hit: the target turned to dust; the shooter should remember that sight picture.
“Dead — only a chip, but it counts.”
A small visible piece broke off, so it is a hit.
“Lost — you were just behind it.”
A miss; the shot went behind the target.
“Don't argue with me later — say it now, before you call.”
An objection must be made at once; calling the next target accepts the ruling.

Check yourself

The target flies on whole, but a puff of dust appears. What is the score?
  1. Zero (lost)
  2. One (hit)
  3. No Bird
  4. Half a point
Correct!

Zero (lost) — Only dust or smoke, with no visible piece, is lost.

A coach shouts 'Smoked it!' How do you translate the meaning?
  1. It only smoked, so the target is lost
  2. The barrels are overheating
  3. A perfect hit: the target turned to dust
  4. The trap machine is on fire
Correct!

A perfect hit: the target turned to dust — In coach slang 'smoked it' is praise for a total break; the rule wording 'only smoke' means the opposite.

A squad member says the last target was hit. Who decides?
  1. A vote of the squad
  2. The coach
  3. The interpreter
  4. The referee alone
Correct!

The referee alone — Target decisions belong to the referee; squad members may not intervene.
