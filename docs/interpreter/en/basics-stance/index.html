Stance: feet to the break point

The stance is how the shooter stands before the shot. The space is small: in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the stand is a 1 m square or circle, and both feet must be inside it before the call. In Compak® Sporting each station is a 1 m square with a Firing Angle Limiter, a frame that stops the shooter stepping forward and limits the swing.

The usual base: feet about shoulder-width apart; for a right-hander the left foot slightly forward (a left-hander mirrors this); knees soft, not locked; weight slightly forward, roughly 60 per cent on the front foot. Coaches say “nose over toes”.

The key instruction: point the front foot at the break point, where the shooter plans to break the target. The body is then comfortable, “unwound”, exactly where the shot happens. The shooter turns back from the hips to the hold point and unwinds into the shot. If the feet face the trap, coaches warn, the body runs out of turn, and the gun stops or the head lifts.

The body turns from the hips, not the arms. Feet may be set slightly differently for every target.

Translate “front foot” and “back foot” as front and back, not left and right: your shooter may be left-handed.

Key points
  • Sporting™: both feet inside the 1 m stand before the call.
  • Compak® Sporting: 1 m station with a Firing Angle Limiter.
  • Feet shoulder-width, knees soft, weight slightly forward.
  • Front foot points at the break point; turn back from the hips to the hold point.
  • Translate front/back foot, not left/right.

The coach says

“Point your front foot at the break point.”
Turn the toe of the front foot towards the place where the target will be broken.
“Set your feet for the break, then wind back to the hold.”
First place the feet for the break point, then turn the upper body back to the hold point.
“Weight forward, soft knees.”
Lean slightly onto the front foot and do not lock the knees.
“Turn from the hips, not the arms.”
The whole upper body rotates; the arms do not swing the gun on their own.

Check yourself

Where does the coach usually want the front foot to point?
  1. At the trap
  2. At the pick-up point
  3. At the referee
  4. At the break point
Correct!

At the break point — Feet set to the break point leave the body comfortable where the shot happens; feet facing the trap make the body run out of turn.

What do the Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ rules require of the feet before the call?
  1. Both feet inside the stand
  2. The front foot on the stand line
  3. Feet together
  4. No requirement at all
Correct!

Both feet inside the stand — The ready-position rule asks for both feet inside the 1 m shooting stand before the shooter calls.

A left-handed shooter. The coach says “Open your front foot”. Which foot is it?
  1. Always the left one, whatever the hand
  2. The right one: a left-hander's front foot
  3. The back foot, nearer the trap
  4. Both feet together
Correct!

The right one: a left-hander's front foot — For a left-hander the front foot is the right one. Translating “front foot” as «передня нога» avoids the mistake.
