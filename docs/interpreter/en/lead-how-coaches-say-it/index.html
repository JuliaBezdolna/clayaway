How coaches say lead, and how to translate it

Units. British and American coaches talk in feet and yards: “give it four foot” (British for four feet), “two feet in front”. European coaches use metres. One foot is about 30 cm, one yard about 0.9 m. But a lead number is the coach's picture, not a measurement, so keep his number and unit. Never convert in the middle of an instruction; if the shooter asks, explain roughly between targets. Distances to a target (“it's 30 yards”) may be given as “about 27 m”.

Target widths: “half a bird”, “a bird's width”, “a clay's length”. Bird means target, never a real bird: «пів мішені». Barrel widths: “a barrel's width”, “two barrels” mean the gap seen at the muzzle. Careful: a referee's “two barrels” means both barrels fired together.

Relative words: “more”, “less”, “a touch more”, “double it”, “half that”, “same lead”. Keep them relative and never invent a number. “Same lead” is vital: the shooter must not add lead.

Directions: “in front” and “behind” refer to the target's travel; “over” and “high”, “under” and “low” are synonyms. A clock face describes positions: 12 o'clock is up, 3 o'clock right, as the shooter sees it. “You hit it at six” means the bottom edge; “it comes from eleven” gives a direction.

Method: translate number, unit, reference and direction exactly, short, in the coach's rhythm. If unclear, ask him one word: “Feet?” In competitions coaching is forbidden.

Key points
  • Keep the coach's number and unit; never convert lead mid-instruction.
  • Bird = target: “half a bird” = «пів мішені».
  • Relative words stay relative; “same lead” must be translated.
  • Clock face: 12 up, 3 right, as the shooter sees it.
  • A referee's “two barrels” is a double discharge, not a lead.

The coach says

“Give it four foot.”
Give the lead the coach calls “four feet”; it describes his picture, so keep his number and unit.
“Half a bird more.”
Add half a target's width to the lead picture.
“Double it.”
Make the lead picture twice as big as last time.
“Same lead, just keep it moving.”
The lead was right; the miss came from stopping the gun.
“You were at two o'clock — a touch low and more in front.”
The pattern was above and to the right of the target on the clock face; shoot slightly lower and give a little more lead.
“A barrel's width in front.”
Keep a gap the width of the barrel, as seen at the muzzle, ahead of the target.

Check yourself

The coach says “Give it four foot” to a shooter who thinks in metres. What do you say?
  1. «Дайте 1,22 метра»
  2. «Дайте приблизно метр»
  3. «Дайте чотири фути»
  4. «Дайте чотири метри»
Correct!

«Дайте чотири фути» — A lead number is the coach's picture; converting it mid-instruction adds false precision or errors, and “four metres” changes the meaning completely. If the shooter asks, explain roughly between targets.

What does “half a bird” mean?
  1. Half the width of a target
  2. A broken target
  3. A small bird flying near the trap
  4. Half of the round
Correct!

Half the width of a target — In clay shooting “bird” means the target, so the coach asks for half a target's width of lead.

The coach says “Same lead, keep it moving”. Why must “same lead” be translated?
  1. It is a call the referee uses
  2. Otherwise the shooter may add lead and miss
  3. It is a polite greeting between shooters
  4. It changes the order of the targets
Correct!

Otherwise the shooter may add lead and miss — After a miss most shooters add lead by themselves; “same lead” tells them the cause was the stopped gun, not the lead.
