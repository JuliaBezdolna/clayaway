Rounds, stands and the order of shooting

A Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ competition is counted in rounds of 25 targets. An international event is 200 targets, so eight rounds; each round is shot on one layout (a set of shooting stands) or one line.

Every layout gives 15 singles and 5 doubles. The number of stands and traps depends on the system: the old system uses 5 traps for 3 stands, while new-system lines have 4 or 5 stands with 3 or 4 traps each. Traps are lettered A, B, C, D from left to right as seen from the stand. The target menu in front of the stand lists them in shooting order, together with any special targets: 90, 70 or 60 mm, battue, rabbit.

The stand itself is a 1 m square or a circle 1 m across. Each trajectory has a difficulty class: A (an average shooter breaks more than 80%), B (60 to 80%) or C (40 to 60%).

Shooters go round in squads of up to six, formed by draw. At each stand everyone shoots the singles first and then the doubles, and the order of shooting rotates at each stand and again for the doubles. The others wait close by, without distracting the person shooting.

The referee, or a person the referee supervises, keeps the scorecard, marking each target under its trap letter. When leaving each stand, every shooter checks the score; a disagreement must be raised with the referee at once, and the referee's decision is final.

Key points
  • Round = 25 targets on one layout: 15 singles + 5 doubles; international event = 200 targets.
  • Traps A, B, C, D from left to right as seen from the stand; the menu gives the order and special targets.
  • Squads of up to six; singles first, then doubles; the order rotates.
  • Check your score when leaving each stand; the referee decides.

The coach says

“Singles first, then doubles.”
The standard order at every stand.
“Read the menu: the traps go A, B, C.”
The menu board lists the traps in the order they will be shot.
“Next shooter.”
The referee calls the next person onto the stand.
“Check your score before you leave the stand.”
Any disagreement must be raised now, not later.

Check yourself

How many targets does one Sporting™ layout give, and how are they split?
  1. 20: 10 singles and 5 doubles
  2. 25: all singles
  3. 25: 15 singles and 5 doubles
  4. 50: 25 singles and 25 doubles
Correct!

25: 15 singles and 5 doubles — Every layout type gives 15 singles plus 5 doubles, which is 25 targets.

In what order does a squad shoot at a stand?
  1. Doubles first, then singles
  2. Everyone shoots the singles first, then the doubles, and the order rotates
  3. Each shooter shoots the whole layout in one go
  4. The shooters choose freely
Correct!

Everyone shoots the singles first, then the doubles, and the order rotates — Singles come first, then doubles, in rotation, unless the jury decides otherwise.

Leaving the stand, a shooter sees that a hit was marked as a miss. What should he do?
  1. Wait for the end of the round and appeal to the jury
  2. Correct the card himself
  3. Ask the squad to vote
  4. Tell the referee at once; the referee decides
Correct!

Tell the referee at once; the referee decides — Scores are checked when leaving each stand; the referee alone decides, and there is no appeal afterwards.
