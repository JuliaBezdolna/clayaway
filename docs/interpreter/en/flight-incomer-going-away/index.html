Incomer, going-away, driven and overhead

An incomer flies towards the shooter, often from a distant trap or a tower, and usually drops as it comes. It can seem to slow down and hang, which tempts shooters to aim. It is normally taken out in front, before it gets overhead, and the barrels may cover (“blot out”) the target at the shot. That is intended, not a fault.

A going-away target leaves from near or behind the shooter and flies away. It needs little sideways lead, but it shrinks fast and often rises or drops, so the lead is mostly vertical and timing matters: do not wait. In Compak® Sporting a going-away target over side BC is compulsory; it may come from a trench in front or from a tower behind the stations, at least 4 m above them.

A driven target is an incomer that passes over the shooter's head, like a pheasant pushed by beaters. Pick it up early, move with it, swing up from behind through it, fire as the barrels cover it and keep swinging. Careful: French “en battue” (Italian “in battuta”) means driven game, not the battue target.

An overhead from behind comes over the shooter's back and goes away. It is seen late, so the shooter looks back for it early, mounts with it, fires as the muzzle touches its front and follows through.

“Incoming” and “going away” always describe the target relative to the shooter.

Key points
  • Incomer: comes towards the shooter, usually dropping; take it in front.
  • Going-away: little sideways lead, mostly vertical; shoot promptly.
  • Driven: an incomer over the head; swing through from behind and cover it.
  • Overhead from behind: look back early, mount with it, follow through.
  • Compak® Sporting always includes a going-away target.

The coach says

“Take it out in front, before it gets on top of you.”
Break the incomer while it is still in front, not when it is overhead.
“Blot it out and keep swinging.”
Let the barrels cover the target at the shot and do not stop the gun.
“Don't wait on the going-away — it's getting smaller.”
Shoot the going-away target promptly, because it is quickly getting further away.
“It comes from behind you — look back for it early.”
It is an overhead from behind; turn the eyes towards it before calling.

Check yourself

The coach says: “Blot it out.” What should the shooter do?
  1. Wipe the lenses of the safety glasses
  2. Let the barrels cover the target at the shot
  3. Wait until the target disappears from view
  4. Shoot only at the falling pieces
Correct!

Let the barrels cover the target at the shot — On incomers, driven and rising targets the gun often covers the clay at the moment of the shot; the coach asks for exactly that.

Why should a going-away target not be left too long?
  1. It quickly gets further away and smaller
  2. The rules allow only three seconds
  3. It always turns into a crosser
  4. The referee calls No Bird after the peak
Correct!

It quickly gets further away and smaller — A going-away target gains distance fast, so the longer you wait, the harder it becomes. Three seconds is the release time, not a shooting limit.

What makes a driven target different from an ordinary incomer?
  1. It is always a rabbit
  2. It comes from behind the shooter
  3. It passes over the shooter's head
  4. It is thrown only in Compak® Sporting
Correct!

It passes over the shooter's head — Driven means an incomer that comes over the head, like driven game. A target coming from behind is an overhead from behind.
