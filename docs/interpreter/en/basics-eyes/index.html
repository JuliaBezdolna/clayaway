Eyes: dominance, both eyes open, hard focus

Most people have a dominant (master) eye that the brain trusts more, usually on the same side as the dominant hand. The dominant eye must be the one looking along the rib. Cross-dominance means the dominant eye is on the other side from the shooting shoulder; central vision means neither eye clearly dominates. Either makes the gun point where the shooter does not expect, often to one side.

A simple test: with both eyes open, frame a distant object in a small triangle made with the hands, then close each eye in turn; the eye that keeps the object in the triangle is dominant. A coach may also stand in front of the shooter and look through the triangle.

Remedies chosen by the coach: close or squint the other eye as the gun mounts; a small dot on the safety glasses; learning to shoot from the other shoulder; or, more rarely, a cross-over stock. Both eyes open is preferred where possible, because two eyes judge speed, angle and distance better.

Focus: a soft, wide focus while waiting at the pick-up point, then a hard focus on the target itself, ideally its leading edge. Looking back at the barrel or bead to check the lead stops the gun.

Safety glasses are compulsory for shooters in both disciplines, so the dot goes on them.

Key points
  • The dominant eye must look along the rib.
  • Cross-dominance and central vision make the gun point off target.
  • Test with a hand triangle; the coach chooses the remedy.
  • Soft focus at pick-up, hard focus on the leading edge; never look back at the bead.
  • Safety glasses are compulsory in both disciplines.

The coach says

“Close your left eye as the gun comes up.”
Shut the left eye during the mount, not before, so the right eye controls the gun.
“Eyes on the clay, not the barrel.”
Keep looking at the target; checking the barrel stops the gun.
“Look at the leading edge.”
Focus on the front edge of the moving target, not its middle.
“Don't look at the trap — look out to your pick-up point.”
Wait with the eyes a little out along the line, where the target is first seen clearly.

Check yourself

A right-handed shooter has a left dominant eye. What is this called?
  1. Central vision
  2. Hard focus
  3. Cross-dominance
  4. Leading edge
Correct!

Cross-dominance — Cross-dominance is a dominant eye on the opposite side to the shooting shoulder.

Why is looking back at the bead to check the lead a fault?
  1. It is forbidden by the rules
  2. It stops the gun and the shot goes behind
  3. The bead reflects the sun
  4. It makes the target change colour
Correct!

It stops the gun and the shot goes behind — When the focus moves to the gun, the swing stops and the target becomes peripheral, so the shot goes behind.

Where does the anti-dominance dot usually go?
  1. On the safety glasses, before the eye to block
  2. On the bead at the end of the rib
  3. On the rib, halfway along it
  4. On the vest line, below the shoulder
Correct!

On the safety glasses, before the eye to block — Safety glasses are compulsory anyway, so a small dot on the lens in front of the non-dominant eye is the simplest remedy.
