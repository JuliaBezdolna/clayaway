1. The sport in ten minutes

Before you translate a single coaching tip, you need the big picture: what people actually shoot at, who writes the rules, what a Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ course and a Compak® Sporting field look like, and how a competition day runs. Five short lessons give you the map; every later mission fills in the details.

2. Gun and equipment

Coaches talk about the gun all day: 'raise the comb', 'check your selector', 'change chokes', 'nines in the bottom'. This mission gives you the words for the gun, its parts, chokes, cartridges and protective gear, together with the few equipment rules every interpreter must know.

3. Targets

A coach who says 'it's a midi off the tower, then a rabbit on report' assumes you can picture it. This mission teaches the target types, how colour and light change what the shooter sees, how traps release targets, how to read a target menu, and the one scoring question that decides everything: hit or lost.

Before any talk about lead, a coach fixes the basics: feet, gun mount, eyes, a three-point plan and a gun that keeps moving. These five lessons give you the coach's words for each step and the rules that limit what the shooter may do.

6. Lead (forward allowance) and shooting methods

Lead is what coaches talk about most: why the gun must point ahead of a moving target, the four ways of getting it there, and the strange units they use to describe it. This mission shows each method step by step and teaches you to translate “four foot”, “half a bird” or “double it” without changing the meaning.

When a target is missed, the coach names the miss in one or two words and then the cause. This mission teaches the words for misses and faults, what each one really means, and how to pass a coach's diagnosis to the shooter quickly and exactly.

8. Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ rules

Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ is the FITASC discipline that imitates hunting over natural ground. This mission walks you through its rules the way a referee applies them: rounds and stands, the gun-down start, singles and doubles, target viewing, malfunctions and time limits. Everything here is a paraphrase; the rule references open ClayArena's rules guide.

9. Compak® Sporting rules

Compak® Sporting packs the variety of Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ into a small field: five stations in a line, six traps and a fixed rectangle the targets must fly over. Many rules look the same, but gun position, time limits, doubles and penalties differ. An interpreter who mixes the two disciplines up gives a shooter wrong information.
