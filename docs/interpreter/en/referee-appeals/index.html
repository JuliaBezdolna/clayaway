Protests, deposits and the jury

A protest must happen on the spot. The shooter raises a hand and says “Protest!” or “Appeal!” on the stand, before calling the next target (in Compak® Sporting, before the next shooter calls). The referee stops the shooting and listens to the reason. By calling the next target the shooter accepts the previous ruling, so as an interpreter you must be fast here.

If the referee is sure, the decision is confirmed and becomes final. If in doubt, the referee may ask another referee, the trapper or the squad’s shooters, purely for advice, and then decides alone. Shooters may never walk out or pick up a target to check it, and a target broken behind a natural obstacle or beyond a shooting limit is the referee’s call.

Some decisions can never go to the jury: whether a target was one or zero, a No Bird, and whether a trajectory was correct. Other decisions can be appealed to the jury in writing with a deposit: in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ it is 40% of the entry fee; in Compak® the amount in force on the day, and only about incidents of the same day. If the appeal succeeds, the deposit is returned.

After the final ruling the shooter must resume within fifteen seconds in Sporting™ and ten in Compak®; otherwise it is a refusal to comply. Continuing to argue, challenging the referee for show, or other people openly criticising a ruling counts as an attempt to influence the referee. Your job is to translate the protest and the answer accurately, not to argue the case.

Key points
  • Protest immediately, on the stand, hand raised: “Protest!” / “Appeal!”
  • Calling the next target = accepting the previous ruling
  • Never appealable: one/zero, No Bird, correct trajectory
  • Written appeal to the jury with a deposit (Sporting™: 40% of the entry fee)
  • Resume within 15 s (Sporting™) or 10 s (Compak®); further arguing = influencing the referee

The coach says

“Protest! The target was broken before I fired.”
The shooter formally challenges the ruling and gives the reason (here: a broken target was thrown).
“I’m certain — the decision stands.”
The referee confirms; the ruling is final.
“Did anyone see a piece come off?”
The referee is in doubt and asks the squad for advice only; the referee still decides.
“You can take it to the jury in writing, with the deposit.”
This decision is appealable; a written appeal and a deposit are needed.
“Please carry on shooting.”
The ruling is final; resume now or it becomes a refusal to comply.

Check yourself

Your shooter thinks a target scored zero was hit, but has already called the next target. Can he still protest that ruling?
  1. Yes, at the end of the round
  2. Yes, in writing to the jury
  3. No: calling the next target means accepting the previous ruling
  4. Yes, if the squad agrees
Correct!

No: calling the next target means accepting the previous ruling — By calling the next target the shooter accepts the previous ruling, and one/zero decisions never go to the jury anyway.

Which decision CAN be appealed to the jury?
  1. Whether a target was one or zero
  2. A No Bird
  3. Whether a trajectory was correct
  4. Another referee decision, such as a lateness penalty
Correct!

Another referee decision, such as a lateness penalty — One/zero, No Bird and trajectory decisions are final; other referee decisions may go to the jury in writing with a deposit.

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™, how big is the deposit for a written appeal to the jury?
  1. 40% of the entry fee
  2. The full entry fee
  3. A fixed 50 euros
  4. No deposit is needed
Correct!

40% of the entry fee — The Sporting™ rules set the deposit at 40% of the entry fee; it is returned if the jury agrees with the shooter.
