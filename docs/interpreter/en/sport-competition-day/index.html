A competition day from start to finish

The day starts at registration: the shooter confirms the entry, receives a competitor number and pins it on the upper back, fully visible. Then everyone checks the squad lists and shooting times. Keeping to the timetable is the shooter's own responsibility.

A squad is a group of up to six shooters, drawn by lot, who shoot together. In ClayAway the word also means a subgroup of a tour, so make clear which one you mean. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the squad shoots all the singles of a stand first, then the doubles, and the shooting order rotates from stand to stand. The first shooter of the squad sees the targets shown before anyone shoots.

A round is 25 targets: one layout in Sporting™, one field in Compak® Sporting. An international competition is 200 targets, so eight rounds over several days. The referee marks each target on the score sheet, usually X for a hit and O for a miss. Shooters check their score when leaving a stand in Sporting™ and sign the sheet at the end of a Compak® Sporting round.

Being late is costly. In Sporting™ the referee calls the name three times within one minute; a shooter who misses his turn gets zeros for the targets not shot. In Compak® Sporting a shooter who arrives after the first shooter of the squad has called gets 25 zeros for the round.

If shooters tie for the top three places, there is a shoot-off: 25 targets, then sudden death. Lower ties are separated by countback, comparing the last round first. The day ends with the results board and the prize-giving.

Key points
  • Registration, competitor number on the back, squad lists and times.
  • A squad is up to six shooters drawn by lot; in Sporting™ singles first, then doubles, with rotating order.
  • A round is 25 targets; international events are 200 targets.
  • Lateness costs zeros; in Compak® Sporting 25 zeros for the round.
  • Top-three ties: shoot-off of 25, then sudden death; lower ties: countback.

The coach says

“You're in squad twelve, starting on layout five at nine-thirty.”
Squad number, start layout and start time.
“Check your card before you leave the stand.”
Make sure the referee recorded your score correctly now; later it is too late.
“Twenty-two in the first round is fine — seven more to go.”
22 out of 25 in the first round; seven rounds remain in a 200-target event.
“Stay close — if it's a tie, you shoot within fifteen minutes of the call.”
A shooter who may be in a shoot-off must be ready soon after being called.

How many targets is an international FITASC competition?
  1. 50
  2. 100
  3. 150
  4. 200
Correct!

200 — 200 targets in rounds of 25.

In Compak® Sporting by squad, a shooter arrives after the first shooter has called for the first target. What happens?
  1. Nothing, he joins in
  2. He gets 25 zeros for that round
  3. He shoots the round alone later without penalty
  4. He loses one target
Correct!

He gets 25 zeros for that round — In squad Compak® Sporting a late arrival after the first call is scored 25 zeros; an appeal to the jury is possible for a valid reason.

Two shooters tie for 5th place. How are they separated?
  1. By a 25-target shoot-off
  2. By drawing lots after the event
  3. By countback from the last round
  4. By age, the younger first
Correct!

By countback from the last round — Shoot-offs are only for the top three; below that, countback compares the 8th round, then the 7th and so on.
