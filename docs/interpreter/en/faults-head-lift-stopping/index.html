Head lift, stopping, riding, poking

After the direction, the coach names the cause. Four causes come up every training day, each with its own short word. Learn them in pairs: the fault and the cure.

Head lift: the cheek leaves the stock at or just before the shot, often because the shooter wants to see the clay break. The eye is the rear sight, so lifting it moves the picture, and the shot usually goes over. Cure: 'head down', 'cheek on the stock', 'stay in the gun'.

Stopping the gun: the muzzles halt at the shot, usually because the shooter checks the lead on the barrel. The shot goes behind. A lighter version is checking: the swing hesitates for a split second. Cure: 'keep the gun moving', 'follow through', 'finish the shot'.

Riding the target: following the clay too long before firing, so it slows, drops or curls away and the shooter loses the line. Cure: 'take it earlier', 'commit to it'. Poking (also 'aiming', 'measuring'): pointing the shotgun slowly like a rifle, eyes on the bead instead of the clay. It is typical when the break point is too close, or on a teal at the top. Cure: 'eyes on the clay', 'let the gun go'.

Coaches often name the cause before the shooter has even lowered the gun. Translate it in the same breath and with the same verb: 'you stopped' is not 'you missed'. A word like 'aiming' is almost always a criticism, so never turn it into advice such as 'aim better'.

Key points
  • Head lift: shot usually over; cure: head down, stay in the gun.
  • Stopping or checking: shot behind; cure: keep the gun moving, follow through.
  • Riding: the clay slows or drops away; cure: take it earlier, commit.
  • Poking or aiming: slow rifle-like pointing; cure: eyes on the clay.
  • Translate the cause at once, with the coach's own verb.

The coach says

“You lifted your head, that's why it went over.”
The cheek left the stock, so the shot went above the clay.
“Keep the gun moving, you stopped on it.”
The swing halted at the shot; continue moving through and after it.
“You checked just before the shot, commit to it.”
A brief hesitation before firing; decide and shoot without slowing.
“Stop aiming, look at the bird and let the gun go.”
Criticism: the shooter is pointing like a rifle; focus on the target and swing freely.
“You rode it too long, take it earlier.”
The shooter followed the clay too long; break it sooner.
“Stay in the gun.”
Keep the cheek on the stock until after the shot.

Check yourself

The coach says 'You lifted your head'. Where did the shot most likely go?
  1. Behind
  2. Over the top
  3. Under
  4. In front
Correct!

Over the top — The eye is the rear sight: when the head lifts, the shot usually goes over.

A coach shouts 'Stop aiming!'. What does he want?
  1. Don't point like a rifle: look at the target and swing
  2. Aim more precisely
  3. Cease fire at once
  4. Lower the gun
Correct!

Don't point like a rifle: look at the target and swing — 'Aiming' in a coach's mouth is a fault (poking); the cure is eyes on the clay and a free swing.

'You rode it too long' means…
  1. The shooter fired too early
  2. The trap was too slow
  3. The shooter started too far behind
  4. The shooter followed the clay too long before firing
Correct!

The shooter followed the clay too long before firing — Riding the target means following it too long, so it slows or drops and the line is lost.
