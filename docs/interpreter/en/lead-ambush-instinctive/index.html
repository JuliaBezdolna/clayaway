Ambush and instinctive shooting

Ambush, or spot shooting (in Ukrainian it can be described as «стрільба в точку» or «на перехоплення»), is the opposite of a swing. 1) The muzzles are placed at a point ahead of the target, on its line, close to the break point. 2) They wait with little or no movement. 3) The trigger is pulled before the target reaches that spot: the shooter's reaction and the flight of the shot both take time, so the target flies into the shot. Firing only when the target arrives at the spot sends the shot behind it.

As a general habit, typical of untrained shooters, it is unreliable: the gun is still and the timing must be perfect. But a deliberate ambush is sometimes the right choice: a target that must be taken very fast, or a rabbit visible only in a short gap between bushes. A related move on edge-on battues at the top of their flight is the “collapse”: hold very close to the break point and make a minimal move. The target must still break where the referee can see it.

Instinctive shooting means no conscious lead at all. The shooter keeps a hard focus on the target and lets the hands follow; the swing and the mount happen together, and the shot comes as the gun reaches the face. The classic school is the one associated with the English gunmaker Robert Churchill, popular in game shooting.

Many top Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ shooters mix methods, choosing per target. “Which method for this one?” is a normal coaching question.

Key points
  • Ambush: muzzles still at a point ahead; fire before the target gets there, so it flies into the shot.
  • Unreliable as a habit, useful deliberately (very fast targets, short gaps).
  • “Collapse”: minimal move near the break point on edge-on battues.
  • Instinctive: no conscious lead, hard focus, mount and swing together (Churchill school).
  • Good shooters choose the method per target.

The coach says

“You're ambushing it — keep the gun moving with the target.”
The shooter is waiting at a point; the coach wants a moving method here.
“Ambush this one in the gap.”
Deliberately wait at the opening and fire just before the target reaches your point, so it flies into the shot.
“Don't think about the lead, just look at the bird and let the gun go to it.”
Shoot instinctively: focus on the target and let the hands follow.
“Hold right on the break point and just collapse onto it.”
Put the muzzles almost at the break point and make only a tiny move as the battue arrives.

Check yourself

What characterises ambush (spot shooting)?
  1. The gun swings faster than the target
  2. The gun waits at a point ahead of the target
  3. The gun tracks the target, then accelerates
  4. The gun starts behind the target, on its line
Correct!

The gun waits at a point ahead of the target — Ambush means little or no swing: the muzzles wait on the line and the target flies into the shot.

When can a deliberate ambush be a good choice?
  1. On every long, steady crosser
  2. Never: the rules forbid it
  3. Only in Compak® Sporting, never in Sporting™
  4. On a rabbit seen only in a short gap
Correct!

On a rabbit seen only in a short gap — When the target is visible only briefly, there is no time for a swing, so waiting at the gap can work. No rule forbids any method.

What is instinctive shooting?
  1. Hard focus on the target, no conscious lead
  2. Shooting without safety glasses
  3. Shooting from the hip, without mounting
  4. Waiting at the trap for every target
Correct!

Hard focus on the target, no conscious lead — Instinctive shooting relies on eye–hand coordination; it is still done with the gun at the shoulder, as the rules require.
