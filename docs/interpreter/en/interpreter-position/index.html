Where to stand and when to speak

In training, stand directly behind the shooter, next to the coach, behind the stand or cage and out of any line of the barrels. From there you see what the coach sees, the target and the muzzles together, and the coach can talk to you quietly. Beside the shooter is the most dangerous place; in front of the stand is out of the question.

Choose the mode for the moment. Consecutive interpreting (the coach speaks, pauses, you speak) suits the safety briefing, the walk between stands and the debrief. Whispered interpreting, chuchotage, lets the coach keep talking while you whisper into the shooter’s ear from behind; it is tiring, so alternate the two. Agree on a “pause, I’m translating” sign, such as a raised palm.

Timing is everything. The shooter’s routine runs from closing the gun to the shot. Speak only when the gun is open: between targets, between pairs, after the stand. Between pairs, mirror the coach’s short cues, “more in front”, “head down”, “same again”, and keep the long explanations for later. Remember the clock: in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ a shooter has at most 15 seconds between targets or doubles, and in Compak® Sporting 10 seconds to call. Your words use that time too.

In competition, wait where the squad waits, close to the stand but never distracting the shooter: a clearly disturbed shooter gets a No Bird, and nobody wants to be the cause. Learn the coach’s hand signals at the start and translate them once for your guests; after that they will need you less, and that is a good sign.

Key points
  • Training: directly behind the shooter, next to the coach, out of the barrels’ line
  • Consecutive for briefings and debriefs; chuchotage on the stand; alternate
  • Speak only when the gun is open
  • Short cues between pairs; time limits 15 s (Sporting™) and 10 s (Compak®)
  • Competition: wait with the squad, never distract

Check yourself

Where should you stand during a training session on a Sporting™ stand?
  1. Beside the shooter, to see the eyes
  2. Behind the shooter next to the coach, out of the barrels’ line
  3. In front of the cage, facing the shooter
  4. Next to the trap
Correct!

Behind the shooter next to the coach, out of the barrels’ line — From behind you see what the coach sees and stay safe; beside or in front is dangerous.

What is chuchotage?
  1. Interpreting after the speaker pauses
  2. Written translation of the rules
  3. Translating hand signals
  4. Whispered simultaneous interpreting into the listener’s ear
Correct!

Whispered simultaneous interpreting into the listener’s ear — Chuchotage is whispered simultaneous interpreting; consecutive is the pause-and-speak mode.

In Compak® Sporting, how long does a shooter have to call after the previous station’s target was shot?
  1. 10 seconds
  2. 15 seconds
  3. 30 seconds
  4. No limit
Correct!

10 seconds — Compak® gives at most 10 seconds to call; Sporting™ allows 15 seconds between targets or doubles.
