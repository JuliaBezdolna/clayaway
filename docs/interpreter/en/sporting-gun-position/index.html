The gun-down start

The most visible rule of Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ is the gun-down start. Before calling, the shooter stands with both feet inside the stand, the heel of the stock touching the body and the top rear point of the stock below a line on the vest. This ready position is held until the target has been released and can be seen.

The line is marked on the vest or jacket 25 cm below the shoulder axis, parallel to it, in a colour that contrasts with the vest so the referee can see it. The rule covers every stock type, adjustable combs included.

The gun may come up only when the target appears. Every target, rabbits included, is shot with the gun at the shoulder; firing from the hip is not allowed. Between the first and second targets of any double the gun position is free, but before the first target the ready position applies.

Penalties: the first time a shooter calls in a wrong position or mounts before the target appears, the referee gives a verbal warning with a yellow card. Any further offence on the same layout brings a red card, and the target is scored zero on a single, zero and No Bird on a double on report, zero and zero on a simultaneous or rafale double.

Mounting and swinging along the imagined line before calling (mock shooting) is forbidden too: a warning first, then a zero. If a coach drills a gun-up start before a Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ event, translate the word 'drill' too.

Key points
  • Before the call: both feet in the stand, stock touching the body, top of the stock below the vest line.
  • The vest line is 25 cm below the shoulder axis, in a contrasting colour.
  • Mount only when the target appears; always shoot from the shoulder; free position between the targets of a double.
  • First offence: warning + yellow card; next on the same layout: red card and the target scored.

The coach says

“Gun down, please.”
Referee: bring the stock below the vest line before calling.
“Your stock was above the line. This is a warning.”
First gun-position offence: verbal warning with a yellow card.
“Don't mount before the target appears.”
The gun may come up only once the target is visible.
“Red card, zero.”
A repeat offence on the same layout; the single is lost.

Check yourself

First stand. A shooter calls with the gun already at the shoulder. It is his first offence on this layout. What is the referee's decision?
  1. The target is scored zero
  2. A verbal warning with a yellow card
  3. No Bird and nothing else
  4. Expulsion
Correct!

A verbal warning with a yellow card — The first gun-position offence is a verbal warning backed by a yellow card.

Same layout, two stands later: the same shooter calls for a single with the stock above the vest line. Decision?
  1. Another yellow card
  2. No Bird
  3. Red card, and the target is repeated
  4. Red card, and the single is scored zero
Correct!

Red card, and the single is scored zero — After the warning, a further offence on the same layout is a red card: zero on a single.

Double on report. After breaking the first target, the shooter lowers the gun slightly before the second. Decision?
  1. Allowed: the gun position is free between the two targets
  2. Yellow card
  3. Zero on the second target
  4. No Bird
Correct!

Allowed: the gun position is free between the two targets — The free position applies between the targets of any double; the ready position is required only before the first one.
