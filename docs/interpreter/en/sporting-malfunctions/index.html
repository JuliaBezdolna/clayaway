Gun and cartridge malfunctions

A malfunction is a failure of gun or cartridge: a misfire, a dead second barrel, or both barrels firing together (double discharge). In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ incidents are counted per round of 25 targets.

First, what the shooter must do: stay in place, gun pointing down the range, without opening it or touching the safety until the referee has examined it. Opening the gun or touching the safety first means the target or targets are scored zero.

The first incident in a round is a No Bird with a warning, and the target is repeated. From the second incident in the same round, the target is no longer repeated but scored zero.

Details of a first incident: if the first shot fails, a single or any double is repeated, but if the shooter still fires the second shot, that result stands. If the second shot fails on a single, the target is repeated and may then only be broken with the second shot; a first-shot break scores zero. On a double on report the first result is kept and the double repeated. A double discharge on a single, or on the first target of any double, is a No Bird with nothing recorded.

If the gun is fixed before the squad finishes the stand, the shooter may shoot last with the referee's permission; otherwise he may continue at once with another gun, or finish in another squad with the jury's approval. Gun, choke or barrel may not be changed once he is on the stand.

Key points
  • After a failure: stay put, gun down range, do not open it or touch the safety.
  • First incident in a round: No Bird + warning, target repeated.
  • Second incident in the same round: zero, no repeat.
  • Second-shot failure on a single: on the repeat only a second-shot break counts.

The coach says

“Stay there, don't open the gun.”
Referee: wait for the inspection; opening first costs the target.
“Let me see the gun.”
The referee examines the gun or cartridge.
“Malfunction. No Bird, first warning.”
First incident in the round: the target is repeated.
“Second malfunction this round. Zero.”
No repeat this time; the target is lost.

Check yourself

First gun problem of the round: on a single the first cartridge misfires. The shooter waits, gun down range, for the referee. Decision?
  1. Zero
  2. No Bird with a warning; the target is repeated
  3. One
  4. Yellow card and zero
Correct!

No Bird with a warning; the target is repeated — The first gun or ammunition incident in a round is a No Bird with a warning, and the single is repeated.

The cartridge misfires, and the annoyed shooter breaks the gun open to look at the primer before the referee arrives. Decision?
  1. No Bird
  2. The target is repeated after a warning
  3. Zero
  4. Red card, and the target is repeated
Correct!

Zero — Opening the gun or touching the safety before the referee's check means the target is scored zero.

Single, first incident: the first shot misses and the second cartridge misfires, so the target is repeated. On the repeat the shooter breaks it with the first shot. Decision?
  1. One
  2. No Bird again
  3. One, with a warning
  4. Zero: on this repeat only a second-shot break counts
Correct!

Zero: on this repeat only a second-shot break counts — After a second-shot failure the repeated single may only be broken with the second shot, so the first shot must be a deliberate miss.
