Dress and conduct

Compak® Sporting events are public, so dress must be suitable and practical. Short shorts are banned, but Bermudas ending no more than 5 cm above the knee are fine. Tops need at least short sleeves and must reach the neck; a T-shirt is fine, but not a bare chest under the vest. Sandals are forbidden in practice and in competition. The number is worn on the back, fully visible; a first breach brings a warning, then sanctions.

Ear and eye protection are compulsory. Arriving at the station without them counts as absence: no shooting.

Punctuality: report to the referee in time. In squad shooting, arriving after the first shooter of the squad has called for the first target costs 25 zeros for the round; an appeal goes to the jury in writing on the same day.

Conduct: coaching during competition is forbidden; the competitor gets a yellow card and the coach is removed from the shooting area. Disputing a final decision after shooting resumes, or openly criticising a decision as a shooter or spectator, is an attempt to influence the referee and can lead to exclusion. At the end of every round the shooter signs the score sheet; refusing to sign means losing the right to complain.

Near the stand you are part of the public: wear protection, keep quiet during the call, never comment on a decision. Whether you may relay a coach's words in competition is an open question for FITASC; the safe practice is to interpret only for the referee, jury and organiser.

Key points
  • No short shorts, no bare chest under the vest, no sandals (also in practice); number on the back.
  • No ear or eye protection at the station = absent, no shooting.
  • Late after the squad's first call: 25 zeros; same-day written appeal possible.
  • No coaching; no public criticism of decisions; sign the score sheet.

The coach says

“Number on your back, please.”
Referee: the competition number must be fully visible on the back.
“Ears and eyes on.”
Put on hearing protection and glasses before the station.
“Sign the sheet, please.”
End of round: the shooter signs the score sheet.
“No coaching on the stand.”
Referee warning: coaching is forbidden in competition.

Check yourself

A shooter comes to the station in sandals for a practice round. Is it allowed?
  1. Yes, in practice only
  2. No: sandals are forbidden in practice and in competition
  3. Yes, with socks
  4. Only with the referee's permission
Correct!

No: sandals are forbidden in practice and in competition — The Compak® dress code bans sandals for safety in practice and in competition.

Squad shooting. A shooter arrives just after the first shooter of his squad has called for the first target. Result?
  1. No penalty
  2. Zero on the first target only
  3. He shoots at the end of the squad
  4. 25 zeros for the round; a same-day written appeal to the jury is possible
Correct!

25 zeros for the round; a same-day written appeal to the jury is possible — The deadline is the squad's first call; after it the late shooter gets 25 zeros unless the jury accepts a valid reason.

After the round a shooter refuses to sign the score sheet because he disagrees with one call. What is the consequence?
  1. He loses the right to make any complaint
  2. The referee must change the result
  3. The round is shot again
  4. Nothing happens
Correct!

He loses the right to make any complaint — Signing the sheet at the end of every round is compulsory; refusing to sign makes any complaint inadmissible.
