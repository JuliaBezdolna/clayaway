FITASC, federations and ClayArena

FITASC is the international federation for hunting-style clay shooting. It writes and updates the rules of Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting; the editions we follow are dated 01/01/2025. Compak® Sporting is also a trade mark protected by FITASC, which is why the name is always written with ®.

The sport is built like a pyramid. Clubs and shooting grounds are at the bottom, national federations above them, and the national federations are members of FITASC. Layouts for national competitions are approved by the national federation, those for international competitions by FITASC. A federation may hand an event to a club, but it stays responsible for applying the rules.

On the ground the rules are applied by referees. Each referee holds a certificate from a national federation, and the chief referee needs an international FITASC certificate. A jury handles protests, penalties and technical problems at international events. You will translate for all of them, so learn the titles: referee, chief referee, jury.

Other bodies exist too. In Britain and the USA national associations run their own versions of the sport, such as English Sporting, with different rules and words. A British or American coach may use those words at a FITASC event; later missions show you how to handle that.

ClayArena is the platform behind this course. It publishes competition calendars, a glossary and a plain-language summary of the FITASC rules, and through ClayAway it runs training tours. Remember: only the official FITASC text is binding; a summary is a guide, not the rule itself.

Key points
  • FITASC writes the rules of Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting (editions of 01/01/2025).
  • Club, national federation, FITASC: national layouts are approved by the federation, international ones by FITASC.
  • Referees hold national certificates; the chief referee holds a FITASC international one; the jury hears protests.
  • Only the official FITASC text is binding; ClayArena offers a summary.

“The jury will decide this one.”
The matter goes beyond the stand referee, to the competition jury.
