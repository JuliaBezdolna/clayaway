Pull-away: on it, with it, pull away, shoot

In pull-away (in Ukrainian sometimes «вихід з мішені»; the pull itself can be called «відрив») the muzzles first join the target, then pull ahead of it. It is widely taught in modern British coaching and is often called the CPSA method today.

How it looks, step by step. 1) “On it”: the gun is mounted onto the target or its front edge; the muzzles touch it on its line. 2) “With it”: the muzzles track the target for a moment at its speed, matching line and pace. 3) “Pull away”: the gun accelerates smoothly ahead until the right lead picture appears. 4) “Shoot”: the trigger is pulled while the gun is still accelerating or moving. 5) Follow-through: the gun keeps going after the shot.

Strengths: tracking the target first lets the shooter judge its speed, distance and line before committing, so the line is good. It suits long targets, quartering targets and rising targets. Because of the acceleration, the perceived lead is smaller than with maintained lead.

Weaknesses: the amount of acceleration varies from target to target, so it takes practice to feel it; tracking for too long becomes “riding the target”, and stopping the push gives a miss behind.

Coaches say “touch it and go”, “mount on it and push out”, or the four-beat “on it, with it, pull away, shoot”. Keep the four-beat rhythm when you translate: it matters more than the exact words.

Key points
  • Muzzles start on the target (or its front edge).
  • Track at its speed, then accelerate ahead.
  • Trigger while the gun is still moving forward; then follow through.
  • Good line control; suits long, quartering and rising targets.
  • Keep the four-beat rhythm when translating.

The coach says

“On it, with it, pull away, shoot.”
Join the target, track it, accelerate ahead, fire.
“Touch it and go.”
Meet the target with the muzzles and immediately start pulling ahead.
“You rode it too long.”
The shooter tracked the target for too long before pulling away and lost the line or the moment.
“Mount on it and push out a bit more.”
Mount onto the target, then pull a little further ahead before firing.

Terms

Pull-away · Insertion point · Gun speed · Perceived lead · Quartering away · Follow-through

Check yourself

In pull-away, what happens just before the trigger is pulled?
  1. The gun stops on the target
  2. The gun accelerates ahead of the target
  3. The gun drops below the target
  4. The gun waits at the break point
Correct!

The gun accelerates ahead of the target — The pull-away itself is the acceleration ahead; the shot is fired while the gun is still moving forward.

The coach says “You rode it too long”. What went wrong?
  1. He stood too long on the stand before calling
  2. He mounted the gun far too early
  3. He tracked it too long before pulling away
  4. The trapper released the target late
Correct!

He tracked it too long before pulling away — “Riding the target” means following it too long, so it slows, drops or escapes and the line is lost.

Compared with maintained lead, the perceived lead in pull-away is usually…
  1. smaller, because the acceleration adds lead
  2. larger, because the gun starts on the target
  3. always exactly double
  4. zero on every target
Correct!

smaller, because the acceleration adds lead — The brief acceleration carries the gun forward during the shot delay, so less gap needs to be seen.
