Your role: training versus competition

In training you are the coach’s voice. Render every instruction completely, in the coach’s order and tone: firm when it is about safety, warm when it is praise. Do not soften criticism, do not add reasons the coach did not give, and never add technique tips of your own, even when you know the answer. If you did not hear a word, ask before you translate.

A competition is different. Coaching a competitor during the competition is forbidden in both Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting: the shooter gets a yellow card, repeats bring further sanctions, and the referee removes the person who was coaching from the shooting area. The referee also decides alone: nobody in the squad comments on hits, and open criticism of a ruling counts as an attempt to influence the referee.

Here is the honest part. The FITASC rulebooks do not mention interpreters at all. Whether passing on a coach’s advice word for word during a competition counts as coaching, and where an interpreter may stand, the rules do not say. It is an open question for FITASC, and you should not present any answer as official.

Until FITASC answers, use the safe practice. In competition interpret only communication with the referee, the jury and the organiser: calls, decisions, warnings, protests, times and squads. Before the event, tell the referee or organiser who you are and agree where you will stand. If a coach asks you to pass on advice during the competition, decline politely and explain why. It protects your shooter from a yellow card.

Key points
  • Training: the coach’s voice — complete, same tone, nothing of your own
  • Competition: coaching is forbidden (yellow card, coach removed)
  • Open FITASC question: rulebooks do not mention interpreters
  • Safe practice: interpret only referee, jury and organiser communication in competition
  • Introduce yourself to the referee and agree where you stand

The coach says

“Tell her she’s lifting her head on the second bird.”
Training: pass it on fully when the gun is open.
“Quick — tell him to give the crosser more.”
Competition: this is coaching. Decline politely; do not pass it on.
“Who are you with?”
The referee asks why you are near the stand; introduce yourself and your role.
“Ask the referee if I can still change chokes.”
A procedural question to the referee: you may interpret it.

Check yourself

During a competition round the coach asks you to tell the shooter “more lead on the crosser”. What do you do?
  1. Whisper it quickly
  2. Translate it after the next target
  3. Politely decline: it would be coaching, which is forbidden
  4. Ask the referee to pass it on
Correct!

Politely decline: it would be coaching, which is forbidden — Coaching during competition is forbidden and the shooter would get a yellow card; the rules do not say whether relaying advice is allowed, so stay safe.

What do the FITASC rulebooks say about interpreters?
  1. They may stand on the stand
  2. They need a referee’s licence
  3. They count as coaches
  4. Nothing: it is an open question
Correct!

Nothing: it is an open question — The rulebooks do not mention interpreters, so their exact position in competition is an open question for FITASC.

In training, the coach says “Not bad, but you stopped the gun.” How do you translate?
  1. Fully and in the same tone, both parts
  2. Only the praise, to keep the guest happy
  3. Only the fault, to save time
  4. Add your own tip on how to fix it
Correct!

Fully and in the same tone, both parts — You neither soften nor inflate: the whole message, in the coach’s tone, with nothing of your own.
