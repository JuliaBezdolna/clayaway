Traps, release and the trapper

A trap («машинка») is the machine that throws targets. Traps can be manual or automatic (the Compak® Sporting rules also name semi-automatic ones). In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the traps of a stand are lettered A, B, C, D from left to right as seen from the stand; in Compak® Sporting the six traps are A to F, each with a letter panel. Keep the Latin letters when you translate: the menu uses them.

The shooter releases the target with a call: the rules name 'pull' or 'ready', though in practice shooters' call words vary. In Sporting™ the referee passes the command to the trapper (in Ukrainian «оператор машинки», the person who operates the release) with a sound signal, and the target must leave within three seconds. Do not translate the call itself: it is a command for the machine, not a sentence.

In Compak® Sporting targets may be released by hand, by remote control or by a voice-activated sonopull. With manual release the target leaves 0 to 3 seconds after the call; a sonopull has a fixed delay of 0.5 seconds. International in-line Compak® Sporting uses only sonopull with electronic refereeing.

A voice release has a microphone at the station. Your own voice can trigger it. On such a stand, never speak aloud while the shooter is ready: whisper between targets or step back.

Trap mistakes are No Birds: a target released more than three seconds after the call, a target from the wrong trap, a target thrown without a call, two targets from traps of the same stand in Sporting™, or a target thrown by mistake by a machine of the same Compak.

Key points
  • Traps: A–D per Sporting™ stand, A–F on a Compak® field; keep Latin letters.
  • The call ('pull', 'ready') releases the target; do not translate it.
  • Sporting™: referee signals the trapper; target within 3 seconds.
  • Compak®: manual 0–3 s, remote, or sonopull with a fixed 0.5 s delay.
  • Late, wrong-trap or uncalled targets are No Birds; a microphone can hear you.

The coach says

“Trap B is throwing lower than in the showing — tell the referee.”
The machine's trajectory has changed; the shooter should report it before shooting.
“It's voice release here — don't chat on the stand.”
A microphone releases targets; talking can launch one.
“The trapper was late on that one — that's a no bird.”
The target left more than three seconds after the call.

Check yourself

In Sporting™, how long may it take for the target to leave after the referee's signal?
  1. Up to 1 second
  2. Up to 3 seconds
  3. Up to 10 seconds
  4. Any time
Correct!

Up to 3 seconds — A target released later than three seconds is a No Bird.

What is the fixed delay of a Compak® sonopull?
  1. 0 seconds
  2. 3 seconds
  3. 0.5 seconds
  4. It is set by each shooter
Correct!

0.5 seconds — The sonopull must apply a fixed, unchangeable delay of 0.5 seconds.

You are on a stand with voice release and the shooter is ready. What do you do?
  1. Translate loudly so that he hears you
  2. Ask the trapper to wait a moment
  3. Repeat the call word for him
  4. Stay silent; whisper only between targets
Correct!

Stay silent; whisper only between targets — Your voice can trigger the microphone and release a target nobody called.
