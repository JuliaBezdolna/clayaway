Singles and pairs: report, rafale, simultaneous

A single is one target from one trap. In both disciplines two shots may be fired at it, so a miss can be saved with the second barrel. A pair, or double, is two targets shot as one unit after one call.

Report pair (double on report): the call releases the first target, and the shot at it releases the second. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the second comes 0 to 3 seconds after that shot. The order is fixed. “Report” means the sound of the shot, not a written report.

Following pair, which FITASC calls rafale: two targets from the same trap on the same line, the second as fast as the trap reloads; either may be shot first. It exists only in Sporting™: a Compak® Sporting double is on report or simultaneous.

Simultaneous pair (“simo”; English Sporting “true pair”): both targets leave at the same moment, from one or two traps (in Compak® Sporting always from two different traps), and may be shot in any order.

Cartridges: in Sporting™ there are two for the pair and both may go at one target; if one shot breaks both, both count. In Compak® Sporting only one cartridge per target is allowed, and both shots at one target score zero-zero; one shot breaking both targets is not simply scored twice either (the Compak® mission explains it).

On a Sporting™ stand the squad shoots the singles first, then the doubles. Simultaneous and rafale doubles are shown before shooting; report pairs are not.

Key points
  • Single: one target, two shots allowed.
  • Report pair: the shot at the first releases the second; order fixed.
  • Rafale (following pair): same trap, same line, Sporting™ only.
  • Simultaneous (true pair): both at once, any order.
  • Sporting™: both cartridges may go at one target; Compak® Sporting: one per target.

The coach says

“It's on report — finish the first one properly, the second waits for your shot.”
The second target only comes when the shooter fires at the first, so there is no need to rush the first.
“Simo: take the rabbit first, the teal will wait for you.”
In the simultaneous pair shoot the rabbit first, because the teal stays in the air longer.
“Rafale — after the first, come straight back to the trap for the second.”
Both targets come from the same trap; after the first shot, return eyes and gun towards the trap to pick up the second.
“True pair from the tower, five of them.”
Five simultaneous pairs thrown from the tower (English Sporting wording).

Check yourself

In a report pair, what releases the second target?
  1. A second call
  2. The referee's signal
  3. The shot at the first target
  4. A timer of exactly 5 seconds
Correct!

The shot at the first target — “On report” means on the sound of the shot: firing at the first target releases the second.

A shooter fires both cartridges at the first target of a simultaneous double in Compak® Sporting. Result?
  1. Allowed, as in Sporting™
  2. Zero-zero
  3. No Bird and repeat
  4. Only the second target is zero
Correct!

Zero-zero — Compak® Sporting allows one cartridge per target of a double; deliberately firing both at one target is scored zero-zero. In Sporting™ it would be allowed.

A British coach says “true pair”. How do you translate it?
  1. A correct, fair pair
  2. A pair of real birds
  3. A rafale
  4. A simultaneous pair
Correct!

A simultaneous pair — “True pair” is the English Sporting name for a simultaneous pair; “true” has nothing to do with correctness.
