British and American words versus FITASC

Many coaches you will work with grew up with English Sporting, governed in England by the CPSA, or with American Sporting Clays under the NSCA. The words are similar to FITASC, but the rules behind them are not, and the biggest difference is the gun position.

In English Sporting and NSCA a shooter may start “gun up”, already mounted, before calling. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ that is not allowed: the stock stays below the line on the vest until the target appears, and every target is shot from the shoulder. Compak® Sporting is in between: the gun position at the call is free. So “pre-mount this one” is a drill in training, but a yellow card at a FITASC Sporting™ competition. Translate it, and if the guest is about to compete, ask the coach whether to mention the rule.

Pairs have different names. A “true pair” (English and American) is a FITASC simultaneous double. A “report pair” is a double on report. A “following pair” is a FITASC rafale. A “show pair” is the viewing of targets before shooting. A “peg” is the shooting stand, a “station” is the American word for a stand, and a “cage” is the frame around it.

Results talk differs too. “HOA”, High Over All, is the overall winner. Classes such as AA, A, B or C are national skill levels from averages, not FITASC age categories, and not the FITASC difficulty classes A, B and C, which grade trajectories. Even within FITASC, pairs differ: in Sporting™ both cartridges may go at one target of a double, in Compak® only one cartridge per target. Always ask yourself which rulebook the speaker has in mind.

Key points
  • Gun up: allowed in English Sporting and NSCA, forbidden in FITASC Sporting™, free in Compak®
  • True pair = simultaneous; report pair = on report; following pair = rafale
  • Peg = shooting spot; station = stand (US); cage = protective frame
  • HOA = overall winner; AA/A/B/C classes are not FITASC categories or difficulty classes
  • Sporting™: both shots at one target allowed; Compak®: one per target

The coach says

“Pre-mount this one for now.”
Training drill: start with the gun in the shoulder. Not allowed in FITASC Sporting™ competition.
“It’s a true pair.”
Both targets leave at the same time (FITASC: simultaneous double).
“Following pair off the left trap.”
Two targets from the same trap one after the other (FITASC: rafale).
“Let’s see a show pair.”
Throw the pair once so everyone can watch it before shooting.
“He’s AA class and won HOA last year.”
A high national skill class, and overall winner of the event.

Check yourself

A British coach says “It’s a true pair.” What is the FITASC term?
  1. A double on report
  2. A rafale double
  3. A simultaneous double
  4. A correct double
Correct!

A simultaneous double — “True pair” is the English and American name for two targets released together, a FITASC simultaneous double.

In which discipline is a “gun up” start NOT allowed?
  1. Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ (FITASC)
  2. Compak® Sporting
  3. English Sporting (CPSA)
  4. Sporting Clays (NSCA)
Correct!

Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ (FITASC) — FITASC Sporting™ requires the stock below the vest line until the target appears; Compak® leaves the gun position free.

A guest reads “Class C” in British results. What is it?
  1. A FITASC age category
  2. A national skill level based on averages
  3. The hardest FITASC trajectory class
  4. A type of cartridge
Correct!

A national skill level based on averages — In British and American results classes are skill levels; FITASC uses A/B/C only for trajectory difficulty and has age categories instead.
