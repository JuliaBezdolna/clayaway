Swing-through: from behind, through the bird

In swing-through the muzzles start behind the target and overtake it. In Ukrainian you may hear «протяжка» or simply “swing-through”.

How it looks, step by step. 1) Start: the muzzles join the target's line behind it, on its tail. 2) Overtaking: the gun moves faster than the target, along its line, through its body. 3) Shot: the trigger is pulled as the muzzles pass the front of the target, just ahead of it. Because there is a small delay between decision and shot and the gun is moving faster than the target, the muzzles are already further ahead when the shot leaves: the actual lead at the shot is bigger than the lead the shooter sees, and little or no gap is seen. 4) The swing continues after the shot. The memory aid is “butt, belly, beak, bang”: tail, body, front, fire. In British English you may hear “bum” instead of “butt”; translate it as «хвіст».

Strengths: natural and fast; good for beginners, for close fast targets, for driven targets and overheads and for erratic or surprise targets.

Weaknesses: a very short window for the trigger; on long crossers the shooter can run out of time, lose the line or overshoot; slowing down as the muzzles reach the target gives a miss behind.

Sources disagree on the name “CPSA method”: some describe it as swing-through, while CPSA coaching today is described as teaching pull-away. If a coach says “CPSA method”, ask which one he means.

Key points
  • Muzzles start behind, on the target's line.
  • Gun faster than the target: tail, body, front, bang.
  • Trigger as the muzzles pass the front; the actual lead is bigger than the lead seen.
  • Good for close, fast, driven and surprise targets; harder on long crossers.
  • “CPSA method” is ambiguous: ask which method is meant.

The coach says

“Come from behind, through the bird, and shoot as you see daylight.”
Start behind the target, swing through it and fire as a gap appears in front.
“Butt, belly, beak, bang.”
Memory aid: tail, body, front of the target, then fire.
“You slowed down as you got to it.”
The gun lost speed when it reached the target, so the shot went behind.
“Brush it out and keep going.”
Swing through the target as if wiping it out of the sky, without stopping.

Check yourself

Where do the muzzles start in swing-through?
  1. In front of the target
  2. On the target's front edge
  3. At a fixed point at the break point
  4. Behind the target, on its line
Correct!

Behind the target, on its line — Swing-through starts behind and overtakes the target; starting in front is maintained lead, a fixed point is ambush.

Why does a swing-through shooter see little or no lead?
  1. The targets are always slower
  2. The gun's speed creates the lead
  3. The rules forbid a visible gap
  4. The shooter closes both eyes
Correct!

The gun's speed creates the lead — The gun moves faster than the target, so between deciding and firing it travels ahead by itself.

A coach says “bum, belly, beak, bang”. How should you translate “bum”?
  1. As «хвіст» (tail)
  2. With a rude word, literally
  3. As «бум» (a sound)
  4. Leave it out completely
Correct!

As «хвіст» (tail) — It means the tail of the target; a neutral «хвіст» keeps the meaning and the rhythm of the aid.
