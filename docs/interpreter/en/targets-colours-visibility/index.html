Colours, light and visibility

Both rulebooks ask the same thing: a target's colour must stand out clearly against the background. Two-colour targets are not recommended. So the colour is a choice by the course setter, made for each trajectory, not a separate target type.

You will hear black, orange, white, yellow and pink targets. Many course setters use black against the sky and orange against trees and grass. A 'blazed' target has a bright orange painted dome. Careful: when a coach says 'the orange one', he means a colour, not a size. A black midi and an orange standard can come from the same stand.

If a target of the wrong colour is thrown, the referee calls No Bird and it is repeated. In Compak® Sporting the rules add that bad weather is never a reason for a No Bird: a broken target counts and a missed one is lost, whatever the conditions. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the referee may pause shooting for a short, violent storm or downpour.

Light changes everything. Coaches talk about backlight (the sun behind the target), flat light on grey days, shadows under trees and the background behind the target: sky, tree line or ground. A target can 'disappear' against dark trees and 'pop out' against the sky. This is why shooters change lens colour during the day.

When you translate, keep colour words and light words exact and short: 'it's coming out of the dark trees', 'you lose it in the sun'. They explain why a shooter missed, so they matter.

Key points
  • A target's colour must stand out against the background; two-colour targets are not recommended.
  • Colour is not type: 'the orange one' is a colour, not a size.
  • Wrong colour = No Bird.
  • Compak®: bad weather never justifies a No Bird; Sporting™ referees may pause for a short storm.
  • Backlight, flat light, background: translate light words exactly.

The coach says

“The black one is the midi, the orange one is the standard — don't mix them up.”
Two targets on the stand differ in colour and size.
“You lose it against the trees — pick it up later, in the sky.”
The target is hard to see in front of trees; start watching where the sky is behind it.
“Flat light today — put the yellow lens in.”
Grey, contrastless light; a yellow lens helps contrast.

What do the rules require of a target's colour?
  1. It must always be orange
  2. It must stand out clearly against the background
  3. It must be two-coloured
  4. Any colour: the rules are silent

It must stand out clearly against the background — Visibility against the background is the requirement; two-colour targets are not recommended.

In Compak® Sporting it starts to rain hard and a shooter misses. Can he ask for a No Bird because of the weather?
  1. Yes, weather always justifies one
  2. Only for the first target of a double
  3. Only if the jury agrees to it
  4. No, weather never justifies a No Bird
Correct!

No, weather never justifies a No Bird — The Compak® Sporting rules state that weather is never a reason for a No Bird.

A coach says 'Watch the orange one.' What is he describing?
  1. The colour of a target
  2. A target size of 90 mm
  3. A rabbit
  4. A broken target
Correct!

The colour of a target — Colour is chosen for visibility and says nothing about size or type.
