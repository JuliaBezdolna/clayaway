Target types: standard, midi, mini, battue, rabbit, flash, ZZ

The FITASC list for Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ names seven target types: standard, midi, super mini, battue, rabbit, flash and ZZ. Compak® Sporting allows any standard or special target, and its list also names the rocket. Each type flies differently.

The standard, the most common target, is a dome about 108–110 mm across. The midi is 90 mm: it keeps its speed well and looks further away than it is. The mini is 60 mm: it leaves the trap fast and slows quickly, so shooters tend to give it too much lead. The Sporting™ list calls the small target 'super mini', and the menu rule names both 70 mm and 60 mm sizes; the names are not used consistently, so always go by the millimetres written on the menu.

The battue is a flat, thin 110 mm target. It flies edge-on, almost invisible, then turns to show its face and drops quickly. The rabbit is a 110 mm target with a thick, strong rim, rolled on its edge along the ground; it bounces and can hop over bumps. In Ukrainian you will hear «кролик» or «заєць»; this course uses «кролик».

A flash target is filled with coloured powder that leaves a bright cloud when it is hit; it is scored like any other target. A ZZ target, also called helice, is a plastic propeller with a white centre cap that flies erratically. The rocket is a special target named only in the Compak® Sporting list; we give no specifications for it.

In Sporting™, a target of the wrong colour, size or type is a No Bird and is repeated.

Key points
  • Sporting™ list: standard, midi, super mini, battue, rabbit, flash, ZZ; Compak® allows any type, rocket included.
  • Standard about 108–110 mm, midi 90 mm, mini 60 mm; the menu may also show 70 mm — go by millimetres.
  • Battue: flat, edge-on, turns and drops; rabbit: thick rim, rolls and hops.
  • Flash leaves a powder cloud; ZZ is a propeller target.
  • Sporting™: wrong colour, size or type = No Bird.

The coach says

“The midi looks far but it isn't — don't give it too much.”
A 90 mm target seems further away than it is, so it needs less lead than the shooter thinks.
“The battue will turn and drop at the end — take it when it shows its belly.”
Shoot the battue once it turns its flat face towards you.
“The mini is dying fast — shoot it early.”
The small target loses speed quickly; do not wait.
“The rabbit will hop on that bump — stay under it.”
Keep the muzzles low on the rolling target so a hop still meets the shot.

Check yourself

Which target rolls along the ground?
  1. Midi
  2. Battue
  3. Rabbit
  4. Flash
Correct!

Rabbit — The rabbit has a thick rim and is thrown on its edge along the ground.

The menu says '70 mm'. The coach calls it a mini. What should you translate?
  1. Correct it to 60 mm, since a mini is 60 mm
  2. Keep the coach's word and the 70 mm on the menu
  3. Call it a midi, since it is over 60 mm
  4. Ask the coach to say 'super mini' instead
Correct!

Keep the coach's word and the 70 mm on the menu — The rules name 70 mm and 60 mm sizes and the name 'super mini'; trust the millimetres on the menu and do not correct the coach.

On a Sporting™ stand, a trap throws a midi instead of the standard shown on the menu. What happens?
  1. No Bird, the target is repeated
  2. It counts as a hit
  3. The shooter loses the target
  4. The round is cancelled
Correct!

No Bird, the target is repeated — In Sporting™ a target of the wrong colour, size or type is a No Bird and is repeated.
