Warnings, cards and penalties

FITASC penalties climb a ladder. By entering a competition a shooter accepts the rules and the sanctions. The first breach of a rule normally brings a warning, shown with a yellow card. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ a classic example is the gun position: a stock above the line on the vest, or a gun mounted before the target appears, earns a verbal warning and a yellow card.

A repeat on the same layout (or the same Compak®) brings a red card, and the target is scored by a table: zero on a single, zero and No Bird on a double on report, zero and zero on a simultaneous or rafale double. The same scale applies to shooting at a declared No Bird and, in Sporting™, to exceeding fifteen seconds between targets. In Compak® Sporting the limit is ten seconds to call.

Coaching during a competition is penalised too: the shooter gets a yellow card, repeats lead to further sanctions, and the referee sends the person who coached out of the shooting area. For an interpreter this is the most important card in the book.

For repeat offences, refusal to comply or attempts to influence a referee, the jury can take away one target, a whole round of 25, or expel the shooter. Throwing a gun, abuse or violence, and deliberately shooting at live animals can mean immediate expulsion. An expulsion is recorded by FITASC for five years and brings no refund. Translate “warning”, “yellow card” and “red card” literally, calmly, and at once.

Key points
  • First breach: warning with a yellow card
  • Repeat on the same layout or Compak®: red card + target scored by the table
  • Coaching in competition: yellow card for the shooter, coach removed
  • Jury scale: one target, one round, or expulsion
  • Expulsion: kept on record 5 years, no refund

The coach says

“Warning — your stock was above the line.”
Sporting™: the gun was too high before the target appeared; next time on this layout it costs the target.
“That’s a yellow card.”
An official first warning; it is recorded.
“Red card — the target is zero.”
A repeat offence: the target is scored as lost.
“You’re over time.”
The shooter took longer than allowed (15 s in Sporting™, 10 s to call in Compak®).
“Sir, please leave the shooting area.”
The referee is removing someone, for example a person caught coaching.

Check yourself

Sporting™: after a yellow card for a mounted gun, the shooter mounts early again on the same layout, on a single. What is the result?
  1. Another warning
  2. Red card and the target is zero
  3. No Bird
  4. Immediate expulsion
Correct!

Red card and the target is zero — After the first warning, a further offence on the same layout brings a red card; on a single the target is zero.

The referee sees a coach giving advice to a shooter during a competition. What do the rules say?
  1. Nothing, coaching is allowed between stands
  2. The coach gets a red card
  3. The shooter gets a yellow card and the coach is removed from the shooting area
  4. The round is cancelled
Correct!

The shooter gets a yellow card and the coach is removed from the shooting area — Both rulebooks give the competitor a yellow card and have the referee send the person coaching out of the shooting area.

Which of these can the jury NOT impose under the penalty scale?
  1. Loss of one target
  2. Loss of a round of 25
  3. Expulsion
  4. A cash fine paid to the referee
Correct!

A cash fine paid to the referee — The jury’s scale is loss of a target, loss of a round, or expulsion; there is no fine to the referee.
