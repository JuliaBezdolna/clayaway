The rabbit: rolling, hopping and bolting

A rabbit is a 110 mm target with a thick, strong rim, thrown on its edge so that it rolls along the ground like a running hare. It is a special target, so in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ it must be shown on the target menu of the stand.

Coaches describe how it runs: a rolling rabbit runs smoothly; a hopping (bouncing) rabbit jumps on bumps and stones; a bolting rabbit bursts out of cover and runs fast. Rabbits can go across, towards or away from the shooter.

A rabbit is heavy and slows quickly, so shooters often over-lead it. Coaches ask to keep the muzzle low and shoot the bottom front edge, so that a sudden hop still meets the shot. Often it is visible only in a short gap between bushes, so the break point must be planned exactly.

Rules: every target, rabbits included, is shot with the gun at the shoulder; firing from the hip is not allowed in either discipline. A special case: if the first shot misses and the rabbit then breaks by itself, for example on a stone, before the second shot, it is a No Bird. On the repeat only a break with the second shot counts.

“Fur and feather” is English Sporting slang for a pair of a rabbit (“fur”) and an aerial target (“feather”). In Ukrainian you will hear «кролик» or «заєць»; this course uses «кролик».

Key points
  • Rabbit: 110 mm, thick rim, rolls on its edge along the ground.
  • Rolling, hopping (bouncing), bolting: describe how it runs.
  • It slows quickly: do not over-lead; keep the muzzle low.
  • Always shot from the shoulder; a rabbit that breaks by itself after a first-shot miss is a No Bird.
  • “Fur and feather” = rabbit + aerial target pair.

The coach says

“It hops on that bump — stay under it and keep moving.”
The rabbit jumps at a known spot; keep the gun low and moving.
“Shoot its feet.”
Put the shot on the bottom edge of the rabbit, not over it.
“It slows down a lot — less lead than you think.”
The rabbit loses speed quickly, so it needs a smaller lead.
“Fur and feather: rabbit first, before it reaches the bushes.”
In the rabbit-plus-bird pair, shoot the rabbit first because it disappears into cover.

Check yourself

The first shot misses a rabbit, then it breaks on a stone before the second shot. What happens?
  1. It is scored as a hit, since it broke
  2. It is scored zero for the first miss
  3. No Bird: repeated, only a second-shot break counts
  4. The shooter just moves to the next target
Correct!

No Bird: repeated, only a second-shot break counts — Both rulebooks treat this as a No Bird; on the repeat the shooter must deliberately miss with the first shot.

Why do coaches warn against too much lead on a rabbit?
  1. The rules limit the lead
  2. It is heavy and slows quickly
  3. It always flies upwards
  4. It is smaller than a mini
Correct!

It is heavy and slows quickly — A rabbit has a thick rim and rolls on the ground, losing speed fast, so the usual crosser lead puts the shot in front.

What does “fur and feather” mean?
  1. A pair of a rabbit and an aerial target
  2. A hunting dress code
  3. A target with feathers glued on
  4. A pair of two rabbits
Correct!

A pair of a rabbit and an aerial target — In English Sporting slang “fur” is the rabbit and “feather” the flying target.
