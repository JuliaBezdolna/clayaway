What the referee says on the stand

A stand has its own short language. The shooter gives the call when ready, usually “Pull!” or “Ready!”. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the referee passes that call to the trapper with a sound signal, and the target must leave within three seconds; a later target is a No Bird. In Compak® Sporting a manual release must also come within three seconds of the call, and a voice release (sonopull) has a fixed half-second delay.

Before anyone shoots, the referee controls the gun. Loading is allowed only on the stand, gun pointing down the range, once the referee has said shooting may start. While targets are being shown, and whenever shooting stops, guns are open and empty and are closed again only on the referee’s word. In Compak® the referee starts the round with “Start” or “Competition”.

After each target the rules know three results: “one” (hit), “zero” (lost) and “No Bird” (the target does not count and is thrown again). Around the world you will also hear “dead”, “good”, “lost” or simply silence for a hit. In Compak® the referee must announce every zero aloud or with a sound signal, so a shooter who disagrees can protest at once.

The shooter has one formal word too: with a raised hand, “Protest!” or “Appeal!”. When you interpret, keep these calls short and identical every time. Agree with your shooter before the round which words you will use, and never add anything of your own between the call and the shot.

Key points
  • The shooter calls “Pull!” or “Ready!”; the target must come within 3 seconds
  • Load only on the stand and only when the referee allows it; guns open during viewing and stops
  • Rule words: one = hit, zero = lost, No Bird = thrown again
  • Compak®: every zero is announced aloud or by a sound signal
  • Protest = raised hand + “Protest!” or “Appeal!”

The coach says

“You may load.”
The referee allows shooting to start: put cartridges in now, gun pointing down the range.
“No bird!”
That target does not count; do not shoot at it. It will be thrown again.
“Lost.”
The referee scored the target as a miss (zero).
“Dead. / Good.”
The target is a hit (one).
“Guns open, please — we’re showing the targets.”
Everyone opens and unloads; the referee will throw the targets for viewing.
“Start. / Competition.”
Compak®: viewing is over, the round counts from now.

Check yourself

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the target leaves five seconds after the referee’s signal. What is the call?
  1. It counts as normal
  2. No Bird
  3. Zero, because the shooter called too early
  4. A warning for the trapper only
Correct!

No Bird — A target must leave within three seconds of the referee’s signal; a later one is a No Bird and is thrown again.

When may a shooter load the gun?
  1. While walking to the stand
  2. Behind the stand while waiting
  3. On the stand, gun pointing down the range, after the referee allows shooting
  4. Whenever the coach says so
Correct!

On the stand, gun pointing down the range, after the referee allows shooting — Loading is allowed only on the stand, pointing down the range, and only once the referee has authorised the start.

Which word starts a formal protest on the stand?
  1. “Protest!” or “Appeal!” with a raised hand
  2. “Pull!”
  3. “No bird!”
  4. “Referee, look!”
Correct!

“Protest!” or “Appeal!” with a raised hand — Both rulebooks name “protest” or “appeal” with a raised hand; “No bird” belongs to the referee alone.
