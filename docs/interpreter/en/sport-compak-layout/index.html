A Compak® Sporting field: five stations, six traps

Compak® Sporting fits on one field. In front of the shooters there is a compulsory rectangle, 35 to 40 m wide and 25 m deep, marked by four corner stakes with flags. In still air every target must fly over it at some point.

Five shooting stations, each a 1 m square, stand in a line with 2 to 5 m between their centres. They are numbered 1 to 5 from left to right («номер» in Ukrainian). Each station has a Firing Angle Limiter, a frame of tubes that stops the gun swinging too far sideways or up and stops the shooter stepping forward.

Six traps, A to F from left to right, each carry a panel with their letter. They may be in front, at the sides, behind the shooters or on a tower; a trap behind the line must be at least 4 m above it. Every Compak has three compulsory targets: a left-to-right crosser, a right-to-left crosser and a going-away target.

A round is 25 targets, five at each station. When a squad of six shoots, five shooters stand on the stations and the sixth waits behind station 1. Each shooter shoots the five targets of the station's menu in turn, starting from station 1. When station 5 has finished, that shooter goes to wait behind station 1, everyone else moves one station to the right, and the waiting shooter steps onto station 1.

Some events shoot Compak® Sporting in line instead: every shooter starts on station 1 and finishes on station 5. Ask the organiser which system is used, because the procedure differs.

Key points
  • A 35–40 m × 25 m rectangle that every target must cross.
  • Five 1 m stations in a line, 2–5 m apart, each with a Firing Angle Limiter.
  • Six traps lettered A to F from left to right; a trap behind the line sits on a tower at least 4 m high.
  • 25 targets per round, five per station; the squad rotates one station to the right.
  • Compak® Sporting can be shot by squad or in line.

The coach says

“Everybody move one to the right. Number five, to the waiting position.”
The squad rotates; the shooter from station 5 goes behind station 1.
“Trap E is the tower — it comes over your head.”
The target from trap E is thrown from a high tower and passes overhead.
“Mind the frame — don't swing past the post.”
Respect the Firing Angle Limiter; do not swing the gun beyond it.

Check yourself

How many traps and stations does one Compak® Sporting field have?
  1. 4 traps, 3 stations
  2. 6 traps, 5 stations
  3. 5 traps, 6 stations
  4. 8 traps, 8 stations
Correct!

6 traps, 5 stations — Traps A to F and stations 1 to 5.

In a squad of six, where is the sixth shooter while the others shoot?
  1. In the clubhouse
  2. Next to the referee at station 5
  3. Behind trap F
  4. Waiting behind station 1
Correct!

Waiting behind station 1 — The waiting shooter stands behind station 1 and steps onto it after each rotation.

What is the Firing Angle Limiter for?
  1. It limits the swing and stops stepping forward
  2. It measures the speed of each target thrown
  3. It holds the cartridges for the whole round
  4. It releases the targets on the shooter's call
Correct!

It limits the swing and stops stepping forward — It is a safety frame at each station limiting sideways and vertical swing.
