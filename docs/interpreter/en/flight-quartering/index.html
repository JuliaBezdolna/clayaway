Quartering targets: away and towards

A quartering target flies at an angle: partly crossing, partly going away or coming in. Quartering away moves away from the shooter at an angle, for example out to the front right. Quartering towards (also “quartering in”) comes towards the shooter at an angle and passes to one side or drops in front. The angle is often around 45°, but anything between a straight target and a full crosser is possible.

The lead depends on the angle: more than for a straight going-away target, less than for a full crosser. A sharp angle needs little lead, an almost crossing one nearly as much as a crosser.

Quartering away gets smaller and slower quickly, so take it promptly. Pull-away suits it well, because the gun stays on the target's line. Quartering towards grows bigger and looks slow, which tempts the shooter to wait; it can then pass too close or drop out of sight.

The same trap gives different presentations from different stands: from one stand a target is a crosser, from the next it is quartering. That is why targets must be read from the exact stand the shooter will use.

In Ukrainian say «коса від стрільця» and «коса на стрільця» (or «навскіс від стрільця / на стрільця»). A literal word built from “quarter” means nothing to a shooter.

Key points
  • Quartering = flying at an angle, between straight and crossing.
  • Quartering away moves off at an angle; quartering towards (in) comes in at an angle.
  • Lead grows with the angle: less than a crosser, more than a straight target.
  • One trap looks different from each stand: read it from your own stand.
  • Ukrainian: «коса від стрільця / на стрільця».

The coach says

“The first one's quartering away to the left — take it quickly before it gets small.”
The first target moves away at an angle to the shooter's left; break it early.
“Quartering in from the right — don't let it get too close.”
The target comes towards the shooter at an angle from the right; do not wait for it.
“Less lead than you think — it's only a slight angle.”
The target is nearly straight, so it needs only a small lead.

Check yourself

A target comes towards the shooter at an angle and passes to the side. What is it called?
  1. Going-away
  2. Quartering towards
  3. Teal
  4. Rafale
Correct!

Quartering towards — Quartering towards (or quartering in) is an angled target coming in; quartering away is the angled target moving off.

How does the lead on a quartering target compare with a full crosser at the same distance and speed?
  1. It is the same as the crosser
  2. It is always zero
  3. It is larger than the crosser
  4. It is smaller, depending on the angle
Correct!

It is smaller, depending on the angle — Only the sideways part of the movement needs sideways lead, so the sharper the angle, the smaller the lead.

Why should a target be read from the exact stand you will shoot from?
  1. One trap can be a crosser from one stand, quartering from another
  2. The rules forbid watching from any other place
  3. Targets change colour from one stand to the next
  4. Only the referee may stand anywhere else
Correct!

One trap can be a crosser from one stand, quartering from another — The angle between the shooter and the target's line changes with the position, so the presentation changes too.
