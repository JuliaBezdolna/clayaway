Chokes: what they do and how coaches talk about them

A choke («чок», дульне звуження) is a slight narrowing of the barrel at the muzzle. It controls how quickly the shot spreads. More constriction keeps the shot together longer, giving a tighter pattern for far targets; less constriction spreads it sooner, giving a wider, more forgiving pattern for close targets.

Most competition guns have interchangeable chokes: short tubes screwed into the muzzle with a choke key. Older guns may have fixed chokes machined into the barrel. Tubes from different makers often do not fit each other, and makers warn never to fire a multichoke gun without its tubes.

Names come from English, from open to tight: cylinder, skeet, improved cylinder, light modified, modified, improved modified, full. Europeans often say them as fractions: quarter («чверть», improved cylinder), half («пів», modified), three-quarter («три чверті», improved modified) and full («повний»). Many Italian guns mark chokes with stars, and there the rule is upside down: more stars means a more open choke.

Coaches say things like 'quarter and half', 'open up the bottom barrel', 'go tighter in the top' or 'change chokes for this stand'. Keep the names exactly as the coach says them; do not convert them into millimetres, which differ between makers.

The rules differ between disciplines. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ chokes may be changed between stands or between the singles and the doubles, if it causes no delay, but never once the shooter has stepped onto the stand. In Compak® Sporting the gun, barrels and chokes may not be changed within a round at all, only between rounds, except after a breakdown.

Key points
  • A choke narrows the muzzle: tighter for far targets, more open for close ones.
  • Interchangeable tubes are screwed in with a choke key; makers warn never to fire without them.
  • Fractions: quarter = improved cylinder, half = modified, three-quarter = improved modified, full.
  • Keep choke names as said; do not convert to millimetres.
  • Sporting™: change between stands, never on the stand; Compak®: no change within a round.

The coach says

“What chokes are you using? — Quarter and half.”
Improved cylinder in one barrel, modified in the other.
“Put quarter in the bottom and half in the top — the second bird is longer.”
More open choke for the first, closer target; tighter for the second, farther one.
“Open up — these are all close.”
Fit more open chokes because the targets are near.
“Check your tubes are tight before the round.”
Make sure the choke tubes have not loosened.

Check yourself

Which choke gives the widest pattern?
  1. Full
  2. Improved modified
  3. Modified
  4. Cylinder
Correct!

Cylinder — Cylinder has no constriction, so the shot spreads soonest.

In Sporting™, a shooter already standing on the stand wants to change a choke. Is it allowed?
  1. Yes, as long as it causes no delay
  2. No, not once he is on the stand
  3. Yes, but only before the doubles
  4. Only with the jury's permission
Correct!

No, not once he is on the stand — Changes are allowed between stands or between singles and doubles, without delay, but never once the shooter has stepped onto the stand.

On an Italian gun, a choke with five stars is…
  1. the most open
  2. the tightest
  3. a broken choke
  4. a rifle barrel
Correct!

the most open — With star markings, more stars mean a more open choke.
