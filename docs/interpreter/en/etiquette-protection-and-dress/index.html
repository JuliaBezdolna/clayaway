Ears, eyes and dress — yours too

Hearing protection is compulsory for everyone on or near a layout: shooters, referees, staff and spectators. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ safety glasses are compulsory, without exception, for shooters, referees, staff and anyone close to a stand. In Compak® Sporting the eyewear rule names competitors, referees and staff. A shooter who comes onto the stand without either is treated as absent.

That includes you. Standing behind the stand, you are “near a layout”. Electronic ear muffs are the interpreter’s best tool: they soften the shot but let voices through, so you hear the coach and the referee. Clear or tinted shooting glasses also protect you from falling target fragments; a cap helps too.

The dress code is about safety and respect. No short shorts: only long Bermuda-type shorts ending no more than 5 cm above the knee. Shirts need at least short sleeves and a collar or a round, T-shirt-style neck; a vest over a bare chest is not allowed. Sandals are banned, and in Compak® this applies in practice as well as in competition. The competitor number is worn on the back, between the shoulders and the waist, fully visible, not folded or cut.

A first breach of dress or number rules brings a warning; if it is not corrected, penalties follow up to exclusion by the jury. At ceremonies team members wear the national team outfit or smart trousers and a blazer, and all prize winners must attend the prize-giving. Dress like a calm professional: closed shoes, neutral clothes, nothing that flaps or shines in the shooter’s eyes.

Key points
  • Ear protection for everyone near a layout, spectators and interpreters included
  • Glasses: Sporting™ — anyone close to a stand; Compak® — competitors, referees, staff
  • Without them a shooter is treated as absent
  • Bermudas max 5 cm above the knee, sleeves, no bare chest under the vest, no sandals
  • Number on the back, fully visible; first breach = warning

The coach says

“Eyes and ears on before you leave the clubhouse.”
Put on glasses and hearing protection before walking out to the layouts.
“Where are your ears?”
“Ears” = hearing protection, not the ears themselves.
“You can’t shoot in sandals here.”
Closed shoes are required for safety.
“Your number’s folded — it has to be fully visible.”
The competitor number on the back must be complete and visible; otherwise a warning.

Check yourself

Your shooter walks onto the stand without safety glasses. How do the rules treat him?
  1. He gets a warning and may shoot
  2. He may shoot if the coach agrees
  3. He loses one target
  4. He is treated as absent
Correct!

He is treated as absent — In both disciplines a shooter without eye or ear protection on the stand is treated as absent.

Which shorts are allowed?
  1. Any sports shorts
  2. Bermuda-type shorts ending no more than 5 cm above the knee
  3. Shorts only in Compak®
  4. No shorts at all
Correct!

Bermuda-type shorts ending no more than 5 cm above the knee — Short shorts are banned; long Bermuda-type shorts no higher than 5 cm above the knee are accepted.

Why are electronic ear muffs a good choice for an interpreter?
  1. They soften the shot but let voices through
  2. They are not required for interpreters
  3. They block all sound completely
  4. They record the coach
Correct!

They soften the shot but let voices through — Hearing protection is compulsory near a layout, and electronic muffs let you hear the coach and referee.
