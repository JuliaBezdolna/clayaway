Around the stand: silence, distance, stillness

A shooter on the stand needs total concentration, from loading to opening the gun after the last shot. The rules put the waiting places close to the stand but say they must not distract the shooter. In practice that means: quiet voices or none, no sudden movement in the shooter’s field of view, guns open, and phones silent and in a pocket. The rules do not mention phones, but every ground expects this courtesy.

Stay behind the shooter, never beside or in front of the stand. In Compak® Sporting a Firing Angle Limiter frame limits the swing of each shooter; in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the stands on many grounds have a cage. Everyone else waits behind it, out of any possible line of the barrels. In Compak® the waiting shooter stands behind station 1, and a shooter who has finished waits without disturbing the next one.

Disturbing a shooter has rule consequences. If the referee sees that a shooter was clearly disturbed, the target is a No Bird and is thrown again. Simulated shooting while a squad member shoots is not allowed. Only the shooter whose turn it is shoots, and only at his or her own target.

For you this lesson is practical. Stop talking when your guest steps onto the stand, even in the middle of a sentence. Do not answer questions while the gun is closed. Speak again only when the gun is open: between targets, between pairs, or after the stand. A missed piece of advice costs less than a pair spoiled by your voice.

Key points
  • Quiet, still, guns open, phones silent behind the shooter
  • Stand behind the stand, cage or F.A.L., never beside or in front
  • A clearly disturbed shooter gets a No Bird
  • No simulated shooting while someone else shoots
  • Interpreter: speak only when the gun is open

The coach says

“Quiet behind, please — she’s on.”
The shooter is on the stand; everyone stops talking.
“Wait until he’s opened his gun.”
Nobody talks or moves until the shooter has finished and opened the gun.
“Stand right behind me so you can see what I see.”
In training the coach wants you directly behind, out of the swing.
“Keep out of his line, please.”
You are in the shooter’s view or near the barrels’ path; move back.

Check yourself

Where does the interpreter stand while the guest is shooting?
  1. Beside the shooter, to see his face
  2. In front of the stand, to see the target
  3. Behind the stand, out of any line of the barrels
  4. Wherever the referee cannot see
Correct!

Behind the stand, out of any line of the barrels — Everyone waits behind the stand or cage; beside the shooter is the most dangerous place.

A loud phone call behind the stand clearly disturbs the shooter at the moment of the shot. What may the referee decide?
  1. No Bird: the target is thrown again
  2. Zero for the shooter
  3. Nothing, phones are not in the rules
  4. Yellow card for the shooter
Correct!

No Bird: the target is thrown again — If the referee sees the shooter was clearly disturbed, the target is a No Bird.

When is the right moment to pass on the coach’s comment?
  1. While the shooter is calling
  2. Between the call and the shot
  3. Whenever the coach finishes speaking
  4. When the gun is open: between targets, pairs or after the stand
Correct!

When the gun is open: between targets, pairs or after the stand — The shooter’s routine must not be interrupted; speak only once the gun is open.
