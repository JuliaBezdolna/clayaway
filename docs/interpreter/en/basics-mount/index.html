Ready position and gun mount

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the start is gun-down. Before calling, the heel of the stock touches the body and the top rear of the stock stays below a line on the vest, 25 cm under the shoulder axis, in a contrasting colour. The shooter holds this until the target is released and visible. Mounting earlier earns a verbal warning with a yellow card; a repeat on the same layout brings a red card and the target is scored zero.

In Compak® Sporting the gun position at the call is free, so the gun may already be shouldered. In both disciplines every target is shot with the gun at the shoulder, and in Sporting™ the gun position is free between the two targets of a double.

The gun mount (Ukrainian «вкладка») brings the gun to cheek and shoulder. Coaches teach “muzzles first”: the front hand starts the muzzles moving along the target's line, the stock rises to the cheek and settles into the shoulder pocket as the gun catches the line. “Move, mount, shoot” is one smooth flow. The gun comes to the face, not the face to the gun; the cheek stays on the comb, because the eye is the rear sight.

A British coach may say “gun up” or “pre-mount this one”. That is illegal in a FITASC Sporting™ competition, normal in Compak® Sporting, and only a drill in training. Mounting and swinging along an imagined line before calling (mock shooting) is forbidden in Sporting™: a warning first, then a zero.

Key points
  • Sporting™: stock below the vest line (25 cm under the shoulder axis) until the target is visible.
  • Early mount: yellow card, then red card and a lost target on the same layout.
  • Compak® Sporting: free gun position at the call; always shoot from the shoulder.
  • Muzzles first, gun to face, cheek on the comb: move, mount, shoot.
  • “Pre-mount” is a training drill, never in a FITASC Sporting™ competition.

The coach says

“Gun to your face, not your face to the gun.”
Lift the stock to the cheek; do not drop the head to meet the gun.
“Muzzles first — move, mount, shoot.”
Start the barrels moving with the target, mount during the move, fire as the mount is complete.
“Your mount's late and too fast — start it with the front hand.”
The gun comes up too late and with a jerk; the front hand should lead the movement earlier.
“Low gun on this one, like in competition.”
Start with the stock below the vest line, as the FITASC Sporting™ rules require.

Check yourself

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™, until when must the stock stay below the vest line?
  1. Until the shooter has called
  2. Until the referee gives a nod
  3. Until the target is out and visible
  4. Until the first shot is fired
Correct!

Until the target is out and visible — The ready position is held until the target has been released and can be seen; only then may the gun be mounted.

In Compak® Sporting, may a shooter call for the target with the gun already at the shoulder?
  1. No: it earns a yellow card
  2. Only for rabbits and other low targets
  3. Only between the two targets of a double
  4. Yes: the gun position at the call is free
Correct!

Yes: the gun position at the call is free — Compak® Sporting has no low-gun rule: the gun may be mounted or not at the call, but every target is shot from the shoulder.

Why do coaches insist on the cheek staying on the comb?
  1. The eye is the rear sight, so it must sit in one place
  2. To reduce the noise reaching the ear
  3. Because the rules of both disciplines require it
  4. To hide the face from the referee
Correct!

The eye is the rear sight, so it must sit in one place — A shotgun has no rear sight: the eye over the rib is the sight, so a lifted cheek sends the shot high.
