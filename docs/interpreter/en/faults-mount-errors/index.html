Mount errors: late, face to the gun, canting

Many misses start before the swing, in the gun mount: the movement that brings the gun to cheek and shoulder. The coach's words for it are short and physical.

Late mount: the gun reaches the shoulder after the moment the shot should have been fired, so the shooter rushes or fires before the cheek is down. The opposite fault is a mount finished too early, after which the gun waits, still and heavy. Cure: 'move, mount, shoot', where the muzzles start with the target and the mount is completed as the shot comes.

Face to the gun: instead of bringing the stock up to the face, the shooter drops the head to meet it. The eye ends up in a different place every time, and recoil hits the cheek. Coaches say: 'bring the gun to your face, not your face to the gun'. A stock landing on the arm instead of the shoulder pocket hurts too.

Canting: the gun is tilted sideways at the shot, and the shot drifts off the line. It often comes with a dropped shoulder. Cure: 'keep the gun level', 'turn from the hips'.

In FITASC Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ competitions the gun may not be mounted before the target appears, so 'pre-mount this one' is a training drill only. Mounting early in a Sporting™ competition brings a warning and a yellow card. In Compak® Sporting the gun may already be at the shoulder at the call. Translate the drill as a drill and add nothing of your own.

Key points
  • Late mount: a rushed shot; cure: move, mount, shoot.
  • Face to the gun: the eye moves and recoil hurts; bring the gun to the face.
  • Canting: the shot drifts off the line; keep the gun level, turn from the hips.
  • Pre-mounting is a drill: not allowed before the target appears in a Sporting™ competition, allowed at the call in Compak® Sporting.

The coach says

“Bring the gun to your face, not your face to the gun.”
Lift the stock to the cheek; do not lower the head to the stock.
“Move, mount, shoot, all in one.”
Start moving with the target, finish the mount as you fire, in one flowing action.
“You canted the gun, keep it level.”
The gun was tilted sideways at the shot.
“Mount on the line.”
The gun should come up already on the target's path.
“Pre-mount this one, just for practice.”
Training drill with the gun already at the shoulder; not allowed in a Sporting™ competition.

Check yourself

In training, a coach tells a shooter preparing for a FITASC Sporting™ competition: 'Pre-mount this one.' What is true?
  1. It is allowed in every FITASC competition
  2. The interpreter should refuse to translate it
  3. It is a training drill; in a Sporting™ competition the gun may not be mounted before the target appears
  4. It means mounting after the shot
Correct!

It is a training drill; in a Sporting™ competition the gun may not be mounted before the target appears — Sporting™ requires the gun-down ready position until the target appears; a pre-mount in competition earns a warning with a yellow card.

'You canted the gun' means…
  1. The gun was tilted sideways at the shot
  2. The gun was mounted too late
  3. The gun stopped
  4. The safety catch was on
Correct!

The gun was tilted sideways at the shot — Canting is a sideways tilt of the gun, which pulls the shot off the line.

Which instruction cures 'face to the gun'?
  1. Push the head down harder
  2. Bring the gun to your face
  3. Close one eye
  4. Hold the gun lower on the arm
Correct!

Bring the gun to your face — The stock should come up to the face, so the eye is in the same place every time.
