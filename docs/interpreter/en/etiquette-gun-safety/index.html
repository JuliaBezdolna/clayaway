Gun safety: open, empty, muzzles safe

Every gun is treated as dangerous, even when it is empty. Off the stand it is carried open: an over-and-under broken over the forearm, a semi-automatic with the breech open, muzzles straight up or straight down. Slings are not allowed. When nobody is using it, the gun stands upright in a gun rack. Nobody touches another shooter’s gun without permission.

Cartridges go into the gun only on the stand, with the muzzles pointing down the range, and only when the referee or instructor allows it. A semi-automatic holds no more than two. Before turning round on the stand, the shooter opens the gun and takes out every cartridge, fired or not. While targets are being shown, and during any stop, guns stay open.

If the gun fails to fire, the shooter stays in place, gun pointing down the range, and does not open it or touch the safety until the referee has checked it. This is a safety rule and a scoring rule at once: opening early costs the target. Mounting and swinging along an imagined target off the stand, or while someone else is shooting, is not allowed either, and guns are tested only at a test stand, never on the competition stand.

When anyone shouts “Stop!” or “Cease fire!”, everyone stops at once and opens the gun. Do not translate this into a sentence: repeat “Stop!” loudly in your guests’ language and check that their guns are open. If a guest turns towards you with a gun to ask something, point the muzzles down with a gesture first and translate afterwards.

Key points
  • Off the stand: gun open, empty, muzzles up or down, no slings
  • Load only on the stand, pointing down the range, with permission; semi-auto max 2
  • Open and unload before turning round; guns open during viewing and stops
  • After a misfire: stay put, do not open, wait for the referee
  • “Stop!” is repeated, not explained

The coach says

“Guns open and empty between stands, please.”
Walking to the next stand, the gun is broken with no cartridges inside.
“Break your gun and show me it’s clear.”
Open the gun (not damage it!) so I can see the chambers are empty.
“Don’t load until you’re on the stand and I say so.”
No cartridges in the gun while walking or waiting.
“Muzzles down when you turn round.”
Never let the barrels point at people, even with an open gun.
“Stop! Stop! Guns open!”
Emergency stop: stop shooting, open the gun, wait.

Check yourself

How is a semi-automatic carried between stands?
  1. Closed, safety on
  2. With the breech open, muzzle straight up or down
  3. On a sling over the shoulder
  4. Loaded with one cartridge
Correct!

With the breech open, muzzle straight up or down — Guns are carried open, semi-automatics with the breech open, muzzles straight up or down; slings are not allowed.

The gun clicks but does not fire. What must the shooter do?
  1. Open it at once and look
  2. Turn round to show the coach
  3. Stay in place, gun down the range, not open it or touch the safety until the referee checks
  4. Put the safety on and call again
Correct!

Stay in place, gun down the range, not open it or touch the safety until the referee checks — Both rulebooks require the shooter to stay put until the referee inspects; opening or touching the safety first scores zero.

Someone shouts “Cease fire!”. What is the interpreter’s first job?
  1. Repeat “Stop!” loudly and check that guests open their guns
  2. Explain why shooting stopped
  3. Ask the coach what happened
  4. Wait until the guest asks
Correct!

Repeat “Stop!” loudly and check that guests open their guns — An emergency stop is repeated at once; explanations come only when every gun is open.
