False friends on the shooting ground

The worst trap is smoke. A coach’s “Smoked it!” (or “inked it”, “powdered it”) means a perfect hit: the target vanished in a cloud. But in the rules a target that gives off only dust or smoke, with no visible piece, is a zero. A “chip”, a small visible piece, is a hit. So translate the coach’s “smoked it” as «розбили вщент», and the referee’s “only dust” as «лише пил — нуль».

Scoring words are not literal. “Dead” and “kill” mean a hit («є», «збита»), never «мертва» or «вбивство». “Lost” is a miss («промах», «нуль»), not «загублена». “Dropped two” means two targets missed. “Straight” in “25 straight” means a perfect run, not «прямо».

Equipment and place words. A “trap” is the throwing machine («машинка»), not «пастка» (Trap is also an Olympic discipline). A “stand” is one position, not «стенд»; a “peg” is the marked spot, not a stake; a “broken gun” is an open gun. “Pull!” is the call, “pull-away” a lead method, “length of pull” a stock size. A “card” is a score card or a yellow or red card. “On report” means at the sound of the shot. A “squad” is the FITASC group of up to six; in ClayAway it also names a sub-group of a tour.

Other languages add traps. French “chandelle” is a near-vertical target (an English teal), not the English chandelle. French “en battue” and Italian “in battuta” mean driven game, not the battue target; Portuguese “batida” is the battue target, “de batida” means driven. For a hit a French referee says “Bon”, an Italian “Buono”; the trapper is a “pulleur” or “puller”. Italian rules use different words for a squad: batteria in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™, squadra di tiro in Compak®.

Key points
  • Coach “smoked it” = perfect hit; rules “only dust or smoke” = zero
  • Dead / kill = hit; lost / dropped = miss; straight = perfect run
  • Trap = machine; stand = one position; peg = the spot; broken gun = open gun
  • Pull, card, report and squad each have more than one meaning
  • French chandelle and “en battue” differ from the English words

The coach says

“Smoked it!”
Perfect hit, the target turned to dust. Not the rules’ “smoke only”.
“Only dust — zero.”
Referee: no visible piece came off, so it is a miss.
“Just a chip, but it’s dead.”
A small visible piece broke off: it counts as a hit.
“Break your gun.”
Open the gun. Nothing to do with breaking it.
“Step into the peg.”
Stand on the marked shooting spot.
“The second one’s on report.”
The second target is released by the sound of your first shot.

Check yourself

The coach shouts “Smoked it!” after a shot. What happened?
  1. Only smoke came off: a zero
  2. The target was destroyed: a perfect hit
  3. The cartridge misfired
  4. The target was a flash target
Correct!

The target was destroyed: a perfect hit — In coach slang “smoked it” is a perfect hit; in the rules “only dust or smoke” is a zero — opposite meanings.

Best Ukrainian for the referee’s “Lost.”?
  1. «Загублена»
  2. «Втрачено час»
  3. «Промах»
  4. «Мішень зникла»
Correct!

«Промах» — “Lost” is the scoring word for a miss (zero), not something lost.

A French guest says he loves shooting “en battue”. What does he mean?
  1. Driven game (birds driven over the guns)
  2. The flat battue target
  3. A target thrown in a pair
  4. A broken target
Correct!

Driven game (birds driven over the guns) — French “en battue” (Italian “in battuta”) means driven game; the battue target is a separate thing.
