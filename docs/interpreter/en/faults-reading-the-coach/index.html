Reading the coach: short commands and signals

On the stand a coach speaks in fragments: 'Behind.' 'Head.' 'More.' 'Again.' There are only a few seconds between targets, so short is deliberate. Your translation should be just as short; a long sentence arrives after the next call.

Many coaches ask before they tell: 'Where did it go?' or 'What did you see?' This is a teaching question, not a test of the interpreter. Translate the question, let the shooter answer, and translate the answer back exactly, even if it sounds wrong. The coach needs to know what the shooter perceived.

Hand signals carry a lot. Common ones: hands moving apart or together for more or less lead, a flat hand moving down for 'lower', a tap on the cheek for 'head down', a finger tracing the target's line, a thumbs-up for 'same again'. Signals differ between coaches, so agree on them before the session, and do not translate a gesture the shooter has already seen.

Some words are pure rhythm and encouragement: 'commit', 'trust it', 'smooth', 'good, same again'. Keep the tone. 'Bad luck' after a miss is kindness, not a comment on the referee.

In competition coaching is forbidden: the referee gives the competitor a yellow card and sends the coach out of the shooting area. The rules do not say whether an interpreter may relay a coach's advice during a competition: it is an open question for FITASC. The safe practice: in competition, interpret only for the referee, the jury and the organiser.

Key points
  • Coaches speak in fragments; translate just as briefly.
  • 'Where did it go?' is a teaching question: translate the shooter's answer exactly.
  • Agree on hand signals before the session.
  • No coaching in competition; interpret only for the referee, jury and organiser.

The coach says

“Where did it go?”
The coach asks where the shooter thinks the shot went; a teaching question.
“What did you see?”
Describe the picture at the moment of the shot.
“More.”
In context almost always 'more lead'.
“Good, same again.”
Repeat exactly what you just did.
“Trust it, commit to it.”
Stop hesitating; decide and fire.
“Bad luck.”
Sympathy after a miss; not a complaint about the referee.

Check yourself

The coach asks the shooter 'Where did it go?'. What should the interpreter do?
  1. Answer for the shooter
  2. Translate the question, then the shooter's answer exactly
  3. Explain the miss in your own words
  4. Say nothing: it is a rhetorical question
Correct!

Translate the question, then the shooter's answer exactly — The coach wants the shooter's own perception, so the answer must reach him unchanged.

During a competition the shooter's coach asks you to pass on: 'Give the next one more lead.' What is the safe practice?
  1. Pass it on quietly
  2. Pass it on only between stands
  3. Do not relay coaching in competition; interpret only for the referee, jury and organiser
  4. Ask the other shooters first
Correct!

Do not relay coaching in competition; interpret only for the referee, jury and organiser — Coaching in competition is forbidden, and the rules do not say whether an interpreter may relay it; staying out of it protects the shooter.

What happens under the rules if the referee judges that a competitor is being coached?
  1. Yellow card for the competitor; the coach is sent out of the shooting area
  2. Nothing: coaching is allowed
  3. The target is repeated
  4. The whole squad is disqualified
Correct!

Yellow card for the competitor; the coach is sent out of the shooting area — Both rulebooks give the competitor a yellow card, with heavier penalties for repeats, and remove the coach.
