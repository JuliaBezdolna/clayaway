Pairs: the second target

On a pair (double) many shooters break the first target and miss the second. The coach then talks about transition, moving from the first target to the second, and recovery, getting gun and head back under control after the first shot.

The golden rule is 'eyes first, then the gun'. The eyes leave the broken first clay and find the second; the gun follows. A shooter who watches the first clay break, or swings the gun before finding the second, loses time. Coaches say 'eyes to the second' or simply point.

Recovery: recoil lifts the muzzles and sometimes the head. 'Let the gun recover, then go' means: cheek back down and gun back on line before going after the second target. A good stance with the weight forward makes recovery faster.

Planning: while reading the pair, the coach decides which target to take first and where to break it so the second is easy to pick up. Typical phrases: 'take the dropping one first', 'break the first early', 'stick to the plan'. A shooter who changes the order on the stand 'went for the wrong one'.

Rules matter here. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the gun position is free between the two targets of any double, and the shooter has two cartridges for a double, both of which may go at the same target. In Compak® Sporting only one cartridge per target is allowed in a double. Do not explain penalties yourself: translate the coach and leave rule questions to the referee.

Key points
  • Eyes first, then the gun.
  • Recovery: cheek back on the stock and gun on line before the second target.
  • The coach plans the order and the break points of a pair while reading it.
  • Sporting™: free gun position between the targets, and both cartridges may go at one target; Compak® Sporting: one cartridge per target.

The coach says

“Eyes first to the second bird, then the gun.”
Find the second target with the eyes before moving the gun.
“Let the gun recover, then go to the second.”
After the recoil, settle gun and cheek before the second shot.
“Take the dropping one first.”
Shoot first the target that will disappear or fall soonest.
“Don't watch the first one break.”
Move the eyes on to the second target at once.
“You went for the wrong one first, stick to the plan.”
The shooter changed the planned order; a technique remark, not a rule breach.

Check yourself

What does 'eyes first, then the gun' mean on a pair?
  1. Close the eyes after the first shot
  2. Look at the barrel before the second shot
  3. Mount the gun before calling for the pair
  4. Find the second target with the eyes before moving the gun to it
Correct!

Find the second target with the eyes before moving the gun to it — In a good transition the eyes lead and the gun follows.

In a Compak® Sporting double, the shooter misses the first target and wants to fire the second cartridge at it too. Is that allowed?
  1. Yes, as in Sporting™
  2. Yes, but only on a simultaneous double
  3. No: in a Compak® Sporting double only one cartridge per target is allowed
  4. Only with the referee's permission
Correct!

No: in a Compak® Sporting double only one cartridge per target is allowed — Compak® Sporting forbids doubling the first target in a double; Sporting™ allows both cartridges at one target.

In a Sporting™ double, may the shooter lower the gun between the first and the second target?
  1. Yes, the gun position is free between the two targets
  2. No, the gun must stay mounted
  3. Only on a double on report
  4. Only if the referee allows it
Correct!

Yes, the gun position is free between the two targets — The free position applies between the targets of any double, but not before the first one.
