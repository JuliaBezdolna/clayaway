Cartridges: gauge, shot size and the limits

A cartridge («патрон») holds everything for one shot: the hull («гільза») with a metal head, the primer («капсуль»), powder («порох»), the wad («пиж») and the shot («дріб»). Shooters also say shell. 'Empties' are fired hulls.

Gauge («калібр») is counted the old way: the smaller the number, the wider the bore, so 12 gauge is wider than 20. FITASC allows guns no larger than 12 gauge, and most shooters use 12. Never translate '12 gauge' as «12 мм».

Shot size is also upside down: the bigger the number, the smaller the pellet, so No. 9 is finer than No. 7½. The same number can mean slightly different sizes in Britain, continental Europe and the USA, so say the number as given and check the millimetres on the box. 'Nines' and 'seven-and-a-halfs' are common coach words.

The rules for Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting are the same: at most 28 g of lead shot, with a +2% tolerance; round pellets of 2.0 to 2.5 mm (±0.1 mm). Reloaded cartridges, dispersers, mixed shot sizes, black powder and tracers are forbidden. In Sporting™ the referee may take two cartridges to check, one from each vest pocket; in Compak® Sporting, shots fired with non-compliant cartridges before an inspection count as zero.

Some grounds add their own environmental rules for practice, for example fibre wads only or steel shot only: check the ground's rules before the tour. Empty cases go into the bin on the stand, and shooters with semi-automatics pick up their ejected cases when they leave.

Key points
  • Cartridge = hull, primer, powder, wad, shot.
  • Gauge: smaller number, wider bore; FITASC maximum is 12 gauge.
  • Shot size: bigger number, smaller pellet; numbering differs by country, check millimetres.
  • Limits: 28 g lead (+2%), pellets 2.0–2.5 mm; no reloads, mixed shot, black powder or tracers.
  • Empties go in the bin; semi-auto users pick up their cases.

The coach says

“Nines for the close stuff, seven-and-a-halfs for the long crossers.”
Finer No. 9 shot for near targets, larger No. 7½ for far crossing targets.
“Maximum twenty-eight grams on this shoot.”
The load limit of the competition, as in the FITASC rules.
“Empties in the bin, please, not on the grass.”
Put fired hulls into the container.
“Steel only on this ground.”
Lead shot is not allowed on this ground (a local rule, not a FITASC one).

Check yourself

Which is the finer shot?
  1. No. 7
  2. No. 7½
  3. No. 8
  4. No. 9
Correct!

No. 9 — The bigger the shot number, the smaller the pellet.

What is the maximum shot load under FITASC rules?
  1. 24 g, with no tolerance
  2. 28 g, with a +2% tolerance
  3. 32 g for long targets only
  4. No limit, any load is allowed
Correct!

28 g, with a +2% tolerance — Both disciplines allow at most 28 g of lead shot, +2%.

A client says his gun is '12 gauge'. What does that mean?
  1. The bore size: wider than 20 gauge
  2. The bore is 12 millimetres across
  3. Twelve cartridges fit in the magazine
  4. The barrels are 12 inches long
Correct!

The bore size: wider than 20 gauge — Gauge is an old unit: the smaller the number, the wider the bore. It is not millimetres.
