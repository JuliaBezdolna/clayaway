Teal, chandelle, looper and dropping targets

These presentations are about height and curve rather than angle. Their names are course-setters' words, not rulebook terms: the rules list target types, and the Compak® Sporting rules ask for varied trajectories, such as rising, dropping and lofted ones.

A teal (springing or rising teal, after the small duck) is thrown almost straight up, slows, stalls at the top and falls back. It is taken either on the way up, swinging up through it from below, or at the peak while it stalls. Poking at it at the top is the classic fault. Some setters use 'springing' for a target that climbs at an angle rather than straight up, like a flushed bird.

A chandelle (French for “candle”) rises steeply on a slight curve, arcs over and drops almost vertically. It is easiest on the way up; on the curl the lead goes to the side and below.

A looper flies a wide arc across the sky, like a rainbow: it climbs, rolls over the top and falls. It is usually a standard target; some setters also throw a battue on such a line, and it then shows its face as it turns. The gun follows the curve, in front and underneath.

A dropping target is any target in the falling part of its flight. The lead is partly vertical: underneath and in front, with the gun moving.

Careful: names vary between countries. Many French speakers use “chandelle” for a target that goes almost straight up, which an English coach calls a teal, and Ukrainian shooters may say «свічка» for either. Whenever you hear chandelle, candle or «свічка», ask: straight up or arcing over?

Key points
  • Teal: almost vertical up, stalls, falls; take it rising or at the peak.
  • Chandelle: steep curving climb, arcs over, drops almost vertically.
  • Looper: wide rainbow arc across, usually a standard target that rolls over the top and falls.
  • Dropping: lead underneath and in front, never stop the gun.
  • Names vary: French speakers often say “chandelle” for a teal; «свічка» is ambiguous too, so clarify.

The coach says

“Take the teal on the way up — keep the barrels moving above it.”
Break the rising teal before the peak; the gun keeps rising past it.
“Don't poke at it on the top.”
Do not stop and aim at the target while it stalls at the peak.
“The looper's turning over — underneath and in front.”
The target is rolling over the top of its arc and starting to fall; the lead goes below and ahead of it.
“Second bird's dropping — go under it and keep the gun moving.”
The second target of the pair is falling; shoot below it with the gun in motion.

Check yourself

Which target is thrown almost straight up, stalls at the top and falls back?
  1. Looper
  2. Quartering away
  3. Rabbit
  4. Teal
Correct!

Teal — That is the teal (springing or rising teal). A looper flies a wide arc across the sky.

Where does the lead usually go on a dropping target?
  1. Above and behind
  2. Underneath and in front
  3. Straight at the target
  4. Only to the side, never down
Correct!

Underneath and in front — A falling target moves down as well as along, so part of the lead is vertical: below and ahead.

A French-speaking coach says “chandelle”. What is the safest thing to do?
  1. Check: straight up, or an arc over the top?
  2. Always translate it as the English looper
  3. Always translate it as the English teal
  4. Ignore it, since it is not a rulebook term
Correct!

Check: straight up, or an arc over the top? — French speakers often mean an almost vertical target (an English teal), while an English setter’s chandelle climbs on a curve and arcs over; a quick check avoids a wrong plan.
