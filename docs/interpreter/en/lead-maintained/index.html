Maintained lead: get in front, stay in front

Maintained lead (US: sustained lead; Ukrainian «постійний винос») keeps the muzzles in front of the target from the start to the shot.

How it looks, step by step on a crosser. 1) Start: the muzzles wait at a hold point well out along the line, ahead of where the target will pass; the eyes look back towards the pick-up point. 2) Insertion: as the target comes, the gun moves and the muzzles join the line in front of it, never behind; this is the insertion point. 3) Tracking: the muzzles move at the target's speed, so the gap stays the same. 4) Shot: the trigger is pulled while the gap is right and the gun is still moving. 5) The gun keeps moving after the shot.

Strengths: precise, little gun movement, quick between targets, and the lead is easy to adjust. It suits consistent, predictable targets: long crossers, steady high targets.

Weaknesses: the hardest method to learn; the shooter must know the target's speed and line in advance; and because the gun is not faster than the target, the shooter sees the biggest gap and is tempted to measure it, slow down and stop.

Coaches say “insert the gun in front”, “stay ahead of it”, “match its speed”.

Key points
  • Muzzles start ahead and join the line in front of the target.
  • Gun speed = target speed; the gap stays constant.
  • Trigger while the gap is right and the gun is moving; then follow through.
  • Best on long, predictable targets.
  • Main risk: measuring the gap and stopping the gun.

The coach says

“Get in front and stay in front.”
Put the muzzles ahead of the target and keep them there until after the shot.
“Your insertion point is behind the bird — put the gun in front of it.”
The muzzles joined the line behind the target; they must join ahead of it.
“Match its speed — don't let it catch you.”
Move the gun at the target's speed so the gap does not shrink.
“You're measuring it — trust the picture and pull.”
The shooter is slowing down to check the gap; fire as soon as the picture is right.

Terms

Maintained lead · Insertion point · Hold point · Gun speed · Follow-through · Crosser

Check yourself

In maintained lead, where do the muzzles join the target's line?
  1. Behind the target
  2. On the target
  3. In front of the target
  4. Below the trap
Correct!

In front of the target — The muzzles are inserted ahead of the target and stay ahead; behind is swing-through, on the target is pull-away.

What is the typical fault of maintained-lead shooters?
  1. Starting too far behind
  2. Measuring the gap and stopping the gun
  3. Mounting from the hip
  4. Shooting too early at the trap
Correct!

Measuring the gap and stopping the gun — The gap is large and visible, so shooters are tempted to check it; the gun slows or stops and the shot goes behind.

For which target is maintained lead often recommended?
  1. A long, predictable crosser
  2. A bouncing rabbit in a short gap
  3. A surprise target from behind
  4. A ZZ target
Correct!

A long, predictable crosser — The method needs a known speed and line, which long, steady crossers give; erratic or surprise targets suit other methods.
