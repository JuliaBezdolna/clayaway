Manners in a squad

A squad is up to six shooters drawn by lot who shoot together. Punctuality is the first courtesy and also a rule. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ a missing shooter is called three times within one minute, and targets not shot are scored zero. In Compak® Sporting a shooter who arrives after the first shooter of the squad has called gets 25 zeros for the round, and reporting to the referee beforehand is compulsory.

Order matters. In Sporting™ the order rotates at each stand and for the doubles; in Compak® shooters move one station to the right after each series. Nobody steps onto the stand until the previous shooter has left it. When called, a shooter is ready at once, with enough cartridges for the whole round.

The referee decides alone. Squad members do not comment on whether a target was hit unless the referee asks them. Open criticism of a ruling, from shooters or spectators, counts as an attempt to influence the referee. Losing self-control, such as throwing a gun or abusing anyone, goes to the jury and can mean expulsion.

Beyond the rules there are customs. Shooters usually greet the referee and squad at the start, say “good shot” or “well shot” after a good stand, and “bad luck” after a miss, but never comment on a stranger’s technique. A word of thanks to the trapper and referee at the end, a handshake, and calm when results go up are common on many grounds. Translate these courtesies warmly; they matter.

Key points
  • Be on time and report to the referee; lateness costs zeros
  • Follow the rotation; step up only when the stand is free
  • Only the referee rules; no comments on hits
  • Losing self-control can mean expulsion
  • Customs: greet, “well shot”, thank the trapper and referee

The coach says

“Number twelve — last call!”
The referee is calling a missing shooter; if he does not come, his targets will be zeros.
“You’re next — get ready.”
Prepare now: glasses, ears, cartridges, but do not step up until the stand is free.
“Well shot!”
Sincere praise after a good target or stand.
“Bad luck.”
Friendly sympathy after a miss, not criticism.
“Thanks, ref — thanks for pulling.”
Thanking the referee and the trapper at the end of the round.

Check yourself

Compak® Sporting, squad shooting: your guest arrives after the first shooter of his squad has called for the first target. What happens?
  1. He joins at the next station
  2. He gets 25 zeros for that round
  3. He shoots last without penalty
  4. The round is restarted
Correct!

He gets 25 zeros for that round — In Compak® squad shooting the deadline is the first call of the squad; after it the late shooter scores 25 zeros (an appeal to the jury is possible).

A squad-mate loudly tells the referee he is wrong about a hit. How do the rules see this?
  1. As helpful information
  2. As a formal protest
  3. As an attempt to influence the referee
  4. As nothing at all
Correct!

As an attempt to influence the referee — Others openly criticising a ruling without the referee’s permission count as an attempt to influence; only the shooter concerned may protest.

In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™, how does the shooting order work in a squad?
  1. It rotates at each stand and for the doubles
  2. It is fixed for the whole day
  3. The best shooter always starts
  4. The referee chooses freely
Correct!

It rotates at each stand and for the doubles — Squads of six are drawn, and the order rotates at each stand and also for the doubles.
