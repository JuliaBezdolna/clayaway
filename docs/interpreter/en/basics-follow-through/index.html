Follow-through, gun speed and tempo

Follow-through means the gun keeps moving along the target's line after the trigger is pulled, like a golf or tennis swing. There is a small delay between deciding to shoot and the shot leaving the barrels; if the gun stops at the trigger, the shot goes behind. Coaches watch the muzzles after the shot: if they freeze or drop, the follow-through was missing.

Gun speed is how fast the muzzles move compared with the target, and it defines the lead method: the same speed as the target is maintained lead; faster than the target is swing-through; the same speed, then a short acceleration, is pull-away. A gun that is too slow falls behind; coaches say one that is too fast overshoots, and the shooter then often stops it.

Tempo is how fast the whole shot is done; rhythm is how even it is. A fast, close target needs a quick tempo, a long, slow crosser a calmer one, but the rhythm stays smooth. Many coaches say that shooters with one tempo for everything lose the targets that need the opposite.

“Stay in the gun”: cheek on the stock and eyes on the target until it breaks. That also keeps the shooter ready for the second barrel, because two shots may be fired at a single.

Translate “finish the shot” as continuing the movement, not as stopping.

Key points
  • Follow-through: the gun keeps moving after the trigger; a stop sends the shot behind.
  • Gun speed defines the method: equal (maintained), faster (swing-through), equal then faster (pull-away).
  • Tempo = speed of the whole shot; rhythm = its evenness.
  • Stay in the gun: ready for the second barrel on a single.
  • “Finish the shot” = keep the movement going.

The coach says

“Follow through — the gun keeps moving after the bang.”
Do not stop the swing at the shot.
“You stopped the gun.”
The muzzles stopped at the trigger, so the shot went behind.
“Match the speed of the bird — you're racing it.”
The gun is moving much faster than the target; move at the target's speed.
“Slow the tempo down, this one gives you lots of time.”
The target is slow or long, so the whole movement can be calmer.
“Stay in the gun until you see it break.”
Keep the cheek on the stock and eyes on the target until it breaks.

Check yourself

The coach says “You stopped the gun”. Where did the shot most likely go?
  1. In front
  2. Over the top
  3. Exactly on the target
  4. Behind
Correct!

Behind — The target keeps moving while the stopped gun does not, so the shot passes behind it.

Which gun speed describes maintained lead?
  1. Much faster than the target
  2. The same as the target
  3. Zero: the gun waits
  4. Slower than the target
Correct!

The same as the target — In maintained lead the muzzles move at the target's speed, keeping the gap constant. A waiting gun is ambush; faster is swing-through.

What is the difference between tempo and rhythm?
  1. Tempo is the speed of the shot, rhythm its evenness
  2. They are two words for the same thing
  3. Tempo is the music played on the ground
  4. Rhythm is the time limit between targets
Correct!

Tempo is the speed of the shot, rhythm its evenness — Coaches change the tempo for each target while keeping a smooth rhythm, so translate both words separately.
