A long day: water, food, sleep and travel

An international Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ competition is 200 targets in rounds of 25, often over two or three days; Compak® Sporting is also counted in rounds of 25 targets. A day at the ground can last from morning to evening, with walking, waiting and sun or wind. By the end of the day fatigue often costs more targets than technique.

Water. Even mild dehydration hurts attention and coordination. Drink water in small sips throughout the day, not only once you are very thirsty. In the heat or under heavy effort you can add electrolyte (salt) drinks after checking with a doctor. Coffee and energy drinks in moderation: they can increase hand tremor and nerves.

Food. A heavy lunch before a round makes you sleepy; hunger makes you irritable and inattentive. Eat little and often: a good breakfast, and at the ground fruit, nuts, a sandwich or a bar. Do not try new foods on competition day. And remember hygiene: wash your hands before eating, because they may carry lead from cartridges.

Sleep. The most important sleep is not just the night before the start but the few nights before it. If you sleep badly before a competition because of nerves, that is common; one restless night rarely spoils a result if you were well rested before.

Travel. A long drive or flight tires you more than you think: stiff muscles, dry cabin air, little movement. If you can, arrive the day before, do a light warm-up and take a walk, drink water. Flying across several time zones adds jet lag — a disruption of your body clock.

To adapt to a new time zone, live on local time from the first day: eat breakfast, lunch and go to bed by it, and spend time in daylight. If the organiser offers official practice, it helps both with fatigue and with unfamiliar targets.

Put together your «day kit»: water, snacks, a cap, a warm layer and a rain jacket. Then at the ground you will think about targets, not about where to find a bottle of water.

Key points
  • Drink water in small sips through the day
  • Eat little and often; wash your hands before eating
  • Sleep well for several nights before the start
  • After travelling — a warm-up, water and local time
  • Carry a day kit: water, snacks, clothes for the weather

Did you know? The body is often said to adjust to a new time zone by roughly one hour of difference per day, which is why many shooters arrive several days early for distant competitions.
