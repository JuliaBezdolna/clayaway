How targets are scored: one, zero and No Bird

Every target in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting gets one of these scores. “One” (a hit): the target was properly thrown, you shot by the rules, and at least one piece visibly came off it, or it was wholly or partly destroyed. “Zero” (a miss): no visible piece came off, only dust or “smoke”. On the score sheet a hit is usually marked X and a miss O.

Only the referee decides. Other shooters have no right to intervene. Going to fetch or pick up a target to check it is forbidden. In Compak the referee announces every zero loudly or with a sound signal. Break your targets where the referee can see them: a target broken behind a natural obstacle or beyond the shooting limit does not count.

No Bird means “does not count”: the target is repeated. Reasons on the target's side: it was thrown broken, from the wrong trap, in the wrong colour or size, on an irregular trajectory, more than 3 seconds after the call, you did not call for it, you were clearly disturbed, or the referee cannot decide whether it was hit. In Compak bad weather is never a reason for a No Bird.

A gun or cartridge malfunction: the first such incident in a round is a No Bird with a warning; later ones in the same round are zeros. After a misfire stay where you are, barrels pointing down the range, do not open the gun and do not touch the safety until the referee has examined it. Otherwise the target is scored zero.

A few more rules. You may not refuse a target that you called for and that was thrown correctly: not shooting at it is a zero. You may not shoot at a target the referee has already called No Bird. If one shot breaks both targets of a double, Sporting scores both, while Compak repeats the double under its own rules.

A round is 25 targets, so your score is the number of “ones” out of 25. Don't worry about every zero: even the world's best shooters sometimes miss.

Key points
  • One: at least one piece visibly came off; zero: only dust or nothing.
  • Only the referee decides; targets are never checked by hand.
  • No Bird: the target is repeated (wrong target, trajectory, over 3 s and so on).
  • First malfunction in a round is a No Bird, later ones zeros; don't open the gun after a misfire.
  • You may not refuse a correctly thrown target you called for.

Did you know? By calling for the next target you accept the referee's decision on the previous one. So if you disagree, say so at once.
