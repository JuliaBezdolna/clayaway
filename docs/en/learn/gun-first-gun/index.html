Choosing your first gun

The most common beginner mistake is to buy a gun before understanding what suits you. A gun has to fit you: your height, the width of your shoulders, your face and your dominant eye. So the first step is not a shop but a shooting ground and a coach.

Many grounds hire out guns, often together with a first lesson, cartridges, glasses and ear protection. Shoot club guns a few times. You will find out whether you enjoy Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting, and you will feel the difference in weight, barrel length and recoil.

Remember a competition rule: two shooters in the same squad or group may not use the same gun. If you hire a gun for a tournament, make sure it is yours alone.

Try before you buy. Good shops and grounds let you shoot demonstration guns. Compare an over-and-under with a semi-automatic, 28 with 30 inches, different weights. Pay attention not to the engraving but to how the gun comes to the shoulder and moves with the target.

For most adult beginners a good start is a 12-gauge competition O/U with interchangeable (multichoke) chokes and 30-inch barrels. Juniors and smaller shooters may be better off with a lighter model or a 20 gauge. A left-handed shooter needs a stock made for the left side.

A used gun from a well-known maker is a sensible choice, but show it to a gunsmith before buying. They will check the barrels, the locking and the mechanism. Buy only from a licensed dealer or as your country's law requires: almost everywhere you need a licence for a shotgun, and the laws differ.

Budget for more than the gun: glasses and hearing protection, a hard case, a cleaning kit, a vest and, above all, cartridges and lessons with a coach. A few lessons will do more for you than a more expensive gun.

Key points
  • First a ground and a coach, then a shop
  • Shoot hire guns and try several models
  • Two shooters of the same squad may not share a gun in competition
  • Have a used gun checked by a gunsmith
  • Licences and buying rules depend on the country

Did you know? A good gun lasts for decades: competition O/Us from well-known makers are often built for hundreds of thousands of shots with proper care and regular servicing.
