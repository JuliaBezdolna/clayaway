Travelling with a gun inside the EU

If you live in an EU country and legally own a shotgun, there is the European Firearms Pass (EFP) for travel to other EU countries. It is issued by an authority of your country of residence and is usually valid for up to five years. People who live outside the EU are not issued an EFP.

The pass lists specific guns. Before the trip check that the gun you are taking really is entered in the EFP and that the pass will not expire during the journey. Bought a new gun? Have it entered in the pass first.

Sport shooters benefit from a simpler procedure: with an EFP and proof of the reason for the trip, they can usually take a gun to another EU country without a separate prior permit. Countries may still have their own exceptions and requirements, so check the destination country's rules with its police or through the organiser.

The proof of the reason for travel is most often an invitation letter from the competition organiser or the ground: it gives your name, the event, the place and the dates. It is often asked for at the border or the airport, so request it in advance and keep a printed copy with the pass.

Transit countries matter too. If you drive through several countries, find out the rules of each one. Several non-EU countries linked to the EU by agreements also usually recognise the EFP — but check every time.

The EFP does not cancel local laws. In the country you are visiting, its rules on carrying, storing and buying cartridges apply. Keep the gun unloaded, in a case, and never leave it unattended.

Key points
  • The EFP is issued by your EU country of residence; usually valid up to five years
  • The gun must be entered in the pass
  • An invitation letter from the organiser is often required — carry a printed copy
  • Check the rules of the destination and transit countries

Did you know? The European Firearms Pass came about through the EU firearms directive of 1991.

Country laws

France: bringing a shotgun to a competition

As across the EU, Regulation 2021/57 (REACH, since 15 Feb 2023) applies: no firing lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them. French hunting law refers to that rule directly, so a breach is also a hunting offence. We found no separate national lead rule for clay ranges; ranges follow the terms of their own permits. In June 2026 the REACH Committee approved a wider restriction on lead in hunting and sport-shooting ammunition with a transition period; as of October 2026 it is not yet in force. Wording is our summary; only the official text is legally binding. (2026-10)

Italy: bringing a shotgun to a competition

As across the EU, Regulation 2021/57 (REACH, since 15 Feb 2023) applies: no firing lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them. The national rule for Natura 2000 sites goes further on distance (150 m from the shore); regions implement it through their own acts, so details may vary. We found no separate national lead rule for clay ranges. In June 2026 the REACH Committee approved a wider restriction on lead in hunting and sport-shooting ammunition with a transition period; as of October 2026 it is not yet in force. Wording is our summary; only the official text is legally binding. (2026-10)

Spain: bringing a shotgun to a competition

As across the EU, Regulation 2021/57 (REACH, since 15 Feb 2023) applies: no firing lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them. Spain's national rules matter for clay shooting too: they expressly cover sport shooting, so a range in such a zone cannot use lead shot. Hunting is a matter for the autonomous communities; two whose official texts we checked are listed below, others may have their own rules. In June 2026 the REACH Committee approved a wider restriction on lead in hunting and sport-shooting ammunition with a transition period; as of October 2026 it is not yet in force. Wording is our summary; only the official text is legally binding. (2026-10)

Czechia: bringing a shotgun to a competition

As across the EU, Regulation 2021/57 (REACH, since 15 Feb 2023) applies: no firing lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them. We found no separate national lead rule for clay ranges; ranges follow the terms of their own permits. Since 1 January 2026 the new Firearms Act No. 90/2024 Coll. applies, replacing Act No. 119/2002 Coll. Wording is our summary; only the official text is legally binding. (2026-10)
