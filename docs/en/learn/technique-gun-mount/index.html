The gun mount

The gun mount is the movement that brings the gun to your face and shoulder. A good mount is the same every time: then your eye always looks along the rib, and the gun shoots where you look.

The main idea: the gun comes to the face, not the face to the gun. Your head stays level and still, your cheek settles on the comb of the stock, and the stock goes into the pocket between shoulder and collarbone. If you tilt your head down to the gun or duck, your eye ends up in a different place each time.

The mount starts with the eyes: first you see the target, then your hands move the barrels with it, and the stock reaches cheek and shoulder at about the same moment as the barrels catch the target's line. Don't rush to fire: movement first, then the shot.

The rules differ here. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the shooter holds the gun low until the target appears: the heel of the stock touches the body and the top of the stock stays below a horizontal line on the vest, marked 25 cm below the shoulder axis. The gun may be mounted only once the target has appeared, and a breach earns a yellow card from the referee. In Compak® Sporting the position at the call is free: the gun may be mounted in advance. In both disciplines every shot is fired with the gun at the shoulder.

Whether the gun fits you matters too: stock length, comb height. A badly fitting gun makes a consistent mount difficult. Gun fit is done by a coach or a gunsmith. Mount practice at home with a checked, unloaded gun is possible only where your country's law allows it, and only as your coach shows you. At the ground, mounting and swinging the gun outside the shooting stand is forbidden.

Key points
  • The gun comes to the face; the head stays level.
  • Cheek on the comb, stock in the shoulder pocket, the same mount every time.
  • Sporting: low gun, vest line 25 cm below the shoulder, mount only after the target appears.
  • Compak: free gun position at the call; always fire with the gun mounted.
  • Gun fit and practice drills with a coach and within the law.

Did you know? The Sporting vest line is drawn in a contrasting colour so that the referee can see from a distance whether the stock is below it.
