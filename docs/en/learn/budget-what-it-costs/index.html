What it costs to start · 1/4 · 5 min

What the budget is made of

Clay shooting, like most sports, has one-off and regular costs. One-off costs are things you buy once every few years: a gun, glasses, ear protection, a vest. Regular costs are what you «shoot» at every session: cartridges and targets. Prices vary a great deal between countries, regions and grounds, so we give no amounts here — only what the budget is made of.

Coaching. At the start it is the best investment: a coach teaches you safety, sets up your stance and mount and saves you from habits that take a long time to fix. Grounds often offer a taster session for beginners that already includes the gun, cartridges and targets.

The gun. A beginner usually starts with the ground's rental gun and buys their own only later. Your own gun is the biggest one-off cost, but it lasts many years. Add a slip or case, cleaning kit and, under the law of many countries, a safe for storage.

Cartridges and targets are the main regular cost. A round of 25 targets in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting needs at least 25 cartridges, and it is wise to have a few spare. Targets are paid for separately — per target or per round, depending on the ground.

Protection and clothing: safety glasses, earplugs or ear muffs, and later a shooting vest, cap and comfortable shoes. Glasses and ear protection are needed from the first session; grounds often lend them.

Documents and insurance. To own a gun, most countries require a firearms permit, and for competitions you often also need federation membership or a sports licence. Many countries or clubs require third-party liability insurance. For trips within the EU you will need a European Firearms Pass. Requirements differ in every country.

Competitions and travel. Competitions charge an entry fee. Add travel, accommodation and meals if the event is far away. Club shoots close to home are the most affordable way to start competing.

Key points
  • One-off costs: gun, protection, vest, safe
  • Regular costs: cartridges and targets, coaching
  • A round of 25 targets needs at least 25 cartridges plus spares
  • Documents, membership and insurance depend on the country
  • Competitions: entry fee plus travel

Did you know? Under FITASC rules an international competition is 200 targets, so a shooter needs at least 200 cartridges for one such event, and more with spares.
