Results, protests and appeals

Scores are recorded by the referee or a person the referee appoints. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ at international events each round's result is posted on a central scoreboard, and when leaving each stand you must check that your score on the card is correct. If not, tell the referee at once: once he has decided, no appeal is possible. In Compak® Sporting the shooter signs the score sheet at the end of the round; anyone who refuses to sign loses the right to make any complaint.

If you disagree with a referee's decision, protest immediately, on the stand: raise your hand and say “protest” or “appeal”. The referee stops the shooting, listens and gives a final decision. If in doubt, the referee may consult another referee, the trapper or the squad's shooters, but decides alone. Remember: by calling the next target you accept the decision on the previous one.

Some decisions cannot be appealed at all, not even to the jury: whether a target was broken or lost (“one” or “zero”), whether it was a No Bird, and whether a trajectory was correct. Here the stand referee has the last word.

Other referee decisions can be appealed in writing to the jury, together with a deposit. In Sporting the deposit is 40% of the entry fee; if the jury agrees with the shooter it is refunded, and if not it goes to charity. In Compak the deposit is the amount in force on the day of the competition, and the complaint must concern an incident of the same day. International events may also have an appeal jury, which hears challenges to decisions of the jury itself.

Protest calmly and politely. Arguing with a final decision after shooting has resumed, contesting it ostentatiously or loudly criticising the referee is an attempt to influence the referee, for which the jury can take away a target or a round or even expel you. After a final decision, resume shooting in time: within 15 seconds in Sporting, within 10 in Compak. For details see the Rules section of ClayArena.

Key points
  • Check your score: in Sporting when leaving each stand; in Compak sign the sheet.
  • Protest at once on the stand: hand up and “protest” or “appeal”.
  • Not appealable: one/zero, No Bird, whether a trajectory was correct.
  • Other decisions: in writing to the jury with a deposit (40% of the fee in Sporting).
  • No arguing after the decision; resume shooting in time.

Did you know? In Sporting a deposit from a rejected appeal does not vanish: it goes to a charity chosen by the FITASC Chairman.
