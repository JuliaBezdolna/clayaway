Shooter's health · 5/5 · 5 min

Alcohol, medication, sun and weather

Alcohol and guns do not mix. Even a small amount of alcohol slows reactions and impairs vision and safety judgement. So the rule is simple: no alcohol before or during shooting, including the lunch break. A glass only in the evening, when the gun is already in its slip and in the safe. In many countries handling a gun under the influence is also against the law.

Medication. Some medicines — for allergies, sedatives, sleeping pills, strong painkillers — can cause drowsiness or dizziness. If you take medication, ask your doctor or pharmacist whether you can shoot and drive with it. If you feel drowsy or dizzy, do not shoot that day.

Anti-doping. At championships and major international events there may be doping control under the rules of the World Anti-Doping Agency (WADA) and the sports federations. In shooting sports, beta-blockers, which calm the heart, receive particular attention. If you need prescribed medication, check its status with your doctor in advance; sometimes a therapeutic use exemption (TUE) is needed. Check the specific rules with your national federation.

Sun. You may stand in the sun at a ground all day. A cap or hat with a peak (shooting cap) protects your head and cuts glare, and sunscreen protects your skin, especially face, neck and arms. Drink water regularly and find shade between rounds.

Heat. Weakness, headache, nausea, dizziness or heavy sweating can be signs of heat exhaustion. Stop shooting at once, move into the shade, drink water and tell someone nearby. If things do not improve or the person becomes confused, call for medical help.

Cold, rain and wind. Dress in layers so you can take clothes off or add them; bring a rain jacket and spare gloves. Cold fingers feel the trigger less well, so warm your hands between rounds. During a sudden downpour or storm the referee may pause shooting. Lightning is a serious danger: in a thunderstorm do not stand in the open with a gun, and take shelter in a building.

This is general information, not medical advice: for questions about your own health, see a doctor.

Key points
  • No alcohol before or during shooting
  • Drowsy-making medicines — ask a doctor; feeling unwell — do not shoot
  • Doping control is possible at championships; check medicines in advance
  • Cap, sunscreen, water and shade against sun and heat
  • Thunderstorm — shelter in a building, do not stand in the open with a gun

Did you know? The WADA Prohibited List is updated every year, so a medicine that was allowed last season is worth checking again.
