How to watch clay shooting · 4/4 · 5 min

A spectator at the ground: where to stand, how to photograph and support

A spectator's first rule is safety. Stand only where it is allowed: in the spectator area, behind or to the side of the firing line. Never step forward, even to see better. If you do not know where you may stand, ask the referee or the organiser.

Hearing protection is needed by everyone, not just shooters. The Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting rules explicitly require ear muffs or plugs for everyone on or near a layout, including spectators. Children outside the clubhouse must always wear hearing protection. Glasses are a good idea too: small target fragments sometimes fly a long way.

While a shooter is on the stand, keep quiet: no loud talk, clapping or shouting. Switch your phone to silent. Applaud after the round or after a good double, once the shooter has opened the gun.

Take photos and film only from the spectator area. Do not use flash, turn off the shutter sound and do not move in the shooter's line of sight while they are aiming. A drone needs the organiser's permission and often compliance with local flight rules. Before posting photos of people, especially children, ask for their consent or their parents'.

How do you support your shooter? Do not give advice during the competition: the rules treat it as coaching, which can earn the shooter a yellow card, and the adviser is sent out of the shooting area. Do not comment on the referee's decisions — open criticism counts as an attempt to influence the referee.

The best support is calm. Bring water, help carry things, smile after the round and talk about the score later, once emotions have settled. A good spectator makes the day easier for the shooter and the whole squad.

Key points
  • Stand only in the spectator area, behind or to the side of the firing line
  • Hearing protection is compulsory for spectators too
  • Silence while shooting, applause after the round
  • Photos without flash and without moving in the shooter's line of sight
  • No advice during the competition and no criticism of the referee

Did you know? Under the Sporting™ rules a shooter on the stand without hearing protection is treated as absent — so the ear-protection rule is taken very seriously.
