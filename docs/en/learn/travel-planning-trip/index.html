Travelling to competitions · 1/6 · 6 min

Planning the trip

A trip to a Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting competition starts with the programme. It gives the dates, disciplines, entry deadline and entry fee. You enter through the organiser, and popular events sometimes fill up long before the deadline, so do not put it off.

Look for accommodation near the ground. Shooting often starts in the morning, and a long drive every day takes energy. Organisers often recommend partner hotels. Book early and ask the hotel whether it accepts guests with sporting guns and where they can be stored safely.

Think about transfers: how you get from the airport to the hotel and from the hotel to the ground each day. A hire car gives freedom, but carrying a gun by car has its own rules in every country. A taxi may refuse a passenger with a gun, and some competitions run an organiser's shuttle.

Documents for the gun and cartridges are the longest part of the preparation. The next lessons cover them; start on them several weeks ahead, and even earlier for trips outside the EU.

Arrive early, at least a day or two before. If you fly across several time zones, your body needs time: jet lag usually passes in a few days, and many people find flying east harder than flying west. Shooting times are announced in local time.

Arriving early lets you practise, but within the rules. In Sporting nobody may practise on the competition layouts before the event — only on other parts of the ground. In Compak you may practise on the competition trajectories, and at continental and world championships official practice opens on the Saturday before the championship.

A short checklist: documents, insurance, ear and eye protection, clothes for the weather, the programme in local time and the organiser's contacts. Being late for your squad can cost zeros for the targets you miss, so spare time is the best insurance.

Key points
  • Enter early: popular events fill up
  • Stay near the ground; ask about storing the gun
  • Plan transfers and the rules for carrying a gun by car
  • Arrive a day or two early to adjust to the time zone
  • Practise within the rules: in Sporting, not on competition layouts

Did you know? Many people find flying east harder than flying west: you have to fall asleep earlier than your body is used to, which is harder than going to bed later.
