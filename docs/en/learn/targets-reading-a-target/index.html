How to read a target

To “read” a target means to understand, before you shoot, how it flies: where it appears, how fast it moves, where it turns, where it climbs or drops. Experienced shooters do this at every stand, and it is often what separates a hit from a miss. It matters equally in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting.

Start with the target menu: which trap, in what order, and whether there are special targets (midi, mini, battue, rabbit). Then watch the presentation carefully. In Sporting all trajectories are shown to the first shooter of the squad, who may ask for repeats, up to two targets for each position on the menu. Doubles on report are not shown, but simultaneous and rafale doubles are. Compak has a show of targets too, under its own rules.

Ask yourself simple questions. Where do I first see the target clearly? That is the point where your eyes start following it, the pick-up point. Where is it easiest to break? That is the break point. At that place the target should still be clearly visible, not dropping behind trees or escaping beyond the shooting limit.

Take note of speed, distance and angle, and of what the target is doing: climbing, slowing down, falling. Wind can lift or push a target down, and a dark background of trees or a bright sky changes how you see it. A target that is slowing down needs less lead than one flying at full speed.

Watch how others shoot, but think for yourself: everyone has their own tempo. Follow the target with your eyes only. Mounting the gun and swinging it along an imagined line (mock shooting) is forbidden: in Sporting the referee gives a warning for it, and in Compak it is not allowed off the shooting station or while another squad member is shooting.

Sporting trajectories are graded by difficulty: in class A an average shooter breaks more than 80% of targets, in class B 60 to 80%, in class C 40 to 60%. Don't be upset if you miss a class C target: it is meant to be hard. Reading targets is best learned with a qualified coach who shows you what to look at.

Key points
  • Menu first, then watch the presentation closely.
  • Find where you first see the target clearly and where you will break it.
  • Note speed, angle, wind, background and whether the target is slowing.
  • Follow with your eyes; mock shooting is forbidden.
  • Sporting difficulty classes: A > 80%, B 60–80%, C 40–60%.

Did you know? In Sporting doubles on report are not shown: the shooter has already seen both targets as singles, because doubles are made only of targets shown from the same stand.
