Singles and doubles

A single is one target from one trap. In both Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting you may fire two cartridges at a single: if the first shot misses, you can still break it with the second.

A double is two targets shot as one unit. In a double on report the shooter calls for the first target and the second is released by the sound of the shot at the first. In Sporting the second target comes 0 to 3 seconds after the shot. In a simultaneous double both targets leave at the same moment and the shooter chooses which to take first.

A rafale double exists only in Sporting: two targets from the same trap along the same trajectory, one after the other, as fast as the trap can reload. They may be shot in either order. Compak has only doubles on report and simultaneous doubles.

For a double you have only two cartridges. The key difference between the disciplines: in Sporting you may fire both at one target if you wish. In Compak you may fire only one cartridge at each target of a double, and according to the referees' quick guide, deliberately firing both at the same target is scored Zero-Zero (both targets lost).

Every Sporting layout gives 25 targets: 15 singles and 5 doubles. Doubles are made only from targets already shown as singles from the same stand. The whole squad shoots the singles first, then the doubles. A Compak round is also 25 targets, 5 at each of the five stations.

If one shot breaks both targets of a double, Sporting scores both: “one” and “one”. Compak is different: on a double on report the first result stands and the second target is repeated as a No Bird; a simultaneous double is repeated in full, and the third time it happens it is scored Zero-Zero.

Key points
  • A single is one target, and you may shoot at it twice.
  • Doubles: on report (second released by the shot), simultaneous (together), rafale (Sporting only).
  • A double gets two cartridges; in Compak one per target.
  • A Sporting layout: 15 singles + 5 doubles = 25 targets.
  • Two targets with one shot: “one” and “one” in Sporting, a repeat in Compak.

Did you know? Rafale is French for a “burst” or “gust”: the targets leave one after the other as fast as the trap can reload.
