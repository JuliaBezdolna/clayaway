What to do in a malfunction

Sometimes a gun does not fire: the cartridge fails, something jams or you forgot the safety. This is called a malfunction. The key is not to panic and not to make sudden moves.

The rule is simple: stay on the stand, keep the gun pointing towards the targets and do not open it. Do not touch the safety. Raise your hand or tell the referee or instructor that there is a malfunction.

Why not open the gun straight away? Occasionally a cartridge fires late, a fraction of a second or even longer after the trigger is pulled. If the gun is open or pointing sideways at that moment, that is dangerous. So in training without a referee, wait with the gun pointing safely before opening it. Your instructor or the ground's rules will tell you how long.

In competition the referee examines the gun first. If the shooter opens the gun or touches the safety before the check, the target or targets are scored as lost — zero.

The first gun or cartridge failure in a round usually gives the right to shoot the target again (a no bird). Further failures in the same round count as misses. If the gun cannot be fixed, the shooter may, with the referee's agreement, carry on with another gun; the details depend on the discipline.

After the check the referee or instructor will tell you what to do next. Never try to take a loaded gun apart yourself on the stand.

Key points
  • Stay put, muzzle towards the targets
  • Do not open the gun or touch the safety before the check
  • Opening it before the check means the target is scored zero
  • The first failure in a round is usually a no bird; later ones are misses

Did you know? Many beginner «malfunctions» are simply a safety catch left on. A coach will teach you to check it before you call.
