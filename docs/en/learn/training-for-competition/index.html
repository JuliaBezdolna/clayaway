How to train · 5/6 · 5 min

Training for competition

Competition is different: new targets, unfamiliar shooters beside you, a referee, nerves. So that this does not throw you, some of your sessions should be run as if they were already a competition.

Shoot rounds of 25 targets as in competition. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ a round on one layout is 25 targets: 15 singles and 5 doubles. Keep score and do not repeat a missed target: in competition there is no second try.

Practise your pre-shot routine: the same actions in the same order before every target. Calm, identical preparation helps you hold your focus when you are nervous.

Practise the start the rules require. In Sporting™ the gun is not mounted before the call: the heel of the stock touches the body and the top of the stock stays below the jacket line until the target is released and visible. In Compak® Sporting the gun position when calling is free. Train the version you will compete in.

Keep to the time limits. In Sporting™ you have no more than 15 seconds between singles or between doubles. In Compak® Sporting a shooter has no more than 10 seconds to call after the previous station's target has been shot. After the call the target must be released within 3 seconds. Your routine should fit these limits without rushing.

Prepare in advance. Coaching a competitor during the competition is forbidden: the referee gives a yellow card for it and sends the person coaching out of the shooting area. Discuss the plan with your coach before the start. Also, in Sporting™ nobody may practise on the layouts set up for the competition beforehand.

Key points
  • Run some sessions as a competition: rounds of 25 and a score
  • The same routine before every target
  • In Sporting™ start with the gun down, below the jacket line
  • Limits: 15 seconds in Sporting™, 10 seconds to call in Compak® Sporting
  • Coaching is forbidden during competition, so plan in advance

Did you know? Some shooters ask a friend to act as 'referee' and call out results in training: it helps them get used to attention and pressure.
