Eyes and vision

At a ground the air is full of small fragments: pieces of targets, sometimes ricocheting pellets, dust and wind. That is why safety glasses are compulsory in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting for shooters, referees and staff, and a shooter without them is treated as absent.

Shooting glasses have impact-resistant lenses, usually polycarbonate, and also cover the eyes from the side. Ordinary sunglasses are not suitable. In Europe look for marking to the EN 166 standard on safety eyewear; other countries have their own standards.

The lens tint helps you see the target against different backgrounds and in different light. But tint is a matter of comfort; protection comes only from an impact-resistant lens. To start, one pair with clear or light yellow lenses is enough.

If you wear glasses for your eyesight, there are two routes: shooting glasses with prescription lenses, or a frame with a separate prescription insert behind interchangeable tinted lenses. Ordinary reading or everyday glasses often lack the strength needed, so discuss the choice with an ophthalmologist or optician, ideally one who knows sports eyewear.

A shooter looks at a target in the distance, so sharp distance vision matters most. From about 40–45 many people develop age-related long-sightedness (presbyopia): close-up vision gets harder and the bead may blur. For shooting this is usually less of a problem than it seems, because your eyes should be on the target, not on the bead.

The master eye is the one that leads your vision. In some people eye dominance weakens or changes with age, and coaches observe that women more often have cross-dominance (right shoulder with a left master eye or the other way round) or no clearly dominant eye. If you suddenly start missing consistently to one side, recheck your master eye, together with your coach.

Have your eyes checked regularly by an ophthalmologist, especially if you notice that targets have become harder to see at dusk or in bright light. This is general information, not medical advice: for questions about your own health, see a doctor.

Key points
  • Safety glasses are compulsory; without them a shooter is treated as absent
  • You need impact-resistant lenses, not ordinary sunglasses
  • For eyesight — prescription lenses or a prescription insert
  • As you get older, recheck your master eye and see an ophthalmologist

Did you know? Yellow lenses boost contrast on dull days, and orange targets against a green background stand out well through purple or pink tints — which is why many shooters own several interchangeable lenses.
