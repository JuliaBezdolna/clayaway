Equipment that fits a child

An adult gun is almost always too long and too heavy for a child. The child leans back to hold it, the stock hits the cheek and shoulder, and after a few shots fear sets in. That is why gun fit matters even more for young shooters than for adults.

The key measure is the length of pull, the distance from the trigger to the butt of the stock. There are youth models (youth gun) with a shorter, lighter stock, and some guns have an adjustable stock and comb. As the child grows the stock is lengthened with spacers, so check the fit every season with the coach or a gun fitter.

A light 20 gauge is a common choice — an over-and-under or a semi-automatic. A gas semi-automatic spreads the recoil over a longer time, so it feels softer. Cartridges have a light load: recoil is lower and shooting is more pleasant.

Protection must fit too: children's ear muffs or plugs, safety glasses with impact-resistant lenses, a peaked cap. A shooting vest must not be too big, or the gun will catch on it during the mount.

Clothing should be comfortable and right for the weather. Competitions have a dress code: for example, in Sporting™ sandals are banned and shorts must be long, Bermuda-type. Closed shoes with good grip are needed in training too.

Do not rush into buying expensive kit. Start with a club gun for hire, then look at second-hand youth models. A gun of their own is worth buying once the child's interest is steady, and always with the coach's advice.

Key points
  • An adult gun is usually too long and heavy for a child
  • Length of pull is the key to fit; check it every season
  • A light 20 gauge and light-load cartridges
  • Ear muffs, glasses and vest in children's sizes
  • Hire first, buy when the interest is steady

Did you know? The FITASC Sporting rules cap the gauge from above — no bigger than 12 — so a 20 gauge gun is allowed in competition.
