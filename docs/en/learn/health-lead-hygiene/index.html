Shooter's health · 3/5 · 4 min

Lead and hygiene

Most clay cartridges are loaded with lead shot. Lead is a toxic metal: it builds up in the body and can harm the nervous system, kidneys and blood formation. It is especially dangerous for children and pregnant women.

At an outdoor ground a shooter takes in lead mainly through the hands, not the air. Fine dust and traces of lead are on cartridges, empties, the cartridge bag, gloves and the gun. Then the hands touch food, a cigarette or the face — and the lead reaches the mouth. Indoor ranges add airborne dust as well.

The main rule: wash your hands (and face, if you touched it) with soap and water after shooting and before eating, drinking or smoking. Wet wipes are only a stopgap; special lead-removal wipes also exist.

Do not eat, drink from open cups or smoke on the stand or while picking up empties. Snack in the clubhouse or a separate area, and only with clean hands. Keep your water bottle closed.

The clothes and shoes you shot in also carry lead dust. Change after shooting, wash shooting clothes separately from children's things and do not wear them in a child's room. Clean your gun wearing gloves, in a ventilated place.

For ordinary training some shooters choose cartridges with steel or soft iron shot where the ground and the law allow it: that means less lead both in the environment and on your hands. Rules on lead differ from country to country.

If you shoot a great deal, are planning a pregnancy or are pregnant, or if a child shoots, talk to a doctor. The level of lead in the body is measured with a simple blood test. This is general information, not medical advice: for questions about your own health, see a doctor.

Key points
  • Lead enters the body mainly through the hands
  • Wash your hands with soap after shooting and before eating
  • No food, drinks or smoking on the stand
  • Keep shooting clothes separate, especially from children's things
  • Shoot a lot, pregnant or a child shooting — talk to a doctor

Did you know? Lead is also a danger to the environment, which is why shooting lead shot in and near wetlands is banned across the EU.
