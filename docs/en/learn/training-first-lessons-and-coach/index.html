First lessons and choosing a coach

The best start in clay shooting is lessons with a coach. A coach keeps you safe, builds correct technique from the first shot and stops bad habits taking root. Fixing a habit that has already set in takes far longer than learning it right from the start.

At the first lesson the coach usually explains safety and gun handling, checks your master eye, shows you the stance and the gun mount. The first targets are simple and slow, often going away from the shooter. The aim of the first lesson is to break a few targets safely and enjoy it, not to post a score.

How to choose a coach. Ask about qualifications: in many countries coaches hold a certificate from the national federation or association. Ask whether the coach works with beginners: a champion is not always the best teacher for a novice. It matters that you understand each other, in language and in the way they explain.

Think about the discipline. A coach who shoots Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting will prepare you better for those. A convenient ground matters too: regular lessons close to home do more good than rare ones far away.

Start with a trial lesson. Afterwards honestly ask yourself: did you feel safe, was it clear, do you want to come back? If not, try another coach; that is normal.

You can look for a coach at your ground, through your national federation or in the ClayArena coaches directory, which lists coaches and the grounds where they work. Friends' advice is useful but does not replace a coach: even an experienced shooter cannot always teach.

Key points
  • Start with a coach: safety and good habits from the first shot
  • First lesson: safety, master eye, stance, mount and easy targets
  • Look for a qualified coach who teaches beginners and speaks your language
  • A trial lesson shows whether a coach suits you
  • Find coaches at your ground, via your federation or in the ClayArena directory

Did you know? Many top shooters train with a coach throughout their careers: even champions need an outside eye to spot small faults.
