The culture of the sport · 1/6 · 4 min

Sportsmanship and respect for the referee

On the stand the key person is the referee. The referee looks after safety and order and decides whether you hit the target. The referee decides alone; other squad members have no right to step in or give an opinion, unless the referee asks them in case of doubt.

Disagree with a ruling? There is a proper way. Straight away, on the stand, raise your hand and say «appeal» or «protest». The referee stops the shooting and listens. If the referee is sure of the decision, it is confirmed and becomes final.

After that, calmly carry on shooting. If a shooter keeps arguing by words or gestures, challenges the ruling for show, or other shooters openly criticise the referee without permission, the rules treat this as an attempt to influence the referee, and penalties apply.

Self-control is also part of sportsmanship. Throwing a gun down, swearing or behaving aggressively towards the referee or rivals is a serious breach: the referee reports it to the jury, and the jury may expel the shooter from the competition.

Remember that referees often work all day in sun or rain, and many are volunteers. Thanking the referee after the round is a simple and very valuable tradition.

Sportsmanship also applies to rivals: greet your squad, wish them good shooting, congratulate them on a good score. In clay shooting rivals are usually friends as well.

Key points
  • The referee decides alone and the decision is final
  • Disagree? Raise your hand at once and say «protest» or «appeal»
  • Further arguing counts as an attempt to influence the referee
  • Losing self-control can lead to expulsion

Did you know? In Compak® Sporting a shooter may never pick up a target to check whether it was hit: only the referee decides.
