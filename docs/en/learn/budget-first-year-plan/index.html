What it costs to start · 4/4 · 5 min

A first-year plan

Below is an example plan, not a mandatory scheme. Everyone moves at their own pace, and gun laws differ from country to country. The aim of the plan is to spend money when you already know what you need.

Step 1. Taster session. Find a ground with a coach and try Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting with the ground's gun. Glasses and ear protection are often lent. You need to buy nothing.

Step 2. A few lessons with a coach. Regular sessions over the first months: safety, stance, mount, master eye. Now it is worth buying your own glasses and hearing protection — inexpensive but important items, and your own fit better.

Step 3. Club and documents. If you enjoy shooting, join a club and find out about federation membership, a sports licence and insurance. Start applying for a firearms permit if your country requires one: it may take a long time.

Step 4. Fitting and your own gun. Once you train regularly, have a fitting with a coach or gun fitter and buy a gun — new, or used and checked by a gunsmith. With it: a slip or case, a cleaning kit and a safe if the law requires one.

Step 5. Vest and small items. A shooting vest, cap, comfortable shoes, a cartridge bag — once it is clear how and where you shoot.

Step 6. First competitions. Start with club shoots close to home: a small entry fee, familiar faces, a calm atmosphere. Keep a diary of training and results; your results can also be found on ClayArena. When you are ready, the next step could be bigger competitions or a training tour such as ClayAway.

Key points
  • First a taster session — no purchases
  • First items of your own: glasses and hearing protection
  • Start on documents early
  • Buy a gun after regular training and a fitting
  • First competitions — club shoots close to home

Did you know? In many countries the law requires a gun to be stored in a safe or locked cabinet, so it is worth budgeting for one at the same time as the gun.
