FITASC and how international shooting is organised

FITASC is the international federation for hunting-style clay shooting. It writes and updates the rules for Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting and approves grounds for international competitions. The current rules we summarise at ClayArena are the editions dated 01/01/2025.

The sport is built like a pyramid. At the base are clubs and shooting grounds, where people practise and hold local competitions. Clubs join national federations, and national federations are members of FITASC.

National competitions follow FITASC rules or the country's own rules, and their layouts are approved by the national federation. Layouts for international competitions are approved by FITASC. A federation may ask a club to run a competition, but the federation stays responsible for applying the rules.

Competitions are run by referees. Every referee holds a certificate from their national federation, and the chief referee holds an international FITASC certificate. There is also a jury, which deals with protests and breaches of the rules.

International competitions come at different levels: Grand Prix events, continental and world championships. They are usually 200 targets over several days. Shooters compete individually and also in categories and teams.

For a beginner this means something simple: the rules are the same from country to country, so what you learn at your own club works at any ground in the world. Only the official FITASC text is binding; ClayArena gives a clear summary with a link to the source.

Key points
  • FITASC writes the rules for Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting
  • Club → national federation → FITASC
  • FITASC approves international layouts; the national federation approves national ones
  • Only the official FITASC text is binding

Did you know? FITASC also keeps a record of shooters expelled from competitions, kept for five years.
