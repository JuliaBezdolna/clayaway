How to enter a competition

Competition calendars are published by clubs, national federations and FITASC, and by ClayArena. Once you have chosen a Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting event, read the programme first: dates, number of targets, schedule, fee, categories, prizes and the organiser's contacts.

An entry is your registration for the competition. It is often made in advance online and only confirmed at the desk on the day. The entry fee is paid with the entry or at registration. The fee matters for the rules too: in Sporting a written appeal to the jury comes with a deposit of 40% of the entry fee.

For official competitions you usually need to be a member of a club or national federation, and for European and World Championships shooters are, as a rule, entered by their national federation. These requirements differ between countries, so check them in advance with your federation or the organiser.

Firearms laws differ from country to country. You must legally own your gun and cartridges at home and legally bring them to where you shoot. In the European Union there is the European Firearms Pass for travelling with guns, but even with it you must follow each country's rules. If the host country bans release triggers, this must be stated in the competition programme.

After entries close, organisers publish the shooting times and squad lists. The rules stress that keeping to the schedule is the shooter's own responsibility, including any delays or advances during the event. In Compak in line, if you are absent on the first day, you are taken off the list from the second day, and you can come back only by appealing to the jury.

Key points
  • Programme first: dates, targets, schedule, fee, categories.
  • Entries are often made online and confirmed on the day.
  • Federation membership and championship entry rules depend on the country.
  • Firearms laws differ; the EU has the European Firearms Pass.
  • Keeping to the schedule is your responsibility.

Did you know? A gun with a release trigger (it fires when the finger lets go) must carry a sticker with a large “R” on a fluorescent background.
