Cartridges and shot · 5/6 · 6 min

Lead, steel and soft iron

Traditional shot is lead. Lead is heavy and soft, so a pellet keeps its speed well and cartridges give an even pattern. The FITASC rules for Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting are written for lead shot: up to 28 g, 2.0–2.5 mm in diameter.

But lead is toxic to the environment, especially to waterfowl and water. So more and more grounds and countries restrict it. In the EU, since 2023 it has been forbidden to shoot lead shot in wetlands and within 100 m of them. Some countries have even stricter rules, and wider restrictions on lead in Europe are being discussed. Before travelling, always check the rules of the country and of the ground.

Steel shot is the most common lead-free replacement. Steel is about a third lighter than lead for the same volume and much harder. So a steel pellet loses speed sooner and ricochets more from hard surfaces.

Because of ricochets, grounds that allow steel often limit its size, for example to no more than 2.5 mm, and sometimes the cartridge velocity too. Each ground sets its own limits: read the rules posted at the club or ask the organiser.

Soft iron shot is a type of lead-free shot that is softer than ordinary steel. It is made to be kinder to barrels and chokes. There are also more expensive options, bismuth and tungsten alloys: they are heavier than steel and behave more like lead.

Steel pellets need a protective plastic or special wad so that the hard steel does not scratch the barrel. Some makers offer steel clay loads with biodegradable or fibre wads; check what your ground allows. Which chokes are safe with steel is covered in the Chokes module.

Where lead shot is already banned or restricted (as of October 2026; laws change, so check the current version):

• European Union — Commission Regulation (EU) 2021/57: since 15 February 2023 it is forbidden to fire gunshot containing 1% lead or more, or to carry it while shooting, in or within 100 m of wetlands. A wider REACH restriction on lead in gunshot was approved by the EU committee in June 2026 with a seven-year transition; at the time of writing it is still under scrutiny by the European Parliament and the Council.

• Great Britain (England, Wales, Scotland) — UK REACH restriction: from 1 April 2029 the sale and use of lead shot is banned for both live quarry and target shooting, including clay grounds; only elite athletes are exempt under conditions set by the law.

• Denmark — lead shot has been fully banned since 1996. Current rule: BEK no. 1479 of 05/12/2023, § 2(4) — cartridges with lead shot may not be sold, carried or used for hunting, nor carried or used for clay shooting (flugtskydning), including at shooting ranges. Import and sale of lead-containing products is also restricted by BEK no. 856 of 05/09/2009.

• Netherlands — lead shot has been banned for hunting since 1993; today this is art. 11.79 of the Besluit activiteiten leefomgeving (under the Omgevingswet). For clay shooting, the Besluit kleiduivenschieten milieubeheer of 2004 banned lead and zinc shot (with an exemption for elite athletes on approved ranges); since 2017 clay-range environmental rules moved into general acts, and the Royal Dutch Hunters' Association (KNJV) states that lead and zinc shot remain banned at clay ranges. Check with the ground.

• Germany — there is no nationwide ban on lead shot for clay shooting, but several layers of restrictions apply. First, EU rule 2021/57: no lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them — including clay grounds located in such zones. Second, the laws of 15 of the 16 federal states (all except Hamburg) restrict lead shot in hunting — most often for waterfowl or over water; in Saxony (since 2014) and Thuringia (since 2022) for all hunting. The full table of state laws is below. Third, each ground has its own permit: some grounds allow only steel or soft-iron shot (for example Dornsberg — steel only, up to 2.5 mm and 28 g), while many others do not allow steel at all because of ricochets. According to a DJV survey (2023), only every second shotgun shooter in Germany can practise with lead-free shot at all. Before travelling, check the rules of the specific ground.

• USA — since 1991 only nontoxic shot is allowed for waterfowl hunting (federal rule 50 CFR 20.21(j)); some states go further.

Besides laws, many grounds allow steel shot only (for example to protect nature around the ground) — that is the ground's own rule and applies even where the law allows lead. Before travelling, always check the country's law and the ground's rules.

Key points
  • The FITASC rules are written for lead shot
  • In the EU, since 2023 lead shot is banned in wetlands and within 100 m of them
  • Steel is lighter and harder than lead, and ricochets more
  • Grounds often limit steel shot size, for example to 2.5 mm
  • Soft iron is kinder to barrels; bismuth and tungsten are costlier alternatives
  • Lead shot is already banned in Denmark and the Netherlands, in Great Britain from 1 April 2029; in the EU near wetlands (Regulation 2021/57).

Did you know? Lead has a density of about 11.3 g/cm³, steel about 7.8–7.9 g/cm³. So a steel pellet of the same diameter is roughly 30% lighter than a lead one.

France: lead shot

As across the EU, Regulation 2021/57 (REACH, since 15 Feb 2023) applies: no firing lead shot in wetlands or within 100 m of them. French hunting law refers to that rule directly, so a breach is also a hunting offence. We found no separate national lead rule for clay ranges; ranges follow the terms of their own permits. In June 2026 the REACH Committee approved a wider restriction on lead in hunting and sport-shooting ammunition with a transition period; as of October 2026 it is not yet in force.
