Junior competitions and costs

The first competitions are best found at your own club: club shoots are short, friendly and free of extra pressure. Next come regional and national events, and with experience, international FITASC competitions in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting.

Competitions have an open ranking and category rankings. Under the FITASC rules in force from 2027, the Junior category runs from 1 January of the year a shooter (girl or boy) turns 13 to 31 December of the year they turn 20; for 2027 that means born in 2007–2014, although exceptions are possible. Only the year of birth counts, not the day and month. A junior also takes part in the open ranking at the same time.

The other FITASC categories from 2027: Lady — women from the year they turn 13; Men — from the year of the 21st birthday to the year of the 55th; Senior — 56–65; Veteran — 66–72; Master — 73 and over, for men and women. These are international rules; national and club competitions may use their own categories and limits, so always read the competition programme.

In FITASC team rankings a national team is usually 3 shooters (4 in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ in the Men category). A shooter picked for a team of another category must also enter that category individually: for example, a junior girl in the Ladies team enters Lady individually.

The rules for juniors are the same as for adults: safety, dress, no coaching, the requirements for guns and cartridges. In Sporting the gauge may not be bigger than 12, so a 20 gauge is allowed, and no cartridge may carry more than 28 g of shot.

Now the costs, in general terms. Regular spending goes on targets and cartridges — the main item — plus coaching sessions. One-off spending goes on kit: a gun or its hire, protection, vest, shoes. Every year there is club membership, the federation licence and insurance. And competitions mean entry fees, travel and accommodation.

Plan a budget for the season and increase spending gradually. Ask the club about junior discounts, club guns and second-hand kit; some clubs and federations run support programmes for juniors.

Key points
  • Start with club shoots
  • Junior under FITASC rules from 2027: from the year of the 13th birthday to the year of the 20th
  • National and club events may have their own categories — read the programme
  • Juniors follow the same rules; a 20 gauge is allowed
  • The main costs are targets and cartridges; plan a season budget

Did you know? For FITASC categories only the year of birth matters: a shooter born on 31 December moves to the next category on 1 January just like one born in January.
