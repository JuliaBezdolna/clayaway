How to read a scoreboard and results

In clay shooting the score is simple: how many targets were broken. An international Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ competition is 200 targets in rounds of 25. In Compak® Sporting a round is also 25 targets. So the best possible score is 200 out of 200.

During a round the referee keeps the score sheet. In the FITASC model a hit is marked X and a miss O. At the end of the round the total is added up, and the shooter checks and signs the card. Remember: the shooter must check the score straight away, because later it can no longer be disputed.

Round scores are collected on a central scoreboard or in an electronic system. Next to a name you will usually see each round's score and the total. That shows not only who is leading, but also who is shooting consistently and who had a bad round.

What happens with a tie? Under FITASC rules ties for the first three places are settled by a shoot-off, an extra round of 25 targets. If still tied, they shoot until the first miss (sudden death). Below third place there is no shoot-off: tied shooters are ranked by their round scores counted back from the last round to the first (countback).

Besides the overall ranking there are categories, for example Lady, Junior, Senior, Veteran. So a prize giving may have several «first places» — each category has its own winner, and there is also a team ranking.

After the competition the results appear on ClayArena. The table shows the place and the number of targets broken. From the table you can go to the shooter profile with their competitions and figures such as accuracy and best score. Compare results only over the same number of targets: 90 out of 100 and 180 out of 200 are different things.

Key points
  • An international competition is 200 targets in rounds of 25
  • On a FITASC card X is a hit, O is a miss
  • Ties for the top three go to a shoot-off; below that, countback
  • There are categories and a team ranking, so there are several winners
  • Compare scores over the same number of targets

Did you know? Under the Sporting™ rules, on the same layout each referee uses a pencil of a different colour for each shooting stand.
