Travelling to competitions · 5/6 · 5 min

Buying cartridges at the destination

Airline limits and rules mean you cannot carry enough cartridges for a competition in your luggage: Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting needs hundreds. So cartridges are usually bought at the destination — in the ground's shop, from a local dealer or through the organiser, who often has a supplier.

Order cartridges in advance, especially if you need a particular brand, load or 20 gauge: popular cartridges can run out at the height of a competition. Ask where and when you can collect them.

Buying cartridges is also governed by law. In many countries the seller asks to see a gun permit, an EFP or a temporary permit, and the rules for foreigners can differ from those for locals. Check with the organiser or the ground.

FITASC competitions have cartridge requirements: a load of no more than 28 g of lead (+2% tolerance), spherical shot of 2 to 2.5 mm diameter. Reloads, dispersers, mixed shot, black powder and tracer cartridges are banned. Read the box label, and try unfamiliar cartridges in practice first.

Some grounds allow only steel shot or other non-toxic shot because of environmental rules. Steel shot does not suit every gun or every choke — check the gun's markings and ask a gunsmith. Find out about such restrictions before the trip: they may apply to practice even if the competition runs under the general rules.

Work out how many cartridges you need with a small margin so you do not end up with leftovers: taking them home can be difficult. Selling or giving cartridges to others is allowed only under local law. At the hotel store cartridges as the country's rules require and do not leave them in the car.

Key points
  • Competition cartridges are usually bought at the destination — order ahead
  • Buying may require documents: check local rules
  • FITASC rules: up to 28 g of lead, 2–2.5 mm shot, no reloads
  • Some grounds allow steel shot only — check your gun and chokes
  • Work out the quantity so nothing is left over

Did you know? Steel is lighter than lead, so a steel pellet of the same size weighs less and loses energy faster in flight.
