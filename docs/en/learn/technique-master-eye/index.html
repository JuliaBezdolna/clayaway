Master eye and cross-dominance

Most people have a master eye: the one the brain trusts more when we look with both eyes. In shooting this matters because the master eye must be the one looking along the rib. If the master eye is on the other side from the stock, the gun “points” in the wrong place and the shooter misses to one side without understanding why.

A simple test. Stretch your arms out and make a small triangle with your hands. With both eyes open, frame a distant object, such as the corner of a building. Now close one eye, then the other. The eye that keeps the object in the triangle is your master eye. Even easier: ask a coach to stand in front of you; through the triangle the coach will see your master eye.

If you are right-handed with a right master eye (or left-handed with a left one), all is well: you can usually shoot with both eyes open. If a right-hander has a left master eye, that is cross-dominance. Some people have no clearly dominant eye at all.

What can be done? There are several options: squint or close the other eye at the moment of the shot, put a small dot on the lens of your glasses in front of the non-dominant eye so it does not take over, learn to shoot from the other shoulder or, less often, use a gun with a special cross-over stock. Which suits you is the coach's decision, because it depends on age, experience and how strong the dominance is.

Dominance can change with age and with tiredness, so the test is worth repeating. Remember that safety glasses are compulsory at the ground for everyone, shooters, referees and staff, so the dot usually goes on them. This lesson explains the basics but does not replace lessons: learn technique with a qualified coach.

Key points
  • The master eye must look along the rib.
  • Test: hand triangle, distant object, close each eye in turn.
  • A master eye on the other side from the stock is cross-dominance.
  • Solutions: squint, a dot on the glasses, the other shoulder; the coach chooses.
  • Dominance can change; repeat the test.

Did you know? The master-eye test works because the brain merges the images from both eyes but takes the aiming centre from one, the dominant eye.
