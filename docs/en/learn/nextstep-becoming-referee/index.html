How to become a referee

The referee is the heart of every competition. The referee looks after safety and order on the stand and decides whether a target was hit. Without referees there is no tournament, so clubs and federations are always glad of new people.

The first step is your country's national federation. It usually runs referee courses: rules theory, practice on the stand and an exam. After the exam you receive a referee certificate or card. The procedure, course length and requirements differ from country to country, so start by asking your federation or club.

The FITASC rules explicitly require this document. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ every referee must hold a valid certificate from their national federation. Someone without the required certificate may only act as an assistant referee, and only if the jury of the competition approves it in advance.

In Compak® Sporting the requirements are similar: the referee is approved by the jury and holds a valid refereeing card from the national federation. A referee without enough international experience is checked by international referees.

The next level is international refereeing. The chief referee of an international Sporting™ competition must hold an international certificate issued by FITASC, and the Head Referee in Compak® Sporting must hold an international card and a FITASC qualification. How exactly to reach the international level is something your federation will explain: usually it takes experience at national events and a recommendation.

At international competitions referees solemnly promise to apply the rules, to judge honestly and impartially, and to set aside their own nationality and federation. That sums up the job well: calm, knowledge of the rules and respect for every shooter.

A tip to get started: ask to help an experienced referee at club shoots. You will see the work from the inside, and the rules will stick much faster.

Key points
  • Referee courses are usually run by the national federation
  • Every referee at a FITASC competition must hold a valid federation certificate
  • Without a certificate, only an assistant referee and only with jury approval
  • The chief referee of an international event needs a FITASC qualification
  • Start by helping an experienced referee at club shoots

Did you know? Under the Sporting™ rules a referee at an international competition must carry the current rules in the FITASC official languages.
