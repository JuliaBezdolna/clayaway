Flying with a gun

Start with the airline, before you even buy the ticket. Not every carrier accepts sporting guns, some only on certain flights, and almost all require advance notice, often at least a day or two before. There may be a separate fee. Each airline has its own rules, so read them on its website and get confirmation in writing.

The gun travels only in checked baggage, never in the cabin. It must be unloaded and taken down (barrels separate from the stock) and packed in a hard lockable case. A soft gun slip is usually not acceptable for a flight. Rules on locks and on who keeps the keys differ between countries — check.

At the airport arrive earlier than usual. At check-in say that you are carrying a sporting gun: the case is often inspected, a separate document is issued and it is accepted at the oversized baggage counter. After landing the gun is sometimes handed over at a separate desk or in the presence of police rather than on the normal belt.

Cartridges are dangerous goods and travel under separate rules. Under the IATA dangerous goods rules a passenger is usually allowed up to 5 kg gross of sporting cartridges for their own use, only in checked baggage and securely packed, ideally in the maker's boxes. A box of 25 cartridges of 12 gauge weighs roughly a kilogram, so 5 kg is only about five boxes.

Many airlines are stricter: a lower limit, a requirement to pack cartridges separately from the gun, or a complete ban. Check before every flight and do not assume last year's rules still apply.

Flying with a connection? Make sure the gun is checked through to your final destination, and find out whether you will have to collect it and check it in again. Keep the documents (gun permit, EFP or temporary permit, invitation letter, airline confirmation) in your hand luggage, not in the case.

Key points
  • Tell the airline in advance and get written confirmation
  • The gun goes unloaded, taken down, in a hard lockable case, in checked baggage only
  • Cartridges: under IATA rules usually up to 5 kg gross per person, but airlines may be stricter
  • Arrive at the airport early
  • Documents go in hand luggage

Did you know? Under IATA rules sporting cartridges are dangerous goods of Division 1.4S — the least hazardous group of explosives, allowed in baggage in limited quantities.
