When a child can start

Parents often ask at what age a child can try Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting. There is no single answer: the minimum age for shooting is set by the country's law, the ground's rules and its insurance. Many clubs accept children from about 10–12, but that is only a guide, so first check local law and ask the club.

The age on the passport is not the main thing. What matters more is whether the child is ready: can they hold a gun steadily, do they listen carefully and follow an adult's commands every time, can they concentrate for at least half an hour. If a child is easily distracted or treats a gun like a toy, it is better to wait a few months.

The first sessions are run only by a qualified coach or instructor, ideally one to one with the child. They explain the safety rules, check which eye is dominant (master eye) and decide when the first shot can be fired. Parents do not replace the coach, even if they shoot well themselves.

A child uses a light gun, often a 20 gauge. The bigger the gauge number, the narrower the barrel: a 20 gauge is lighter and has less recoil than the 12 gauge most adults use. It is paired with soft, light-load cartridges (low-recoil cartridges), for example 21–24 g of shot. The main aim is that the shot neither frightens nor hurts.

The first targets should be simple and slow: for example, a target flying straight away from the shooter. The number of shots per session is small, and the session ends on a good shot so that the child leaves the ground smiling.

Before the first visit, call the ground and ask: from what age do they accept children, is there a junior programme, a coach for children and a 20 gauge gun for hire. That tells you straight away whether the club suits you.

Key points
  • The minimum age is set by the country's law and the club's rules — check them
  • The child's readiness matters more than age
  • Start only with a coach, one to one
  • A light 20 gauge gun and soft, light-load cartridges
  • Simple targets, few shots, finish on a good shot

Did you know? A shotgun's gauge historically means how many equal lead balls the size of the bore can be made from one English pound of lead. That is why a 20 gauge has a narrower bore than a 12.
