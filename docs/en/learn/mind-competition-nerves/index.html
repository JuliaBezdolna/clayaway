Nerves in competition

Your first Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting competition will almost certainly make you nervous: strangers, a referee, a score sheet, spectators. That is normal. Experienced shooters get nervous too — they have simply learned to live with it.

When you are nervous the body releases adrenaline: the heart beats faster, breathing becomes shallow, hands may shake and time seems to speed up. That is why nervous shooters often rush the mount, stop the gun or lift their head off the stock to see whether the target broke.

The first technique is breathing. Try box breathing: breathe in for a count of 4, hold for 4, breathe out for 4, hold for 4. A few cycles before you step onto the stand calm you noticeably. Making the out-breath longer than the in-breath also helps you relax.

The second technique is self-talk. Instead of «just don't miss», tell yourself what to do: «see the target», «smooth», «follow through». The brain handles negatives poorly, so phrase everything as an action.

The third technique is preparation. Arrive early, know your start time and stand, check your cartridges, glasses and ear protection. The fewer surprises, the fewer reasons to worry.

If you miss, do not argue with yourself and above all do not show anger. FITASC rules explicitly forbid losing self-control: throwing the gun down, swearing or behaving aggressively. Breathe out, remember your routine and think only about the next target.

Start with club shoots: the atmosphere there is calmer and you get used to the procedure without much pressure. With every competition the nerves become familiar and manageable.

Key points
  • Everyone gets nervous; adrenaline speeds up the heart and movements
  • Box breathing: 4 — 4 — 4 — 4
  • Tell yourself what to do, not what to avoid
  • After a miss — breathe out and think of the next target; anger is against the rules
  • Start with club shoots

Did you know? Moderate nerves are actually useful: they sharpen attention. They only become a problem when there are too many — and that is what breathing exercises are for.
