Names, constrictions and markings

Chokes have English names, and in Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting they are used all over the world. Below are typical constrictions for 12 gauge. Remember: 1 thou is a thousandth of an inch, about 0.025 mm, and actual values depend on the maker and even on the bore diameter of the particular barrel.

Cylinder: no constriction, 0 mm. Skeet: a very light constriction, about 0.1–0.15 mm (4–6 thou); in European usage it is sometimes called 1/8. Improved cylinder (IC): about 0.25 mm (around 10 thou), in Europe '1/4', quarter choke.

Light modified (LM): about 0.35–0.4 mm (around 15 thou), sometimes marked 3/8. Modified (M): about 0.5 mm (around 20 thou), '1/2', half choke. Improved modified (IM): about 0.65–0.75 mm (25–30 thou), '3/4'.

Full: about 0.9–1 mm (35–40 thou), '1/1' or simply 'Full'. There are even tighter chokes (extra full), but clay shooting rarely needs them.

The fractions 1/4, 1/2, 3/4 and 1/1 are a European habit of naming a choke as a share of full. They are handy in conversation but are not exact millimetres: one maker's '1/2' may differ slightly from another's.

Italian makers, Beretta in particular, mark chokes with stars: ***** cylinder, **** improved cylinder (1/4), *** modified (1/2), ** improved modified (3/4), * full; skeet chokes carry no stars. Remember: the more stars, the more open the choke. Other makers cut notches on the rim of the tube, use coloured bands or print the name (IC, M, LM).

The most reliable source is the marking on the choke itself or the gun's manual. If in doubt, a gunsmith can measure the constriction with a gauge in a minute.

Key points
  • Cylinder 0 · Skeet ~0.1–0.15 mm · IC ~0.25 mm (1/4) · LM ~0.35–0.4 mm
  • M ~0.5 mm (1/2) · IM ~0.65–0.75 mm (3/4) · Full ~0.9–1 mm (1/1)
  • 1 thou ≈ 0.025 mm; 10 thou ≈ 0.25 mm
  • Stars: the more stars, the more open the choke
  • Values are approximate and differ between makers

Did you know? Modern competition barrels are often slightly overbored. So the same choke with the same name can give slightly different constriction in different guns: what counts is the difference between bore and choke, not just the choke's diameter.
