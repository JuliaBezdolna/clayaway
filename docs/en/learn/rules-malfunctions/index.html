Rule 5. Gun or cartridge malfunctions

Sometimes you pull the trigger and nothing happens: a cartridge misfire or a gun fault (malfunction). Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting have clear rules for this.

Most important: stay where you are, muzzle pointing down the range. Do not open the gun or touch the safety until the referee has examined it. If you open it first, the target, or both targets of a double, are scored zero.

Malfunctions are counted within one round of 25 targets. The first one is a No Bird and a warning (in Compak® Sporting a yellow card). The second one in the same round is no longer repeated but scored zero.

If the first shot fails, or both barrels fire at once (double discharge), a single is repeated and a double is repeated as a whole. In Sporting™ there is an exception: if you still managed to fire the second shot, its result counts.

If the second cartridge fails on a single, the target is repeated, but it can only be scored with the second shot. So you must miss deliberately with the first. Otherwise in Sporting™ it is a zero.

What about the gun? In Sporting™, if it is fixed before the squad finishes the stand, you may shoot last with the referee's permission, or take another gun at once. In Compak® Sporting you have 2 minutes to repair or replace it; otherwise the targets not shot are zero.

Lesson for beginners: check your cartridges and gun beforehand, at the dedicated test place, not on the stand. Test shots on the stand are forbidden.

The full wording of every item (our summary of the FITASC rules with a link to the official document) is in the ClayArena rules guide, and you can test yourself in the ClayArena rules trainer.

Key points
  • After a malfunction: stay put, muzzle down range, do not open the gun.
  • Opened before the referee's check: zero.
  • First malfunction in a 25-target round: No Bird and a warning; the second: zero.
  • Second cartridge fails on a single: on the repeat only the second shot counts.
  • Compak® Sporting: 2 minutes to repair or replace the gun.

Did you know? Both barrels firing at once is called a double discharge, and the rules treat it as a gun fault.
