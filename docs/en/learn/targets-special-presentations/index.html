Targets and trajectories · 3/6 · 4 min

Special presentations: teal, springing, looper and rabbit

Besides crossing and quartering targets, Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting also use presentations where the key is not the angle but a change of height or speed. Their names also come from hunting.

A teal (named after the small duck) springs almost straight up, hangs at the top and falls. Many shooters break it on the way up or at the top, where it almost stops for a moment. A springing target climbs steeply at an angle, like a bird flushing from the ground.

A looper, or chandelle, flies an arc: it climbs, rolls over the top and comes down, often sideways as well. The difficulty is that it keeps changing direction, so the barrels must follow the curve rather than a straight line.

A rabbit is rolled on its edge along the ground. It bounces on bumps and can suddenly change height. Pointing a little low and in front helps you not to shoot over it. Under the Sporting rules a rabbit, like every target, is shot only with the gun mounted to the shoulder: firing from the hip is not allowed.

There is a special case: if the shooter misses with the first shot and the rabbit then breaks by itself (for example on the ground) before the second shot, it is a No Bird and the target is repeated. On the repeat only a break with the second shot counts, so the first shot must be a deliberate miss. This rule exists in both Sporting and Compak.

Safety matters especially with rabbits: shoot only inside the permitted zone, never swing the barrels towards people or traps, and do not shoot beyond the shooting limit the referee has shown.

Key points
  • A teal springs almost vertically; a springing target climbs steeply at an angle.
  • A looper (chandelle) flies an arc and keeps changing direction.
  • A rabbit rolls and bounces along the ground; it is shot only with the gun mounted.
  • A rabbit that breaks by itself after a first-shot miss is a No Bird; on the repeat only the second shot counts.

Did you know? The French word chandelle means “candle”; it is also the name of an aerobatic manoeuvre in which a plane climbs steeply while turning.
