How to save money

In shooting the biggest saving comes not from cheap gear but from a sensible plan. Here are some proven ways. But one rule never changes: never save on safety. Safety glasses, hearing protection and a sound gun always come first.

Club membership. In many clubs members pay less for targets and training, have access to club shoots and may get discounts on cartridges. If you shoot regularly, membership often pays for itself. Compare the terms of several clubs.

Cartridges in bulk. Buying cartridges in large batches is usually cheaper than buying single boxes. But do so only where the law allows you to buy and store that quantity, and store cartridges somewhere dry and cool as your country's rules require. Club members often buy together.

Remember the competition rules. Under FITASC rules, reloaded cartridges are forbidden in Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting competitions, and the load may not exceed 28 grams. So saving with home-made cartridges does not work in competition, and for training, lighter factory cartridges are often cheaper anyway.

Used gear. Besides the gun, vests, cases and chokes are often bought used. But replace safety glasses with scratched or cracked lenses: they no longer protect as they should. Earplugs are personal; it is better to have your own.

Train with a purpose. A few rounds with a clear task from your coach give more than lots of cartridges fired «just because». Dry mounting with an unloaded gun at home costs nothing and is very useful. A training diary helps you see what works.

Shared trips. It pays to travel to competitions together and share travel and accommodation. Organised tours such as ClayAway bundle training, accommodation and transfers into one package, which is often easier than organising everything yourself.

Key points
  • Never save on safety
  • Club membership often pays for itself
  • Bulk cartridges only where the law allows buying and storing them
  • Reloaded cartridges are forbidden in FITASC competitions
  • Purposeful training, dry mounting and shared trips

Did you know? Many clubs have club guns that members can use in training, which lets you take your time before buying your own.
