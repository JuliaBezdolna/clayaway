Compak® Sporting: the compact format

A full Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ course needs a lot of land: woods, hills, several layouts. Not every club has that. So the idea arose to bring the variety of sporting onto a small plot.

That is how Compak® Sporting appeared. On the cover of its rules FITASC states that the discipline was created in 1989 and adopted by FITASC in 1991. The federation's history materials link the creation of Compak Sporting to the 1980s, so other sources may give a slightly different date. What we can say reliably is that it is a late 20th-century format.

The idea of the compact format is five shooting positions in a line and six traps placed around a marked rectangle. Targets fly in different directions, as in Sporting™, but everything happens on one field.

The format has several advantages. A club needs less land and equipment. Shooting moves faster. And spectators see the whole squad and all the targets from one spot, which makes Compak® Sporting well suited to television and video.

The name and rules of Compak® Sporting are a registered trade mark of FITASC. That is why we write the ® sign and do not invent our own versions of the name.

Today Compak® Sporting is often a beginner's first discipline: it is easier to find near home, and the skills you build here carry over to a full Sporting™ course.

Key points
  • Compak® Sporting is a compact version of Sporting™ on one field
  • According to the rules cover: created in 1989, adopted by FITASC in 1991; other sources give another date
  • 5 shooting positions and 6 traps around a rectangle
  • The name and rules are a registered trade mark of FITASC

Did you know? In Compak® Sporting a spectator can stay in one spot and see every target of the competition — impossible on a full Sporting™ course.
