Which choke for which distance

Distance to the target is the main factor in choosing a choke. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ targets are set at distances that respect hunting ethics: not so close that game would be spoiled, not so far that it would only be wounded. In Compak® Sporting targets fly over a rectangle 35–40 m wide and 25 m deep in front of the shooting positions.

Here is a rough guide for lead shot No. 7½–9. Up to 20 m: cylinder or skeet. About 20–30 m: improved cylinder (1/4) or light modified. About 30–35 m: modified (1/2). Beyond 35 m: improved modified (3/4), and occasionally full. This is not a law: coaches and experienced shooters have their own preferences.

Judging distance is hard for a beginner. Look at where you are most likely to shoot the target, not where it comes from. Ask your coach or an experienced shooter in your squad: on a shooting ground that is an ordinary question.

The type of target matters too. A mini target is small, so it needs a denser pattern. A battue is flat and often flies edge-on; it is easier to break when it shows its belly. A rabbit rolls along the ground and is tougher than a standard target.

In an over-and-under the more open choke usually goes in the barrel that fires first, and the tighter one in the second. For example, a 1/4 and 1/2 pair (IC and M) is a popular all-round choice for many Sporting™ stands and for a whole Compak® Sporting round, where chokes cannot be changed within the round.

Advice for beginners: do not get carried away with tight chokes. A narrow pattern does not forgive mistakes, and beginners make plenty. A more open choke gives more hits, and with them confidence. Once your technique is more consistent, you can experiment.

Key points
  • Choose the choke for the distance at which you will shoot
  • Up to 20 m cylinder/skeet, 20–30 m IC/LM, 30–35 m M, beyond 35 m IM
  • More open choke for the first shot, tighter for the second
  • An IC and M pair is a popular all-round choice
  • Beginners do better with more open chokes

Did you know? Many of the world's best Sporting™ shooters use fairly open chokes and break even far targets thanks to precise pointing rather than a tight pattern.
