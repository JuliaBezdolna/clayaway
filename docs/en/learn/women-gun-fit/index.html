Gun fit for women

Most production guns are made for the «average man». A woman with different proportions — shorter arms, a longer neck, higher cheekbones, a different chest shape — often has to stretch with such a gun, tenses her neck and feels more recoil. The cause is not strength but the gun's dimensions.

Length of pull is the distance from the trigger to the end of the stock. If it is too long, the stock catches on clothing during the mount and lands on the arm instead of the shoulder. Many women need a shorter stock; it is cut down or fitted with a thinner pad.

The comb is the top of the stock where the cheek rests. Its height decides how the eye looks along the rib. Because of a longer neck and high cheekbones, women often need a higher comb. The easiest solution is an adjustable comb, which is raised or lowered without remaking the stock.

Pitch is the angle at which the end of the stock sits relative to the barrels. It decides how the stock meets the shoulder and chest. If the toe of the stock digs into the chest, it hurts; then the pitch is changed or the lower part of the stock is turned slightly outwards (cast). This fitting is best done by a gun fitter.

The grip and fore-end must suit the hand: a grip that is too thick is hard to hold with a small hand, and the finger struggles to sit properly on the trigger.

Weight is a compromise. A light gun tires the arms less but kicks harder into the shoulder. A heavier one swings more steadily and recoils more softly, but is tiring over a long day. FITASC rules require a calibre no larger than 12 gauge, so 20-gauge guns are also allowed; but you can also find a lighter 12-gauge model and shoot lighter cartridges.

Do not buy your first gun by eye. First shoot several guns at a ground, then ask a coach or gun fitter to check how the gun fits you. A well-fitted gun means both better scores and better health.

Key points
  • Production guns are often built for a man's build
  • Women often need a shorter length of pull and a higher comb
  • Pitch keeps the stock from hurting the chest
  • Weight is a trade-off between tired arms and recoil
  • Before buying — try several guns and have the gun fitted

Did you know? The smaller the gauge number, the wider the bore: 12 gauge is wider than 20, because the number is how many equal lead balls of bore diameter can be made from one pound of lead.
