Warm-up and mobility

Clay shooting looks like a calm sport: you stand, mount the gun and shoot. But over a day of Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ or Compak® Sporting you make hundreds of mounts and body turns, and the gun weighs about 3.5 kilograms. Cold muscles respond worse and the movement becomes jerky.

A warm-up is 5–10 minutes of light movement before the first round. The aim is not to get tired but to wake the body up: warm the muscles, loosen the joints and settle your breathing.

Mobility is the ability of a joint to move calmly through the range it needs. For a shooter the key areas are the neck (the head goes down onto the stock), the shoulders (the mount), the upper back and hips (turning with the target) and the ankles (the stance).

A simple routine: slow head turns and tilts without sharp circles; shoulder rolls forwards and backwards; trunk turns with hands on hips; a few shallow squats; rocking from heels to toes. Do each movement 8–10 times, smoothly and without pain.

Dynamic exercises, where the range of movement grows gradually, work best. Long static stretching to the limit is not needed before shooting; keep it for the evening.

Important: in competition you may not simulate shooting — mounting the gun and swinging along an imagined target — away from the shooting position (mock shooting). So warm up without the gun, and practise mounting an unloaded gun (dry mounting) at home or wherever the ground's rules allow it.

If you have injuries or chronic pain in the back, neck or shoulder, put your routine together with a doctor or physiotherapist. Pain during an exercise is a signal to stop, not to push through.

Key points
  • 5–10 minutes of light dynamic movement before the first round
  • Focus on the neck, shoulders, back, hips and ankles
  • No jerky movements and no pain
  • At a competition warm up without the gun; mock shooting off the stand is forbidden

Did you know? Many coaches suggest a few slow trunk turns between rounds as well: over a long day the muscles have time to cool down again.
