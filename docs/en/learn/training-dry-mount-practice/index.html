Dry practice of the mount

The gun mount is the movement that brings the gun to your shoulder and cheek. To make it the same every time you need to repeat it hundreds of times. Many coaches recommend dry mounting, practising the mount without firing, between sessions. But it may only be done safely and legally.

Law first. Rules on storing and handling guns at home differ greatly between countries. In some places a gun must be kept in a safe and not taken out without need; elsewhere there are other restrictions. Find out what the law in your country allows; if in doubt, do not do it, and ask your coach for other exercises.

Safety has no exceptions. The gun must be unloaded and checked: open it, look into the chambers and make sure they are empty. There must be no cartridges at all in the room where you practise. The muzzle always points in a safe direction, even when the gun is empty. Use snap caps only as your coach advises.

How to practise. Do the exercise exactly as your coach showed you, not as you saw it online. Often it is slow mounts in front of a mirror to check that your eye lines up with the rib, or a slow swing along the line between wall and ceiling. Ten slow correct repetitions beat fifty fast, sloppy ones.

Where you must not do it. At FITASC competitions mock shooting is forbidden: if a shooter mounts the gun before calling and swings along an imagined target, the referee gives a warning, and repeats are penalised. So at a competition there is no dry mounting on or near the stand.

After the exercise check again that the gun is unloaded and put it away as the law requires. Dry practice supplements sessions with your coach; it does not replace them.

Key points
  • Practise the mount only where the law allows
  • The gun unloaded and checked, no cartridges in the room
  • The muzzle always in a safe direction
  • Do the exercise as your coach showed you, slowly and correctly
  • Mock shooting is forbidden at competitions

Did you know? Many coaches believe a few minutes of correct slow mounts do more than an hour of fast ones: the body remembers the quality of movement you repeat.
