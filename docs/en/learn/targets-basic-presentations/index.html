Targets and trajectories · 2/6 · 5 min

Basic presentations: crossing, quartering, incoming and going away

A trajectory is the path a target flies or rolls along. Coaches also say “presentation”, meaning how the target is presented to the shooter. In Parcours de Chasse – Sporting™ the trajectories copy the flight of game such as partridge, duck or pheasant. The rules ask for a mix of incoming, outgoing, crossing and quartering targets, low and high. Compak® Sporting has trajectories too, but they are shorter.

A crosser flies from side to side in front of the shooter. It crosses your field of view at a right angle, so it needs the most lead: you have to shoot clearly in front of it. The further and faster the target, the more lead it needs.

A quartering-away target moves away from the shooter at an angle, for example to the front right. A quartering-towards target comes towards the shooter at an angle and passes to one side. The angle can be sharp or almost crossing, and it decides how far in front you need to shoot.

An incomer flies towards the shooter, often dropping. It is usually broken out in front, before it comes too close. A going-away target flies away from the shooter; it looks simple, but it quickly gets further and smaller, so you cannot wait.

A driven target is a high bird that comes from in front of the shooter and passes overhead, like a pheasant on a driven shoot. The barrels must move up from below and “blot out” the target, which feels strange to a beginner.

The same trap can give different presentations from different stands: from one stand a target is a crosser, from another it is quartering. So always look at a target from the exact stand you will shoot from.

Key points
  • A trajectory is the target's path; a “presentation” is how it is shown to the shooter.
  • A crosser flies side to side and needs the most lead.
  • Quartering away moves away at an angle; quartering towards comes in at an angle.
  • An incomer comes at you, a going-away target moves away, a driven target passes overhead.
  • One trap gives different presentations from different stands.

Did you know? Sporting presentation names come from hunting: driven is the pheasant pushed towards the guns by beaters, and teal is the small duck that springs almost straight up off the water.
