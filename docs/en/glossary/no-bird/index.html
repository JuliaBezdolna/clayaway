Refereeing

No Bird

Also: No bird, Irregular target (when declared)

What it is

The referee's call that a target or attempt does not count and must be repeated.

In detail

A No Bird is called because of the target (broken on release, wrong trap or colour, irregular trajectory, released more than 3 seconds after the call or signal, not called, shooter clearly disturbed, or the referee cannot decide) or because of the first gun or ammunition malfunction in a round. On a single the target is repeated; on doubles the rules say when the whole double is repeated and when the first result is kept. Only the referee declares a No Bird, and that decision cannot be appealed. A declared No Bird must never be shot at; doing so after a warning is penalised. In Compak bad weather and targets from another Compak never justify a No Bird.

In Sporting™ and Compak® Sporting

Sporting™

A target more than 3 s after the referee's signal is a No Bird (5.4, 6.5.2).

Compak® Sporting

Also a No Bird when a machine of the same Compak throws a stray target; weather never.
